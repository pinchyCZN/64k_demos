PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                        THE UKKO BICYCLE GAME


Abstract

   What follows the demo. UKKO.EXE ends at keyframe 0x2b6 and hands
   over to a side-scrolling bicycle game, and this document describes
   that game.

   NOTHING HERE IS A RECONSTRUCTION. The game is our own work, not a
   transcription, so the evidence tags of docs/README.txt section 2 do
   not apply to it - there is no binary to be right or wrong about.
   The music that keeps playing underneath it is the demo's own S3M,
   and that is the only part of the scene the original contributed.

   It lives in src/ukko/ukko_game.c behind three entry points and
   shares nothing with the demo's renderer but the framebuffer and the
   line drawer.

1.  The Primitive Model

   The bike is DATA, not code: a table of primitives in model space,
   each carrying its own thickness, and one transform maps them to the
   screen. Moving a wheel means editing a number.

   +-----------+---------------------------+-----------+
   | Primitive | Fields                    | Thickness |
   +===========+===========================+===========+
   | line      | ax, ay -> bx, by          | 1 to 5    |
   | circle    | ax, ay centre, by radius  | 1 to 5    |
   +-----------+---------------------------+-----------+

   A circle ignores bx and reads its radius out of by, which keeps the
   table one shape rather than a union.

   Thicknesses are deliberately unequal, so the stick man stays the
   finest thing on screen: road 5, wheels 3, frame 2, rider 1.

   The model origin is THE REAR AXLE, which is also what a wheelie
   pivots about, so the transform needs no second centre.

   That split is the path to the isometric version. Today the
   transform is a rotation and a translation in two dimensions; a
   three-dimensional one replaces xf_pt and gives Prim a z, and
   nothing that describes the bike has to change.

   A circle is drawn as AN ANNULUS TEST, not a plotted arc: every
   pixel of the bounding box is in if its squared distance from the
   centre falls between the inner and outer radii. Squared throughout,
   so there is no square root and no accumulated error, and a
   thickness of one is as exact as a thickness of five.

2.  The Road

   Flat ground at y = 150, plus bumps. A bump is a triangle - half
   width w, height h, centred on a world x - so its height is linear
   in the distance from its centre. Bumps are put back out ahead once
   they have left the screen - section 2.2.

   The generator is a plain linear congruential one, seeded fixed, so
   the course is identical every run. A jump cannot be practised
   against a road that changes.

2.1.  Why A Bump Is Wider Than The Wheelbase

   Two constraints on the generator, and between them every special
   case in the physics disappears:

   +------------------------+--------------------------------------+
   | Constraint             | What it prevents                     |
   +========================+======================================+
   | w >= wheelbase + 2     | the bike bridging the bump, so the   |
   |                        | peak passes between the wheels and   |
   |                        | neither of them touches it           |
   | slope under 45 degrees | the lean clamping, which leaves the  |
   |                        | front wheel short of the road        |
   +------------------------+--------------------------------------+

   With both held, both wheels sit on the surface by construction.

2.2.  When A Bump Is Put Back

   There are eight bumps and they are reused: once one has gone by it
   is moved out beyond the furthest, with a fresh width and height.

   IT MAY ONLY BE REBUILT ONCE ITS WHOLE WIDTH HAS LEFT THE SCREEN,
   and that is a stricter test than it sounds. The bike does not sit
   at the left edge - it sits BIKE_SCREEN_X into the view, and the
   road is drawn a margin further still, so the leftmost visible
   world x is behind the bike by the sum of the two. A bump centred
   less than its own width to the left of that is partly on screen,
   and rebuilding it there changes its shape in front of the viewer:
   it appears to flatten out while still in view.

   SO THE RECYCLER AND THE RENDERER HAVE TO AGREE ON WHERE THE SCREEN
   ENDS. They are the only two places that care, and one owns the
   answer - the margin and the left edge are each defined once.

3.  The Rider

   OUR skeleton, not the demo's, and simpler: nine joints against
   twelve. The demo's own mapping is formats/pose.txt section 3.

   THE FEET ARE THE PEDAL CIRCLE and everything else follows from
   where they are. There are no keyframes: the hips and shoulders are
   fixed to the frame, the feet are driven by the crank phase, and
   knees and elbows are solved.

   The solver is two-bone inverse kinematics in closed form. The knee
   is where two circles meet - one of thigh length about the hip, one
   of shin length about the foot - and a flip flag picks which of the
   two intersections to use, which is the whole difference between a
   knee and a backwards knee.

   Thigh plus shin must reach the furthest pedal position or the leg
   detaches.

3.1.  The Head

   A TRIANGLE, because the demo's is. Its skeleton has exactly one
   closed loop - joints 0, 10 and 11, formats/pose.txt section 3 - and
   that loop sits at the crown: its base midpoint is 0.007 of the way
   down the figure and joint 0, the neck, is 17 px below it. So the
   head is a WEDGE, flat across the top of the skull with its apex
   down in the neck.

   +-----------------+----------+------------------------+
   | Measure         | Median   | Of figure height       |
   +=================+==========+========================+
   | base, 10 to 11  |  12.4 px | 0.085                  |
   | apex to base    |  17.3 px | 0.119                  |
   | figure height   | 146.0 px | -                      |
   +-----------------+----------+------------------------+

   Our rider is about 46 px tall, so the same proportions give a base
   of 4 and a height of 5. Both are defines.

   THE RIDER LOOKS FORWARD, NOT AT THE CAMERA, and that is the whole
   difference from the demo. The triangle lies in the plane across the
   face, and the camera is side on, so the plane is edge on: all three
   vertices land on one screen x and the head draws as a bare vertical
   line. That is correct, not a missing head.

   It is the one real projection in the game, and the only piece
   already written as though the world were three-dimensional.

   +-------+------------------------+----------------------------+
   | Turn  | About                  | What the camera sees       |
   +=======+========================+============================+
   | yaw   | the head's up axis     | WIDTH ONLY - the triangle  |
   |       |                        | swings out of its plane    |
   | pitch | the side axis, which   | a lean, directly           |
   |       | the camera looks along |                            |
   +-------+------------------------+----------------------------+

   So a yaw never draws a wider triangle - it draws the demo's own
   triangle rotated, and the width that appears IS the turn.

   Each axis has its own sine and its own period, so the two never
   lock into one motion. All four values are defines and are meant to
   be changed; as they stand the yaw is +/- 70 degrees on a 0.8 s
   sweep and the pitch +/- 30 degrees on 2.5 s.

   An amplitude comes back a fraction under whatever it is set to.
   The sine table has 256 entries to a turn, so an angle quantises to
   about 1.4 degrees.

   The arithmetic stays 8.8 until the last step. Rounding each vertex
   to whole pixels before projecting would open a four-pixel base by
   one pixel and lose most of the sweep.

   AT THESE PROPORTIONS THE SWEEP IS SMALL. The drawn width is
   HEAD_BASE * sin(yaw), so a four-pixel base is one pixel wide
   looking straight ahead and three at either extreme. The demo's
   head is three times the size because its figure is three times the
   size. HEAD_BASE and HEAD_LEN are the dials if a broader head is
   wanted; nothing else depends on them.

4.  Physics

   All angles are 8.8 fixed point in SINE TABLE STEPS, 256 to a turn,
   so 45 degrees is 32 steps and the sine table is indexed directly.
   The bike's vertical position is carried in 8.8 as well as in whole
   pixels; an integer-only position truncates the fraction every frame
   and halves the height of a jump.

4.1.  The Lean

   sin(angle) IS (front - rear) / wheelbase, exactly, because the
   axles are a wheelbase apart along the bike.

   THE SIGN MATTERS: a front wheel on a bump is HIGHER, which is a
   SMALLER screen y, which is a NEGATIVE angle and a nose pointing up.

   The angle comes back from a scan of the sine table rather than from
   a small-angle approximation, which on a steep bump would leave the
   front wheel short of the road by a pixel or two. A hundred and
   thirty compares a frame is nothing.

   ON A WHEELIE THE REAR WHEEL IS THE ONLY CONTACT, so the front stops
   sampling the road entirely. Otherwise a bump under a lifted front
   wheel still tips the bike.

4.2.  The Crest Launch

   The bike leaves the ground at a crest, not at a key press.

   Slope is read ACROSS THE WHEELBASE, not between frames. The
   frame-to-frame climb on a rideable ramp is about one pixel, which
   integer truncation swallows; the rise over 28 pixels is ten times
   that and survives.

   THE STEEPEST NOSE-UP IS LATCHED, not compared against the previous
   frame. Crossing a peak, the slope sweeps from nose-up to nose-down
   gradually over a whole wheelbase, so it is never steep on one frame
   and level on the next - a test that wants both at once never fires
   at all. The latch is cleared on flat ground and when it launches.

   The velocity it imparts is scaled by a gain, and that gain is
   unphysical on purpose: the true vertical speed off one of these
   ramps lifts the bike six pixels, which is not a jump. The ramp's
   rotation carries on into the air, so the bike pitches nose-up off a
   crest and lands as it fell.

   Over 900 frames of the fixed course, ridden at idle, the bike is
   airborne for 119 of them.

4.3.  The Jump

   VARIABLE HEIGHT, the way a platformer's is. The press buys the same
   push every time, and HOLDING buys a weaker gravity while the bike
   is still rising, up to a fixed number of frames. Measured:

   +-------------+---------+
   | Frames held | Apex    |
   +=============+=========+
   |           1 |   17 px |
   |           5 |   23 px |
   |           9 |   28 px |
   |          13 |   33 px |
   |          17 |   35 px |
   |          60 |   35 px |
   +-------------+---------+

   No amount of leaning on the key goes past 35.

   CUTTING GRAVITY RATHER THAN ADDING THRUST is what keeps the arc
   looking like one arc. Extra impulse part way up reads as a second
   shove.

4.4.  The Wheelie

   HELD, not toggled. The lean ramps to 45 degrees over ten frames
   while the key is down, holds there, and ramps back when it is
   released. It is a second angle that simply adds to the ground's,
   because both are in the same units.

4.5.  The Throttle

   THE PRESS IS A STEP AND WHAT FOLLOWS IS A RAMP. Holding the key
   jumps straight to the fast speed, then keeps building to twice
   that; letting go eases back to idle rather than dropping. Speed is
   in 64ths of a pixel per frame, and the three rates and two ramp
   times are defines.

   +--------------------+--------------+----------------+
   | State              | 64ths/frame  | px/frame       |
   +====================+==============+================+
   | idle               |          150 | 2.34           |
   | the press itself   |          220 | 3.44           |
   | held to the top    |          440 | 6.88           |
   +--------------------+--------------+----------------+

   As they stand it reaches the top 3 s after the press and is back
   at idle about 2.2 s after release.

   TWO THINGS KEEP THE RAMP SMOOTH, and without either it reads as a
   gearbox rather than a throttle:

   o  The travel carries its remainder. There are only five whole
      pixel rates between idle and flat out, so truncating the
      division every frame quantises the whole ramp to five speeds.

   o  The ramps accumulate rather than dividing a range by a frame
      count. A plain step of range/frames truncates to zero once the
      period passes about four seconds, and the throttle would then
      silently stop ramping at whatever the define was raised to.

   Releasing and pressing again part way through does not restart
   anything: the ramp continues from the speed it is at, and only the
   remainder is dropped, because it belongs to the other direction.

5.  Controls

   +-----+-----------------------------------+
   | Key | What it does                      |
   +=====+===================================+
   | c   | jump - tap for low, hold for high |
   | z   | wheelie, while held               |
   | x   | throttle, while held - see 4.5    |
   +-----+-----------------------------------+

   The host's own keys keep working: pause, single step, fast forward,
   volume, mute and the section skip all drive the game through
   Demo_Frame exactly as they drive the demo, because none of them
   know what is being drawn. The last entry of the section table is
   the game, so ] from the final hold lands here.

6.  Open Questions

   o  Nothing lands on anything. The bumps are scenery to be cleared,
      and there is no scoring, no crash and no end.

   o  The isometric wireframe world is the intended direction and
      only the transform stands in the way - see section 1.

   o  The rider's arms are solved but the handlebar grip is fixed, so
      a hard landing does not move the shoulders.
