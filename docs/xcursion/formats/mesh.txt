PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                   EXCURSION'S 3D MODEL FORMAT


Abstract

   Four models live in XCURSION_U.EXE's code segment. A vertex is THREE
   SIGNED BYTES, which is how a 64k intro affords geometry at all, and
   a face is ten bytes.

   The format is verified by its own arithmetic: model 0's faces end at
   img 0x585f, which is exactly where model 1's header begins, and
   every face index in all four models lands inside its own vertex
   count.

   The credits name "3D-Models" as a job - see effects/scroller.txt
   section 4 - so the models are expected to be there.

1.  The layout

   +--------+--------+-------------------------------------------------+
   | Offset | Size   | What                                            |
   +========+========+=================================================+
   | +0     | word   | vertex count N                                  |
   +--------+--------+-------------------------------------------------+
   | +2     | N x 3  | vertices: x, y, z as SIGNED BYTES               |
   +--------+--------+-------------------------------------------------+
   | then   | byte   | face count F                                    |
   +--------+--------+-------------------------------------------------+
   | then   | F x 10 | faces                                           |
   +--------+--------+-------------------------------------------------+

   A face record:

   +--------+--------+-------------------------------------------------+
   | +0     | byte   | THIS FACE'S vertex count, 3 or 4                |
   +--------+--------+-------------------------------------------------+
   | +1     | byte   | colour or shading attribute                     |
   +--------+--------+-------------------------------------------------+
   | +2     | word   | vertex index 0, ONE-BASED                       |
   | +4     | word   | index 1                                         |
   | +6     | word   | index 2                                         |
   | +8     | word   | index 3; for a triangle this REPEATS index 2    |
   +--------+--------+-------------------------------------------------+

   A TRIANGLE STORES ITS THIRD INDEX TWICE, which is why
   Mesh_FaceNormals at img 0x4418 can read four indices and average
   them unconditionally without caring how many sides the face has
   [verified]. The +0 byte is there for whatever walks the edges.

   Indices are one-based: Mesh_FaceNormals does `shl bx, 5` then reads
   `[bx - 0x20]`, so a stored index k addresses vertex k-1 at the
   32-byte stride of the transformed array [verified].

2.  The four models

   The four g_MeshFaces values the script stores, at img 0x0466,
   0x04ea, 0x51a3 and 0x52c9 [verified]:

   +----------+-------+-------+------+------+-----------------+----------------+
   | At img   | Verts | Faces | Tris | Quads| Attributes      | Extent x,y,z   |
   +==========+=======+=======+======+======+=================+================+
   | 0x572f   |    17 |    25 |   20 |    5 | 0x20, 0xc0      | 54 x 29 x 44   |
   +----------+-------+-------+------+------+-----------------+----------------+
   | 0x585f   |    34 |    45 |   26 |   19 | 0x20, 0xc0      | 58 x 25 x 41   |
   +----------+-------+-------+------+------+-----------------+----------------+
   | 0x94f4   |    54 |    54 |   30 |   24 | 0x00,0x20,0x60  | 30 x 12 x 43   |
   +----------+-------+-------+------+------+-----------------+----------------+
   | 0x5c47   |   120 |    94 |   12 |   82 | 0x08            | 55 x 40 x 10   |
   +----------+-------+-------+------+------+-----------------+----------------+

   The first two are compact and elongated along z with a pointed end -
   the shape of a craft. The last is FLAT, 10 units deep against 55 by
   40, and almost all quads with one attribute: that is extruded
   lettering or a logo, not a vehicle [inferred].

   WHAT IS ON SCREEN, reported from running the demo [observed]: a
   spaceship at the start; a second spaceship that duels it mid-demo,
   firing lasers; a car that drives over the landscape near the end;
   and 3D letters reading "THE END" over a plasma, with a water-wave
   effect over them.

   Four models and four described objects, and the dimensions line up
   one-to-one - 0x572f and 0x585f as the two craft, 0x94f4 long and
   flat in y as the car, 0x5c47 flat in z as the letters.

   +----------+---------------+------------------------------------------+
   | Model    | Set at sync   | What it is                               |
   +==========+===============+==========================================+
   | 0x572f   | 0x14, 0x15    | on screen WITH 0x585f, so the two craft  |
   |          |               | of the battle                            |
   +----------+---------------+------------------------------------------+
   | 0x585f   | 0x14, 0x23    | two separate passages - the craft that   |
   |          |               | recurs                                   |
   +----------+---------------+------------------------------------------+
   | 0x94f4   | 0x3a          | THE CAR. Its part is the only one that   |
   |          |               | calls Terrain_HeightAt, and twice        |
   +----------+---------------+------------------------------------------+
   | 0x5c47   | 0x4b          | THE "THE END" LETTERS - the part         |
   |          |               | straight after the credits scroll        |
   +----------+---------------+------------------------------------------+

   The two decisive pieces of evidence are structural rather than
   visual. Terrain_HeightAt answers "how high is the ground here",
   which is what something driving over the landscape needs and an
   aircraft does not; and an end card goes after the credits.

   Both agree with what the demo shows [observed] - 0x572f recognised
   as the attacking ship, the other craft seen in several scenes - and
   neither was derived from it.

   THAT IS NOT THE WHOLE SET. Scanning every store to g_MeshFaces
   found SIX models: these four plus 0x5b81 and 0x5a8a, the two
   weapons Shots_Draw alternates by music position, and 0x5be7, the
   flag Car_Run plants beside the terrain. effects/battle.txt section
   5 lists all six with their counts.

   The planets are not meshes - they are Planet_Render's mapped
   sphere, effects/planet.txt.

3.  The pipeline

   +-----------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | Mesh_SetupTransform   | 1000:45eb, the object-to-camera offset     |
   | Mesh_TransformVerts   | 1000:4960, per vertex                      |
   | Vec_Rotate3           | 1000:4a22, three Euler angles              |
   | Mesh_FaceNormals      | 1000:43d4, per face: centre and normal     |
   | Mesh_Draw             | 1000:465c, cull, light, sort, fill         |
   | Poly_Fill             | 1000:4b2c, the rasteriser                  |
   | Terrain_HeightAt      | 1000:3d93, puts an object on the ground    |
   +-----------------------+--------------------------------------------+

   1000:43d4 IS NOT A RENDERER - nothing in it draws. It computes a
   face centre and a unit normal, which is a preprocessor; the
   rasteriser is Poly_Fill and it is a separate, general routine.

   Per vertex, img 0x4978-0x4a19 [verified]:

       x = sx << 3
       y = sy << 3
       z = (sz * g_MeshZScale) >> 5
       Vec_Rotate3 by g_ObjPitch, g_ObjYaw, g_ObjRoll
       x += g_ObjTransX ; y += g_ObjTransY ; z += g_ObjTransZ
       if z < 0x2328: z = 0x2328
       d = z >> 3
       screen x = ((x << 4) / d) + 0xa0
       screen y = ((y << 4) / d) + 0x48 + g_SkyBias

   So the focal length is 128 and the centre is (0xa0, 0x48) - and
   0x48 IS THE SAME HORIZON CONSTANT Voxel_Render uses, so a model and
   the landscape share a horizon by construction rather than by tuning.

   The transformed array is 32 bytes a vertex: the rotated x, y, z as
   dwords at +0, +4, +8, the projected screen x and y as words at +0xc
   and +0xe, then scratch.

   The near clamp at 0x2328 is not defensive. Without it the divide
   would fault on a vertex behind the camera, and clamping rather than
   culling means a face straddling the plane STRETCHES instead of
   disappearing - a visible artefact that is part of the effect.

3.0.  Where the object is put

   img 0x45eb - Mesh_SetupTransform, read in full [verified]:

       g_ObjPitch = 0
       g_ObjYaw   = cs:[0x987d], THE CAMERA'S OWN HEADING
       g_ObjRoll  = 0

       v = (cs:[0x9368] - cs:[0x9360],              the camera's
            -((cs:[0x9460] - g_CamScale) << 5),      target, minus
            cs:[0x936c] - cs:[0x9364])               the camera
       v = Vec_Rotate3(v, pitch, yaw, roll) >> 6
       v.z -= v.z >> 3                               ie. times 7/8
       g_ObjTransX/Y/Z = v

   SO THE OBJECT SITS AT THE CAMERA'S TARGET AND FACES ALONG ITS
   HEADING. 0x9368 and 0x936c are the same target pair the second
   camera handler reads - ../effects/voxel.txt section 8.3 - so the
   craft and the camera are aimed at one point by construction.

   The `sar 6` here is the shift Mesh_TransformVerts omits, which is
   section 4.

3.0.1.  Four values that are globals, not constants

   EVERY ONE OF THESE WAS A PLAUSIBLE CONSTANT AND NONE OF THEM IS.
   Each is read from the variable block at draw time, so a static
   value taken from the file is only right until something writes it.

   +-----------------+---------+----------------+--------------------+
   | What            | At img  | Read at img    | Countdown value    |
   +=================+=========+================+====================+
   | the model       | 0x98ad  | 0x4960         | 0x585f, 34 verts   |
   | the z scale     | 0x9374  | 0x498f         | 0x100              |
   | the y term      | 0x9460  | 0x4606         | 0x0a10             |
   | the y bias      | 0x996d  | 0x49f9         | -39                |
   +-----------------+---------+----------------+--------------------+

   o THE MODEL IS WHICHEVER img 0x98ad NAMES, not the first in the
     block. 0x572f is a 17-vertex model belonging to some other part.

   o THE Z SCALE IS 0x100, so img 0x498f's `imul` and `sar 5` scale z
     by 8 - exactly what img 0x4999 does to x and y with `shl 3`. The
     transform is UNIFORM. A larger value stretches the model along
     its own axis and changes nothing else, which reads as a wrong
     model rather than a wrong number.

   o THE Y TERM IS NOT ZERO once the landscape has flown. img 0x4606
     takes g_9460 minus a MOVZX of g_CamScale - unsigned, so the
     difference is signed only through g_9460. Left at zero the
     object hangs about a screen and a half low.

   o THE Y BIAS IS A SECOND ADDEND after the 0x48 at img 0x49f6, and
     it is zero in the file. At the countdown it is -39, which is
     about a fifth of the screen.

   All four are in the same state the landscape's flight leaves them,
   and that flight is unported - ../effects/voxel.txt section 8.3.

3.1.  Signed or unsigned, per divide

   EVERY DIVIDE THAT CAN SEE A NEGATIVE NUMERATOR IS IDIV, which
   truncates toward zero. A transcription that floors instead differs
   by one wherever the numerator is negative [verified]:

   +----------------------+-----------------------+-----------------+
   | Function             | Divides               | Signed?         |
   +======================+=======================+=================+
   | Mesh_TransformVerts  | img 0x49e5, 0x49f4    | idiv - signed   |
   | Poly_Fill            | img 0x4bf7, 0x4c06,   | idiv - signed   |
   |                      | 0x4d3a                |                 |
   | Mesh_FaceNormals     | img 0x45b1, 0x45c4,   | idiv - signed   |
   |                      | 0x45d7                |                 |
   | Mesh_Draw            | img 0x475d, 0x486f    | div - UNSIGNED  |
   +----------------------+-----------------------+-----------------+

   Mesh_Draw's two are the sort key's 4/3 and the averaged vertex
   shade, and both operands are non-negative there, so the choice is
   deliberate rather than incidental.

   THE DIFFERENCE IS VISIBLE, NOT THEORETICAL. One frame of model
   0x572f at pitch 0x400, yaw 0x800, z 0x8000: truncating and flooring
   disagree on 512 of 64,000 pixels - 333 of them lit in both but one
   or two shade indices apart, the rest edges off by a pixel.

   C's / truncates, so plain C is right here. sim/xcursion/parts.py
   floors, in mesh_transform, poly_fill and face_normal, and is wrong
   on the negative half - it carries an _idiv helper for exactly this
   and those three do not call it.

3.2.  The far gate, and which scene the craft belongs to

   img 0x2373 calls Mesh_SetupTransform and img 0x237f calls Mesh_Draw,
   with img 0x2376 between them:

       cmp ecx, 0x28000
       jae  skip

   ecx is the object z Mesh_SetupTransform just stored, and JAE IS
   UNSIGNED - so a negative z is a huge unsigned one and the craft is
   skipped. That reads correctly as "behind the camera, do not draw".

   IT REJECTS THE CRAFT FOR THE WHOLE OPENING, AND THAT IS CORRECT.
   Halting at img 0x2376 and reading ecx in a running demo [verified]:

   +---------------------+-------------+-------------+--------------+
   | Scene               | ecx         | As unsigned | Draw?        |
   +=====================+=============+=============+==============+
   | the opening         |    -321,740 | 0xfffb1734  | skipped      |
   | the countdown       |     +24,780 | 0x000060cc  | DRAWN        |
   +---------------------+-------------+-------------+--------------+

   Section 3.0's arithmetic predicts -321,740 for the opening from the
   statics alone, and that is the value the register holds - so the
   sign and axis mapping are right to the byte.

   SO THE CRAFT IS NOT IN THE OPENING AT ALL. A breakpoint on the call
   at img 0x237f never fires across 120 frames of the voxel scene and
   fires in the countdown, which is what section 2's "a spaceship at
   the start" was seeing: ../effects/scenes.txt has the countdown as
   "camera freezes on landscape and ship", and that is the first scene
   the craft appears in.

4.  The scale trap

   It reads as symmetrical and is not.

   Vec_Rotate3 returns its result MULTIPLIED BY 64. Mesh_SetupTransform
   removes that with `sar eax/ebx/ecx, 6` at img 0x4634.
   MESH_TRANSFORMVERTS DOES NOT. A vertex therefore stays at x64 while
   being added to a translation that was brought DOWN to the same scale
   from much larger world values.

   Put the shift in both places, as the symmetry invites, and every
   model collapses to about four pixels across. That is how the
   omission was found.

5.  Mesh_Draw: cull, light, sort, fill

   1000:465c, and it is the whole object renderer [verified].

   CULLING IS A 2D SCREEN-SPACE WINDING TEST, not the 3D normal: two
   edge vectors from vertex 2 into g_EdgeAX..g_EdgeBY and a cross
   product at img 0x472a. Negative is back-facing, and the face's sort
   key is set to 0.

   EVERY STEP OF IT IS 16-BIT, img 0x470c to 0x473b. The four edge
   terms are word subtractions of the words Mesh_TransformVerts stored
   at +0xc and +0xe, both IMULs keep only the low word, AND SO DOES
   THE SUBTRACTION BETWEEN THEM. Taking a 32-bit difference of two
   truncated products keeps the wrong sign wherever the true one
   exceeds a word.

   AND THE CULL ONLY ZEROES THE KEY - IT DOES NOT SKIP THE LIGHTING.
   img 0x473d stores the 0 and img 0x4744 jumps to img 0x4775, which
   is ONE INSTRUCTION SHORT of the normal read at img 0x4778, so a
   back-facing face still accumulates its intensity into all of its
   vertices [verified]. The face is not drawn; its light still counts.

   THAT IS LOAD-BEARING, not an oversight to tidy. With only the front
   faces contributing, every vertex averages the lit side alone and the
   model comes out far too bright and too contrasty. Measured against a
   running demo at order 0x09, the first five vertices' touch counts
   are 3, 3, 6, 6, 4 and their shades 6, 2, 32, 39, 32; skipping the
   lighting on a cull gives 2, 3, 3, 2, 1 and 66, 46, 16, 20, 50 -
   shades past the band's own 0x3f, which spills a face into the next
   palette band and turns a blue window cyan. Accumulating for culled
   faces too makes all ten exact [observed].

   THE SORT KEY IS THE SUMMED VERTEX Z, never divided. img 0x474c
   scales a triangle's three-vertex sum by 4/3 so it compares against
   a quad's four - cheaper than a mean and it orders identically.

   LIGHTING IS PER FACE, ACCUMULATED PER VERTEX:

       n = the normal Mesh_FaceNormals left, << 4
       rotate by g_ObjPitch/Yaw/Roll into world space, >> 6
       rotate again by g_LightPitch/Yaw/Roll into light space
       i = (x >> 0xc) + 0x12c, floored at 0, then SQUARED and >> 0xe
       add i to every vertex of the face, and count the touches

   The square is the falloff - a plain Lambert term is linear - and
   0x12c is the ambient floor. Then img 0x4848 divides each vertex's
   accumulated intensity by its touch count and scales by
   g_ShadeScale >> 9.

   THE NORMAL IS IN MODEL SPACE AND IS ROTATED EXACTLY ONCE, so the
   lighting here is ordinary. Mesh_FaceNormals is a PREPROCESSOR, not
   a per-frame step: it is called four times in the whole demo - img
   0x014c, 0x0474, 0x51aa and 0x52de, once per model - and it sets up
   its own fixed reference pose first, zero angles and a translation of
   (0, 0, 0x1e000) at img 0x43d4. img 0x47b5 then takes that stored
   normal into world space and the light rotation finishes the job.

   IT LEAVES THAT POSE BEHIND. Nothing restores g_ObjTransX, Y or Z
   afterwards, so A PART THAT SETS ITS OWN TRANSLATION AND THEN BUILDS
   NORMALS GETS 0x1e000 BACK. The THE END part does exactly that - img
   0x52a4 asks for 0x20000 and img 0x52de overwrites it three
   instructions later - and its letters consequently start 0x2000
   nearer than that read of the script suggests. A transcription that
   treats the reference pose as local to the routine puts them 32
   ticks' worth too far away for the whole of the tumble, and the
   white flash that follows then jumps in size instead of matching.
   effects/theend.txt section 1.

   TAKING THE NORMAL FROM THE ROTATED VERTICES INSTEAD IS THE TRAP, and
   it is a plausible-looking one: the shading still moves with the
   object, so it reads as lighting. What it actually does is rotate the
   normal twice - the light then sweeps at roughly twice the object's
   rate, faces go dark pointing at the viewer, and a craft lit from the
   front in the demo is lit from behind. A reconstruction with this
   wrong shows two barely-lit ships where the demo shows two clear
   ones.

   The two rotations do not use the same yaw, and that part is real:
   the vertices take g_ObjYaw + g_CamAngleNext at img 0x4668 and the
   normal g_ObjYaw alone at img 0x47a1.

   THE LIGHT DIRECTION IS DELIBERATE, NOT RANDOM. Four writes in
   Demo_Entry set the triple and the parts between them inherit it
   [verified]:

   +-----------+------------------------+--------------------------+
   | At img    | pitch, yaw, roll       | Holds for                |
   +===========+========================+==========================+
   | statics   | 0x100, 0xff00, 0       | the opening              |
   | 0193      | 0x100, 0x421, 0x200    | THE LAUNCH, and the only |
   |           |                        | part that does not use   |
   |           |                        | 0xff00 or 0xfe00         |
   | 02b4      | 0, 0xfe00, 0           | the planet               |
   | 037f      | 0, 0xff00, 0           | the star cut, both roto  |
   |           |                        | passes, the chase and    |
   |           |                        | the first two fights     |
   | 0657      | 0x100, 0xff00, 0       | the formation fight, the |
   |           |                        | aftermath, the warp out  |
   +-----------+------------------------+--------------------------+

   SO THE SHADING IS TRUE GOURAUD FROM AVERAGED FACE NORMALS, which is
   why Poly_Fill interpolates four independent vertex shades rather
   than taking one per face.

   THE SORT IS A SELECTION SORT, img 0x4894: scan every face for the
   largest key, zero it, draw it, repeat, stop when no key is positive.
   Far to near, and culled faces are already 0 so they fall out for
   free. O(n^2) on up to 94 faces, which the demo evidently found
   acceptable at 100 Hz.

   An object's yaw is RELATIVE TO THE CAMERA: img 0x4664 latches
   g_ObjYaw + g_CamAngleNext. And the clip rectangle latched at
   img 0x4698 is the whole screen, 0..0x13f by 0..0xc7.

   Static light values from the variable block: g_LightPitch 0x100,
   g_LightYaw 0xff00, g_LightRoll 0, g_ShadeScale 0x190.

6.  What the reconstruction does

   sim/xcursion/parts.py's mesh_draw, part 12 of sim/xcursion/demo.py. 7 to
   13 ms a frame for the four models.

   NOTHING IN THE 3D PATH IS OURS ANY MORE. parse_model, vec_rotate3,
   face_normal, mesh_draw and poly_fill transcribe Mesh_Draw,
   Mesh_TransformVerts, Mesh_FaceNormals, Vec_Rotate3 and Poly_Fill
   including the 2D winding cull, the summed-z key with its 4/3
   triangle scale, the squared Lambert term, the per-vertex averaging,
   the selection sort, the span buffer's reuse and each face's own
   palette band. An earlier draft of this section listed four
   divergences - the cull, the sort, the shade and the band - and all
   four are now the demo's.

   Two things remain ours and both are outside the renderer: the
   ROTATION RATE, standing in for whichever animation handler drives
   the object - g_AnimMode 8, 0xc, 0x18 and 0x1a for the four models,
   all unread - and the model's DISTANCE.

   One optional divergence, default OFF and labelled on screen: the L
   key skips the normal's world-space rotation, which pins the light
   near the viewer instead of letting it turn with the object. It is
   OURS and exists only so the two can be put side by side. The C key
   adds camera yaw to the normal's rotation, which is the term the
   vertex transform includes at img 0x4668 and the normal rotation
   does not.

   THE NORMALS COME FROM mesh_normals, the reference pose - section 5.
   Both it and src/xcursion/xcursion_mesh.c reproduce the demo's
   stored table byte for byte, checked against segment 0x3a79 offset
   0x8000 for craft A and 0x6a79 for craft B in a running demo
   [observed].

   SO DOES THE SHADING. The demo leaves each vertex's accumulated
   intensity and touch count in the same record, at +0x10 and +0x14,
   and Mesh_Draw overwrites +0x10 with the final shade - so both are
   readable out of a paused demo. src/ matches all five shades and all
   five touch counts at order 0x09 and again at order 0x0b [observed].

   One deliberate substitution, with a proof rather than a guess:
   isqrt_as_demo replaces the descending-search square root at img
   0x457f, which costs about 12000 Python iterations a face. Checked
   against the loop over 0..5000, both boundaries and 400 random values
   below 0x4000 squared - one difference, sq = 0, where the loop
   returns 1 and so avoids a divide by zero. That case is handled
   explicitly, so the outputs are identical.

7.  The attribute byte

   THE ATTRIBUTE BYTE IS THE PALETTE BAND BASE. Mesh_Draw copies it
   straight into g_PolyBandBase at img 0x4947, so a face's colours
   are a 6-bit Gouraud ramp starting at its own attribute - 0x20 the
   blue band, 0xc0 the greyscale, and so on.

8.  Open questions

   o THE SHADE RUNS THE WRONG WAY ACROSS THE CRAFT. At the countdown
     frame the demo's hull is dark on the left and light on the
     right; the reconstruction's is the mirror of that, over a
     silhouette that otherwise matches to within a few pixels. Every
     input has been checked against the running demo and agrees -
     g_LightPitch 0x100 at img 0x9883, g_LightYaw 0xff00 at 0x9885,
     g_LightRoll 0 at 0x990f, the object angles at 0x9909/0x9881/
     0x990b, and the term itself: img 0x47e5 takes the rotated X,
     `sar 0xc`, `add 0x12c`, clamps at zero and squares with
     `sar 0xe`. So the discrepancy is in Mesh_FaceNormals' sign or in
     the `movsx` at img 0x4790 that the reconstruction omits, and
     neither has been read against the binary yet.

   o **Which model is used where.** Four g_MeshFaces stores are known;
     which part each belongs to has not been traced.

   o **g_MeshZScale at 1000:9374.** It scales z before rotation, so it
     is a depth stretch. Its writers have not been chased.

   o **Whether a fifth model exists.** Model 1's faces end at img
     0x5a8a and model 3 starts at 0x5c47, leaving 445 unaccounted
     bytes between them.
