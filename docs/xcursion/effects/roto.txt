PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                   EXCURSION: THE ROTOZOOMER


Abstract

   Roto_Render at 1000:2f28, runner Roto_Run at 1000:25b5. One
   renderer serves two scenes that look nothing alike: the "HUI"
   cuts at sync 0x0c, and the plasma rotozoom at sync 0x26.

   THE LETTERS ARE NOT DRAWN OVER THE EFFECT. Text_BuildBanner
   blends them INTO the 256x256 field before the part begins, so the
   rotozoom turns them with everything else. That is the whole trick
   and it is why the word rotates rather than sitting on top.

1.  The renderer

   img 0x2f28, once per frame [verified]:

       zoom  = g_RotoZoom   at img 0x94bc
       angle = g_RotoAngle  at img 0x9961
       du = (sin(angle) * zoom) >> 12            img 0x2f4c
       dv = (cos(angle) * zoom) >> 12            img 0x2f5a

       u = -160 * du + 100 * dv                  img 0x2f62
       v = -160 * dv - 100 * du

   So the start is the texture coordinate of the screen's top left
   carried through the same rotation, and img 0x2f62 assembles both
   out of shifts and adds rather than a multiply - 32 plus 128 for
   the 160, 4 plus 32 plus 64 for the 100.

   The texel is addressed by the HIGH BYTES of the two accumulators,
   img 0x2fd1, so the field is 256x256 and wraps for free.

   The destination is two segments, img 0x98b7 and img 0x98b5 - the
   work buffer and 0xa000 itself. A reconstruction with one
   framebuffer writes once.

2.  Why it looks soft

   TWO THINGS, AND NEITHER IS A SIDE EFFECT.

   IT DRAWS A QUARTER OF THE PIXELS A RENDER. The walk is 160 by 100,
   stepping two across and two down, and img 0x2fa0 offsets the
   start by a pixel in x, a row in y, or both, from the low two bits
   of its own counter at img 0xa87b. Four renders cover the screen, so
   parts of the image are always a render or two old.

   SO HOW OFTEN IT RENDERS IS PART OF THE EFFECT, not a performance
   detail. Roto_Run waits a tick per render at img 0x25b8 and the
   animation modes run every other tick, which would be two renders an
   animation pass if the render kept up; Roto_Render's 32000 byte
   writes do not, and THE MEASURED RATE IS ONE RENDER PER PASS - zoom
   868 to 660 against g_RotoPhase 2 to 6, four passes and four renders
   [observed].

   Halving that doubles how much rotation the four-phase interleave
   spans. Adjacent columns then differ by two passes of angle instead
   of one, and the result combs rather than resolving - most visible
   at the end, where the freeze is supposed to let the blend converge
   and stamp the word.

   AND IT BLENDS RATHER THAN WRITES. img 0x2fd5:

       new = old + ((texel - old) >> g_RotoBlend)

   with g_RotoBlend 2 at img 0xa59d, so each pass moves a quarter of
   the way toward the new texture. The subtraction is a BYTE one and
   the shift is signed, so the step is signed in both directions.

   Together they give the smeared spiral the scene is known for.
   Writing the texel straight gives a hard, flickering rotozoom that
   is recognisably the wrong effect.

   WITH THE RATE RIGHT THE WHOLE EFFECT IS EXACT. src/ matches the
   demo's screen byte for byte across 128 pixels of row 100 at order
   0x0c row 0x17 [observed], which covers the texture, the banner
   blended into it, the blend, the interleave, the phase and both
   animated terms at once.

3.  The banner

   img 0x33a0 Text_BuildBanner, and it writes to the CALLER'S
   segment, which at img 0x0316 is the field at 0x2a79 [verified].

   img 0x33ec halves what is already there and adds g_BannerBright,
   0x3f at img 0xa59e, so a glyph is a lightened copy of the field
   beneath it and carries the texture with it.

   It takes a record of the same shape the caption masks use - text
   pointer, destination offset, row count - through a pointer at img
   0x98bb, which holds img 0x97d1: the text at img 0xa588, "  HUI!  ",
   one line at offset 0xef80. Eight glyphs to a line at img 0x33b6,
   32 pixels apart, and a row strides 0x100 because the field is 256
   wide.

4.  The part, and the cut

   img 0x02ec, sync 0x0d, runner Roto_Run:

       g_RotoZoom  = 0x400                        img 0x02ec
       g_RotoAngle = 0                            img 0x02f6
       g_AnimMode  = 5                            img 0x02fd
       g_FadeLevel = 0                            img 0x0303
       Pal_BuildBlue, Pal_Fade                    img 0x030d, 0x0310
       HALVE EVERY BYTE of the field              img 0x0322
       clear the work segment
       Text_BuildBanner                           img 0x0335

   THE CUT IS THE MUSIC AND NOTHING ELSE. Mode 5 at img 0x1093 tests
   g_MusicRow, the row counter inside the current pattern:

       row <= 0x18 : zoom -= 0x34, angle += 0x18
       otherwise   : fade -= 0x100 and Pal_Fade

   THE TICK RATE IS NOT 6 HERE. g_TicksPerRow reads 3 by this part
   [observed], so a row is three sequencer ticks and six animation
   ones - the modes run on every timer tick and the sequencer on
   every other. Rows 22 to 24 are therefore about eighteen passes of
   mode 5, not the thirty-six a rate of 6 would give.

   IT INHERITS THE COUNTER AT 22, NOT 0. The part before this one
   ends on Planet_Render's row condition - section 4.1 - which fires
   exactly at g_9917, 0x16. So mode 5 has only the three rows from 22
   to 24 to turn in, about thirteen ticks, and then everything
   freezes [observed: zoom 0x400 to 348, angle 0 to 312, in ten
   frames from the part's first].

   THE FREEZE IS WHAT STAMPS THE WORD. While the angle moves, the
   blend in section 2 never catches up and the field stays smeared.
   The moment it stops the same blend converges on the texture within
   a few frames, so the letters resolve hard out of the smear and
   then the fade takes them. Rotate, stop, stamp, fade - in that
   order, and all of it out of one counter.

   A part entered with the counter at 0 instead turns for the full 24
   rows. The zoom runs to about -6500 by then, which tiles the field
   some thirty times across, and the letters never resolve - the
   effect reads as noise that happens to be blue.

   The zoom falls by 0x34 a tick from 0x400 and would pass through
   zero after about twenty ticks, mirroring the field beyond it. It
   does not get that far here: the freeze lands it around 200 to 350,
   which is roughly one copy of the field across the screen and is
   why the letters come out full size.

4.1.  It runs twice, with a cut of stars between

   THE PART ENDS ON ITS SYNC AND THAT MATTERS. Left running past it
   the zoom keeps falling, the field tiles finer every tick and the
   letters go with it, so the scene is only legible inside its own
   pattern. Three parts in a row [verified]:

   +-------+------+-------+---------------------------------------+
   | At img| Sync | Mode  | What it is                            |
   +=======+======+=======+=======================================+
   | 02ec  | 0x0d |   5   | the first roto. Blue palette, the     |
   |       |      |       | field halved, the banner blended in   |
   +-------+------+-------+---------------------------------------+
   | 0342  | -    |   4   | a cut of the craft against stars,     |
   |       |      |       | ball off at 0xfa000                   |
   +-------+------+-------+---------------------------------------+
   | 03b4  | 0x0e |   5   | the second roto, angle 0x100. It      |
   |       |      |       | clears only the work buffer, so the   |
   |       |      |       | letters from the first are still in   |
   |       |      |       | the field                             |
   +-------+------+-------+---------------------------------------+

   THE STAR CUT HAS NO SYNC OF ITS OWN. Planet_Render loops on two
   conditions, img 0x29ad and img 0x29ba - the order AND the row
   counter against g_9917, which the part sets to 0x16. The order is
   already past its target, so the burst lasts a fixed 22 rows and
   then the second roto takes over.

   The letters read best on the SECOND pass. The first begins at
   angle 0 with the zoom at its most tiled, and the second starts
   fresh at 0x100 with the field already carrying the glyphs.

   Both entry states read out of the running demo, one to three
   ticks in [observed]: the first at row 22, zoom 0x400, angle 0;
   the second at row 22, zoom 868 and angle 328, which is 0x100 plus
   three ticks of 0x18.

   EVERY ONE OF THESE PARTS CLEARS THE SCREEN - img 0x030a, 0x039a
   and 0x03cb. The star cut's clear is the one that matters most: it
   rebuilds the full palette, so a frame left holding the
   rotozoom's pixels would be shown through the land and water bands
   and the blue scene turns brown on its way out.

   THE SECOND PASS MUST STOP AT ITS SYNC. Nothing in mode 5 bounds
   the effect by itself, so a part left running sees the pattern
   roll over, the row counter reset to zero, and the full 24-row
   turn start again from whatever zoom it had reached - a long smear
   that belongs to no part at all.

4.2.  And a third time, on a plasma

   img 0x07b0, sync 0x26, mode 0xe, the same Roto_Run [verified]:

       g_RotoZoom = 0x30c, g_RotoAngle = 0        img 0x07b0
       g_AnimMode = 0xe                           img 0x07c1
       g_FadeLevel = 0, Pal_Fade                  img 0x07c7
       Pal_BuildBlueCyan                          img 0x07ce
       rough 1, bias 0x800, mask 0xfff            img 0x07d9
       Plasma_Generate                            img 0x07ed
       HALVE EVERY BYTE, and clear the work       img 0x07fa
       g_MusicPosTarget = 0x26                    img 0x080c
       Roto_Run                                   img 0x0813
       Tunnel_BuildDepth                          img 0x0816

   MODE 0xe IS MODE 5 AT A FORTIETH OF THE SPEED - two off the zoom
   and two onto the angle a pass against 0x34 and 0x18 - so this one
   drifts where the banner scenes spiral. The fade at img 0x15b7 only
   arrives once the order reaches the target, so it is the last pass
   or two.

   THE HALVING AND THE HALF-PALETTE ARE ONE DECISION. Pal_BuildBlueCyan
   at 1000:3bf4 writes only entries 0..127, black to blue to cyan, and
   leaves 128..255 as the warp part's palette left them. img 0x07fa
   shifts every byte of the field right one, so nothing ever indexes
   the upper half. Reading the half-build as unfinished, or leaving the
   field full-range, each break the other.

   Clearing the work buffer in the same pass is what gives the blend
   black to converge out of, so the plasma rises rather than cutting
   in.

   THE PLASMA DEPENDS ON THE BATTLE. It is the fifth draw on the shared
   stream and the fourth was the warp's, two scenes back - and between
   them the glow's particles draw unparked. Measured, the stream
   advances 1536 between them: 512 from mode 0xc's trickle, 64 for the
   warp-out burst, and 64 for each of fifteen hits in the formation
   fight [observed]. Get the hit count wrong and this part gets a
   different cloud. effects/script1_landscape.txt section 6.1.

   Tunnel_BuildDepth at img 0x0816 belongs to the part that follows,
   not to this one - it fills two 64000-byte radial tables while the
   screen is already cleared.

5.  Roto_RunWithMesh, and the buffer that moves

   Roto_RunWithMesh at img 0x25f7 is the renderer with a craft over
   it [verified] - Roto_Render, Mesh_Draw, Vga_Present, then
   Demo_WaitTick, so it renders ONCE PER TIMER TICK rather than flat
   out. Its one part is img 0x4e97, sync 0x35, mode 0x15: the moon's
   own surface seen from just behind the craft.

   THE RENDERER HAS TWO DESTINATIONS AND READS BACK FROM ONLY ONE.
   img 0x2fe0 stores the blend through DS and img 0x2fe2 stores it
   again through FS, but img 0x2fd8 takes the OLD value through DS
   alone. DS comes from cs:0x98b7 and FS from cs:0x98b5, so one of
   them is the feedback the blend accumulates in and the other only
   ever receives a copy.

   Their statics are FS = 0xa000 and DS = 0x5a79 [verified] - the
   feedback in the work buffer, the copy straight to the screen - and
   every earlier rotozoom runs on those, which is why the two read as
   one buffer everywhere else.

   THIS PART SWAPS THEM, img 0x4e9a and img 0x4f51: DS becomes 0x7a79
   and FS the work buffer. It is the only rotozoom with a craft drawn
   over it, and Mesh_Draw writes the work buffer - so leaving the
   feedback there would blend the craft into the surface and smear it
   across the following frames. Moving the accumulator to a buffer
   nothing else writes is what keeps the craft off its own trail.

   0x7a79 is the tunnel's eighth-scale depth table, finished with by
   now; img 0x4f2e clears it and the work buffer in the same pass
   that copies the shade map into the texture. That copy is PLAIN -
   the moon's part transforms the same map through (v << 2) + 0x80 on
   its way across and this one does not, so the two parts put
   different things in 0x2a79 from the same source.

   THE BLEND SHIFT IS A GLOBAL, NOT A CONSTANT. cs:0xa59d is 2 by
   static and this part sets it to 1 at img 0x4f58, which doubles the
   step each pass takes toward the new texture - a surface that
   settles twice as fast, because this one is moving under the
   camera rather than turning in place.

   MODE 0x15 CARRIES BOTH HALVES OF THE CUT. It climbs g_FadeLevel
   back to 0 by 0x100 a tick from wherever mode 0x14's fade-out left
   it, and past row 0x3c drives it down again - so one handler fades
   the part in and out, and the moon before it fades out through the
   same arithmetic running in mode 0x14.

   Only the climb reaches the screen: img 0x17cb leaves for the ISR
   tail without calling Pal_Fade, where the climb calls it at img
   0x17df. Mode 0x14's fade-out does call it, at img 0x178b. The
   level therefore keeps falling at the end of this part while the
   palette stops following it.

6.  Open questions

   o THE PART'S ENTRY COSTS RENDERS AND THE RECONSTRUCTION'S DOES
     NOT. img 0x07ed's generate is 0x10000 random draws and the
     halving loop another 0x10000 iterations, and mode 0xe keeps
     advancing the zoom throughout - so the demo is about twelve
     passes along with only about six renders done, and its rotation
     leads its convergence by that much. src/ enters instantly and
     reaches the same zoom with twice the renders, which shows as a
     plasma that has resolved further at the same angle. Measured:
     zoom 0x2f4 at order 0x25 row 2, screen row 100 running
     00 04 03 05 ... against src/'s 0a 08 10 14 ... [observed]. The
     lead is machine speed, not code, so nothing here is tuned to it.

   o What follows the second roto. img 0x03ee sets mode 6, rebuilds
     the plasma at mask 0x7ff, bias 0x400, roughness 1, takes the
     blue-purple palette and runs img 0x2639 - Warp_RenderFull - to
     sync 0x12. That is the plasma scene and it is not ported.
