PCDEMOS 64k                                            Analysis Note


              EXCURSION: THE AIRCRAFT, AND ITS RECORDED TRACK


Abstract

   The mode 0x16 animation handler at img 0x17e5 - 400 bytes, the
   largest in the table - is an aircraft flight model, and IT DOES NOT
   COMPUTE A PATH. It replays a recorded one: g_FlightTrack at
   img 0xcc1a, one byte of control flags per ten ticks.

   Six bits - pitch up, pitch down, roll left, roll right, throttle
   down, throttle up. No yaw bit, because the model derives yaw from
   roll the way an aircraft does.

   Its part is sync 0x37, whose runner is Landscape_Run - Voxel_Render
   with a mesh over it.

1.  The track

   g_FlightTrack at img 0xcc1a, walked by g_FlightTrackPtr
   [verified]:

       img 17e5   di = g_FlightTrackPtr
       img 17ea   g_TickDivider++
       img 17ef   every TEN ticks, g_FlightTrackPtr++
       img 1802   al = cs:[di]

   So one byte buys a tenth of a second at g_Speed 100, and the
   pointer only ever advances - there is no loop and no reset.

   THE MODE IS ARMED AFTER THE PART'S SETUP, img 0x4fb5 against the
   Plasma_Generate at img 0x4f91, so the craft does not integrate
   while the field is built - but the module's row counter does
   advance through it. Fifteen ticks of flight sit two rows into the
   pattern rather than one [observed], and how far apart those run is
   machine speed.

   422 bytes from 0xcc1a carry only the six valid bits, which is
   42.2 seconds; 92 of them are non-zero. img 0xcdc0 is the first byte
   that cannot be control flags [verified]. How much of the 422 the
   part actually consumes depends on how long sync 0x37 runs, which is
   set by the music and has not been measured.

   What the pilot did, counted over the first 160 bytes [verified]:

   +------------------------+-----------+
   | Input                  | Duration  |
   +========================+===========+
   | nothing                |   7.5 s   |
   | pitch up + roll left   |   1.6 s   |
   | pitch down + roll right|   1.5 s   |
   | roll right             |   1.2 s   |
   | pitch down             |   1.0 s   |
   | roll left              |   0.9 s   |
   | pitch down + roll left |   0.8 s   |
   | pitch up               |   0.7 s   |
   | pitch up + roll right  |   0.4 s   |
   | four throttle taps     |   0.4 s   |
   +------------------------+-----------+

   Almost all stick and almost no throttle, and nearly half the time
   hands off. That is a recording of someone flying, not a hand-authored
   table.

2.  The controls

   img 0x1802 to 0x1840, one bit test each [verified]:

       bit 0x01   g_ObjPitch += 4
       bit 0x02   g_ObjPitch -= 4
       bit 0x04   g_ObjRoll  -= 4
       bit 0x08   g_ObjRoll  += 4
       bit 0x10   g_FlightThrottle--
       bit 0x20   g_FlightThrottle++

   Nothing is clamped and nothing decays. Hold a bit and the value
   ramps; release it and the value stays. So the stick is a RATE
   control on attitude, not a position - which is what makes a
   ten-per-second sample rate enough.

3.  The banked turn

   One instruction, and it is why there is no yaw bit [verified]:

       img 1841   g_ObjYaw += g_ObjRoll >> 6

   ROLL INDUCES YAW. Bank the craft and it turns, at a sixty-fourth of
   the bank angle per tick, exactly as a real aircraft does and for the
   same reason a flight sim does it. The recording therefore only ever
   needed to say "stick left" and the turn follows.

4.  Integration, and the terrain

   img 0x1865 to 0x18f0 [verified]:

       latch pitch, yaw and roll
       ecx = g_FlightThrottle, CLAMPED TO AT MOST 0 at img 0x1871
       Vec_Rotate3 -> the velocity along the craft's heading
       ebx >>= 7 ; eax >>= 1 ; ecx >>= 1
       g_FlyX += eax ; g_FlyY -= ebx ; g_FlyZ += ecx

   The y term is shifted SIX PLACES FURTHER than x and z, so vertical
   motion from the heading is a sixty-fourth of the horizontal. The
   craft flies much flatter than it points.

       eax = (g_FlightThrottle >> 1) + 0x10
       if eax > 0: g_FlyY -= eax

   Gravity, and its strength depends on the throttle - more throttle,
   which is more negative, means less sink.

   g_FlightThrottle is -64 by static and the clamp at img 0x1871
   allows only values at or below 0, so MORE NEGATIVE IS FASTER.
   Nothing in the part resets it, and it is still -64 fifteen ticks
   into sync 0x35 [observed] - the track spends its throttle bits
   late or not at all.

   THE TERRAIN COLLISION LEVELS THE CRAFT OUT, img 0x18bd:

       ground = Terrain_HeightAt(g_FlyX, g_FlyZ) + 0x10
       if g_FlyY < ground:
           g_FlyY = ground
           g_ObjPitch += (0x30 - g_ObjPitch) >> 5
           g_ObjRoll  -= g_ObjRoll >> 5

   So touching the ground does three things at once: lifts the craft to
   the surface, eases its pitch toward a slight nose-up 0x30, and
   decays its roll toward level - both by the demo's usual
   shift-and-add. A crash becomes a recovery, which means the recorded
   track cannot fly the craft into the ground and lose it.

5.  The chase camera

       img 18fa   g_CamAngleNext -= 2
       img 1900   g_CamWorldX = g_FlyX - 8 * sin(g_CamAngleNext)
                  g_CamWorldZ = g_FlyZ - 8 * cos(g_CamAngleNext)

   A camera on a radius-8 arm that sweeps round the craft. Then a
   SECOND terrain probe, at the camera's own position, clamps
   cs:0x9871 to at least that ground plus 0xc8 - so the view is kept
   above the landscape independently of the craft.

   Two Terrain_HeightAt calls a tick, where the car's handler makes
   four.

   cs:0x9871 IS THE VOXEL CAMERA'S SCALE [verified]. img 0x18f1 puts
   the craft's altitude in it as a WORD, the camera probe raises it,
   and img 0x35eb - the latch Voxel_Render opens with - copies it
   into cs:0x9975, which effects/voxel.txt section 8.1 lists as the
   scale term. The same latch takes cs:0x9360 and cs:0x9364 into the
   camera's world position and cs:0x987d into its angle, so the
   handler writes sources and the renderer reads copies.

   THE PART FADES IN THROUGH THIS HANDLER, img 0x195e: while
   g_FadeLevel is non-zero it climbs by 0x100 a tick and calls
   Pal_Fade. The rotozoom part before it drives the level negative
   without applying it - effects/roto.txt section 5 - so the screen
   is still bright at the cut and darkens on this handler's first
   tick before climbing back.

6.  Why this is worth the bytes

   The track is 422 bytes for 42 seconds of flight. A keyframed spline
   over the same path, at even one key a second with three positions
   and three angles as words, would be 42 * 12 = 504 bytes and would
   still need interpolation code.

   Recording the INPUT instead of the OUTPUT means the flight model
   code - which the demo needs anyway to make the motion look right -
   doubles as the decompressor. That is the whole trick, and it is why
   the handler is the largest in the table while its data is the
   smallest it could be.

   The cost is that the path is not editable and not reproducible
   except by running the same model, which is exactly the property that
   makes it cheap.

7.  The recorder is not in the image

   Whatever captured the track was a separate tool. Three pieces of
   evidence, all of them negative and all of them checked [verified]:

   +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | Looked for                | Found                              |
   +===========================+====================================+
   | writes to g_FlightTrack   | NONE. img 0xcc1a has exactly one   |
   |                           | reference in the image and it is   |
   |                           | the read at img 0x1802             |
   +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | mouse support             | NONE. No INT 33h anywhere in the   |
   |                           | image                              |
   +---------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | keyboard beyond the exit  | ESC and F1 to F6, and they are a   |
   |                           | TIMER SELECTOR                     |
   +---------------------------+------------------------------------+

   The F-keys are worth spelling out, because six of them against six
   control bits is a coincidence that invites the wrong conclusion.
   Input_Poll at img 0x0ace reads port 0x60 and tests 0x3b to 0x40;
   every one of them lands at img 0x0aef, which computes

       cs:0x9867 = 0x19 << (scancode - 0x3c)

   and reprograms the PIT through img 0x0b37, so F2 to F6 give 25,
   50, 100, 200 and 400 Hz. F1 substitutes F2's scancode and raises
   g_RenderDisabled instead, which is a detail switch rather than a
   speed - see the plate on Input_Poll. Nothing in that path touches
   the craft.

   The only other port 0x60 read is img 0x55e9, a wait-for-keypress
   that spins on the break bit.

   So the demo ships the output of a recorder and not the recorder,
   which is what section 6 would predict: the replay needs the flight
   model, and the flight model is in here, but capture needs neither.

8.  Open questions

   o Where the track really ends. 422 bytes is where the first byte
     appears that cannot be control flags; whether the last stretch is
     used, padding, or a different asset is not established.

   o THE CHASE CAMERA RUNS ~20 TICKS AHEAD OF THE DEMO'S, because
     the part's setup costs the demo that much and costs src/ none.
     g_CamAngleNext falls 2 a tick, so at the same music position the
     demo is about 32 units - under three degrees - behind, closing
     to under one by the part's end [observed]. The craft itself is
     exact: at order 0x36 row 62 both have it at x -729112, z 242798.

     The offset is machine speed, like every other entry cost here,
     and injecting it would be tuning rather than transcription. What
     it does mean is that the flag part after this one inherits a
     camera angle a degree off, since img 0x50c2 takes whatever this
     part left and adds 0x200.

   o sim/ still has no part for this. sim/xcursion/parts.py has
     flight_tick and reads the track out of the image, but nothing
     drives the voxel camera and the mesh from g_FlyX/Y/Z together
     there. src/xcursion/xcursion_demo.c has scene_landing.
