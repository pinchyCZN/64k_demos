PCDEMOS 64k                                            Analysis Note


                    EXCURSION: THE WAVING FLAG


Abstract

   Flag_Render at 1000:2504 - the word VISTA, drawn into a field and
   then displaced by two scrolling samples of a sine field, written as
   2x2 blocks straight to 0xA000.

   Its part is sync 0x37 mode 0x17, between the ship landing and the
   car scene.

   THE ROUTINE WAS TWICE MISNAMED and once mis-described. Section 4
   says why, because the cause was a misreading of g_TickCount that
   affected other documents too.

1.  What it draws

   The source is text, not an image [verified]:

       img 5116   g_BannerTextRec = 0x97d7
       img 5123   Text_BuildBanner
       img 5144   Flag_Render

   The record at cs:0x97d7 is three words - string pointer 0xa590,
   destination offset 0x3000, length 0x20 - and the string at img
   0xa590 is "VISTA   !" [verified]. g_BannerRecs holds three such
   records; the other two are "bEXCURSION" and "  HUI!".

   So the flag's cloth is the word VISTA rendered from the same font
   and the same string block as every other caption in the demo -
   g_TextStrings at img 0xa523, through g_Font32 at img 0x615e, the
   demo's own 32x32 font. No flag bitmap exists in the image.

   AND Text_BuildBanner BLENDS RATHER THAN DRAWS. img 0x33ec does
   al = dest[di] >> 1 + g_BannerBright, which the part sets to 0x30 at
   img 0x511d - so the letters are a halved-and-brightened copy of
   whatever was already in the field. That is why the cloth carries
   the plasma's texture instead of being flat.

   IT WRITES THE CALLER'S SEGMENT and sets none itself, unlike
   Text_BuildMask which pins ES = 0x6a79. At img 0x5123 ES is 0x2a79 -
   what Voxel_Render left at img 0x364b - which is exactly the segment
   Flag_Render then reads as DS. The two are connected by a register
   nobody reloads.

1.1.  The field it is displaced by

   img 0x507a builds it, immediately before the part runs, so the
   question of which producer last wrote 0x8a79 + 0x4564 is settled
   [verified]: this part does.

   IT IS NOT THE WATER'S FIELD, though the two share a shape. Both
   are 128 by 128 sums of two sine lookups, but this one samples at
   SINGLE frequency where img 0x3a7f doubles it, shifts the sum right
   7 where that shifts 9, and adds a 0x80 bias where that leaves the
   result signed. The flag reads it unsigned, and the bias is what
   centres the ripple on no displacement.

2.  The displacement

   Per 2x2 block, img 0x2524 to 0x2584 [verified]:

       DS = 0x2a79   the source, holding the text
       ES = 0xa000   VGA, with no work buffer in between
       FS = g_WorkSeg
       GS = 0x8a79   the sine field, wrapped & 0x3fff

       bl = GS[(g_WarpOfsX + SI) & 0x3fff + 0x4564] >> 2 - 0x70
       al = GS[(g_WarpOfsY + SI) & 0x3fff + 0x4564] & 0xfc
       ax = (al << 6) - 0x4200
       bx = bl + ax + SI

   The y displacement is shifted left 6 BECAUSE THE SOURCE STRIDE IS
   0x100 - it is pre-multiplied into a row offset rather than being
   multiplied per sample.

       img 255a   the bounds test is BL < 0xa0 AND BH < 0x80, the two
                  halves of BX checked separately
       img 2574   out of range reads THE WORK BUFFER

   That fallback is the whole reason the scene works: the 186x152
   panel sits over whatever the previous frame left, so the landscape
   shows around and through the flag instead of a black box.

       img 258c   SI += 0x146, DI += 0x1c6 per row. With 93 columns of
                  2 that is 512 source bytes and 640 screen bytes -
                  two rows of each, so a half-resolution copy.

   The panel is 186x152 at x=70, y=24.

3.  The waving, and the two fades

   The mode 0x17 handler at img 0x1975 scrolls the two offsets while
   this runs [verified]:

       g_WarpOfsX++
       g_WarpOfsY += 0x102

   0x102 advances that word's low byte by 2 and its high byte by 1 -
   two rates in one add, the same idiom as mode 0's g_FireTexBase and
   the opposite side of the byte-packing Warp_RenderFull does.

   So the x phase creeps and the y phase moves at two speeds at once,
   which is what keeps the ripple from looking periodic.

   THE SAME HANDLER FADES THE PART IN AND OUT WITH ONE SUBTRACT
   [verified]. img 0x1993 takes 0xf0 off g_FadeLevel and applies it:

       row <= 0x1c and level == 0    leave it alone, img 0x1989
       otherwise                     level -= 0xf0, then Pal_Fade

   THE STEP IS 0xf0 AND NOT 0x100 BECAUSE OF WHERE IT STARTS. The
   part sets the level to 0x2d00 at img 0x5129, and 0x2d00 is exactly
   48 steps of 0xf0 - so the fade-in from white reaches zero on the
   nose and the test stops it there. A step of 0x100 would skip past
   zero and the scene would keep darkening instead of holding.

   Past row 0x1c the test stops guarding and the same subtract runs
   on, carrying the level below zero and fading the part out to
   black. One instruction, both transitions, and the part ends four
   rows later at the g_TickTarget of 0x20.

   The white it fades in FROM is not a fade at all: the previous
   part's tail sets the level to 0x2d00 and calls Pal_Fade once at
   img 0x5062, so the landing cuts to white in a single frame.

4.  The pole, and what is behind the cloth

   The part draws its backdrop ONCE and freezes it [verified]:

       img 50c2   g_CamAngleNext += 0x200
       img 50c9   Voxel_Render, one frame into the work buffer
       img 50d7   THE FLAGPOLE, straight over it
       img 5126   Vga_Present, the only one in the part

   The pole is two dwords of fixed gradient - 9f 98 90 88 80 78 70
   68 - stored at offset 0x1e48 and repeated down 0xb0 rows: eight
   pixels wide, 176 tall, at screen x 72, y 24. No model, no shading,
   no loop over anything but the rows.

   Flag_Render then writes STRAIGHT TO 0xA000 every frame with no
   present of its own, which is why the work buffer behind it never
   changes for the whole part. The out-of-range fallback at img
   0x2574 copies that frozen buffer back, so it both lets the
   landscape show around the cloth AND erases the cloth's previous
   position - the two buffers cannot be collapsed into one.

5.  The two errors

4.  The two errors

   The routine was maybe_Warp_Render, then Warp_RenderPanel with a
   note reading "ONE CALL SITE, and it is a TRANSITION not a scene".
   The reasoning was that img 0x25a7 makes the loop wait on
   g_TickTarget as well as the music position, and the part sets 0x20.

   G_TICKCOUNT COUNTS MUSIC ROWS, NOT TIMER TICKS. Timer_Isr
   increments it at img 0x0cb7 only once g_RowTickCount reaches
   g_TicksPerRow - statically 6 - and maybe_Music_Step zeroes it at
   img 0x222d on each order change. So it is rows elapsed within the
   current order position, and 0x20 is 32 rows: half a pattern, which
   is seconds.

   EVERY g_TickTarget AND g_TickCount THRESHOLD IN THE DEMO IS
   THEREFORE IN ROWS. The ones already recorded elsewhere - 0x1c in
   mode 0x17, 0x20 in mode 0xd, 0x3c in modes 0x14 and 0x15, 0x18 in
   modes 5 and 0xb - are all scene-scale, not sub-second.

   The second error followed from the first: a routine believed to run
   for a third of a second was not worth asking what it displaced, so
   the VISTA banner feeding it went unnoticed even though the call sits
   two instructions away at img 0x5123.
