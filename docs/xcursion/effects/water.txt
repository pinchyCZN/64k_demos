PCDEMOS 64k                                            Analysis Note


                      EXCURSION: THE WATER


Abstract

   Water_Step at 1000:29d4 is A 2D WAVE EQUATION SOLVER - the real
   thing, in eleven instructions - and it serves two scenes through
   two different renderers.

       sync 0x2d   Water_Run      Water_Step + Water_RenderSpans
       sync 0x52   TheEnd_RunWater  Mesh_Draw + Water_Step +
                                    Water_Refract

   So the dripping water in the middle of the demo and the water drop
   that closes it are the same solver, the same field and the same
   damping. Only the way the field reaches the screen differs.

1.  The solver

   img 0x29e9, per cell [verified]:

       ax = [di-2] + [di+2] + [di-0x140] + [di+0x140]
       ax >>= 1                     the four-neighbour sum, halved
       ax -= ES:[di]                MINUS THE OLD VALUE
       ax -= ax >> CL               the damping
       ES:[di] = ax

   new = mean(neighbours) * 2 - old is the discrete wave equation in
   its standard two-timestep form. SUBTRACTING THE OLD VALUE IS THE
   SECOND TIME DERIVATIVE, and it is the reason two buffers are
   needed rather than one - img 0x2a10 XCHGs g_WaterBufA and
   g_WaterBufB at the end of every step, so their roles alternate.

   THE DAMPING IS A SHIFT, NOT A MULTIPLY: ax -= ax >> 4 is ax * 15/16
   with g_WaterDamping at its static 4. Without it the wave would ring
   forever.

   Eleven instructions, two memory buffers and one shift is the whole
   of it. There is no velocity field and no explicit timestep.

   IT PROCESSES ROWS 1 TO 100 OF A 0 TO 99 GRID. img 0x29de starts at
   word 160 and img 0x29e1 counts 0x3e80 = 16000 cells, so it runs one
   row past the end, and the +0x140 neighbour read reaches a row
   beyond that again - the same over-run Water_Refract has. Section 6.

2.  The grid, and what drives it

   THE GRID IS 160 x 100 SIGNED WORDS - not bytes, and not 320 wide
   [verified]. DI steps by 2, the vertical neighbours are at
   +/-0x140 BYTES which is 160 words, and the loop count 0x3e80 is
   16000 = 160 * 100. Each cell drives a 2x2 screen block.

   The buffer pair is the script's choice, not the solver's:

       img 096b, 0972   0x5a79 and 0x2a79, for sync 0x2d - and
                        img 0x0962 and 0x0968 CLEAR BOTH first
       img 53a0, 53a7   0x6a79 and 0x7a79, for the finale, which
                        leaves g_WorkSeg free to hold the letters

   WHAT DRIVES IT IN THE FINALE IS ONE WORD. The mode 0x1c handler at
   img 0x1c8f writes sin(phase) * amp >> 0xb to offset 0x3f20 of
   g_WaterBufB. 0x3f20 bytes is word 8080, and 8080 is row 50 column
   80 of a 160 x 100 grid - DEAD CENTRE. g_EndOscAmp climbs every tick
   with no ceiling, so the drop builds.

   What drives it at sync 0x2d is the mode 0x12 handler - section 8.

3.  Water_RenderSpans - the sync 0x2d scene

   1000:2a1a. The field drawn as COLUMN SPANS, the same y-buffer
   technique Voxel_Render uses - so the water is seen from the side as
   a landscape [verified].

       DS = 0xa000        the screen, written directly
       ES = g_WaterBufB   the height field

   Per column, walking from the BOTTOM field row upward:

       img 2a33  DI starts at 0x7bc0, word 15840, field row 99
       img 2a38  EBP starts at 0xf8c0, 320*199, the bottom screen row
       img 2a4a  the shade is (next column's height - this one) >> 7
                 plus the row index, floored at 0 - A HORIZONTAL
                 GRADIENT, which is a lighting term, PLUS depth
       img 2a5c  BH = BL then BL += 3, so the word store lays two
                 ADJACENT palette entries and img 0x2a83 XCHGs them
                 each row: a dither between two shades
       img 2a65  the screen y is EBP - (height << 4)
       img 2a6f  fill upward from the previous y to this one
       img 2a8b  EBP -= 0x280 - TWO screen rows per field row

   Under Pal_SetRgbRamps' cold black-blue-cyan-white palette, with no
   fire band at all, that is water.

4.  Water_Refract - the finale

   1000:2a99. The work buffer displaced onto the screen through the
   field [verified].

       ES = 0xa000        the screen
       FS = g_WaterBufB   the height field
       DS = 0x5a79        g_WorkSeg - THE SOURCE, holding the THE END
                          letters over their plasma

       ax = height >> 3
       bx = di + ax + (ax << 6) + (ax << 8)   =  di + 321 * ax

   321 IS 320 + 1, so the displacement runs along the screen DIAGONAL
   - down and right together, in proportion to height. Refraction in
   three shifts and three adds, with no trigonometry and no per-axis
   split.

   One field cell drives a 2x2 block: words written at DI and
   DI + 0x140, DI stepping 2 per cell and another 0x140 added per row.

   IT READS ONE FIELD ROW PAST THE GRID. SI starts at word 160, field
   row 1, and walks 100 rows without resetting, so it ends on row 100
   of a grid with rows 0 to 99. In real mode that reads whatever
   follows the field in its segment. Harmless, and reproduced rather
   than corrected in the transcription.

   The only bounds handling anywhere is img 0x2ac7: an offset of
   0xfd00 or more is reduced by 0x600.

5.  What it looks like over time

   Transcribed and run with mode 0x1c's drive [verified by
   transcription]:

   +--------+---------+-----------+--------------+-------------+
   | ticks  | amp     | live cells| reach, cells | peak height |
   +========+=========+===========+==============+=============+
   |   300  |   300   |   269     |      9       |     258     |
   |   600  |   600   |   945     |     19       |     364     |
   |   900  |   900   |   389     |     12       |     383     |
   |  1200  |  1200   |   593     |     14       |    1110     |
   +--------+---------+-----------+--------------+-------------+

   THE AMPLITUDE GROWS WITHOUT BOUND WHILE THE REACH DOES NOT. The
   drive climbs forever but the damping holds the wave to roughly
   fifteen cells of the centre, so the effect is not a spreading ring
   - it is a small disc that becomes increasingly violent.

   A peak height of 1110 feeds (1110 >> 3) * 321, about 44000 bytes of
   displacement, which wraps the source image several times over. So
   the centre of the screen tears itself apart while the edges stay
   calm, and that is the demo's closing image.

6.  The transcription

   sim/xcursion/parts.py has water_step and water_refract, and
   sim/xcursion/demo.py part 20 is the finale: Mesh_Draw into a work
   buffer, Water_Step, Water_Refract to the screen, driven at the
   centre cell exactly as mode 0x1c drives it. Z resets it.

   src/xcursion/xcursion_water.c has Xcur_WaterStep and
   Xcur_WaterRenderSpans, with Xcur_PalSetRgbRamps in xcursion_pal.c
   and the part as scene 22 of xcursion_demo.c. ITS DRIVE IS THE
   DEMO'S: the sequencer keeps each channel's note age and sample step
   and mode 0x12 reads them, so nothing about the drips is invented.

   THE STEPS ARE EXACT. Read out of a running demo at order 0x2b, all
   eight channels' steps match src/'s to the dword - 0x229b8, 0xb92c,
   0x2e4bc, 0xc438, 0x12224, 0x57624, 0xc438, 0xa4f8 [observed] - so
   the period table, the octave shift, both divides and the final
   shift are all right, and with them every drip's column.

   water_render_spans is transcribed in sim/ too, and
   sim/xcursion/demo.py part 22 is the sync 0x2d scene.

   THE PALETTE AND THE DRIVE ARE STILL OURS IN sim/, and its window
   title says so: part 22 shows the palette index rather than
   Pal_SetRgbRamps' ramp, and pokes the centre cell the way mode 0x1c
   does - which is the FINALE's driver, not this part's.

   The y-buffer is the detail worth checking in the transcription: ESI
   is set once per column and only ever decreases, so each column
   fills bottom-up and nothing is overdrawn. Run against the finale's
   wave it fills all 64000 pixels with 104 distinct shades.

   The water field lists are padded by TWO rows. Both Water_Step and
   Water_Refract process field rows 1 to 100 of a 0 to 99 grid -
   img 0x29de counts 0x3e80 cells from word 160, and img 0x2aaa walks
   100 rows without resetting SI - and Water_Step's +0x140 neighbour
   read reaches a row beyond that again. Reproduced, not tidied.

7.  Open questions

   o Whether g_WaterDamping is ever changed from 4. It is a byte with
     no writer found.

   o Water_Run waits a tick per render at img 0x29c5, so the solver
     advances once per render and the drips once per animation pass -
     the same rate question the rotozoom and the beams have. Unmeasured
     here, because the scene has no second counter to measure it
     against.

8.  The drips - the mode 0x12 handler

   img 0x166f, and it IS the sync 0x2d scene's driver [verified].

   THE EIGHT SLOTS ARE THE EIGHT MUSIC CHANNELS. That is the whole
   point of the handler and it is easy to read as a drop table
   instead. cs:0x9388 is a per-channel counter that the SEQUENCER'S
   NOTE DECODER arms to 0x10 at img 0x0d26, and nothing else in the
   image reads or writes it - mode 0x12 is its only consumer. And
   cs:0x93c8 is that channel's SAMPLE STEP, written by the sample
   setup at img 0x22cf.

   SO THE MUSIC IS THE ONLY THING DRIVING THE WATER. Every note arms
   its channel's drip for sixteen ticks, each channel owns one row,
   and THE NOTE'S PITCH SETS THE COLUMN because the column comes off
   the step. Nothing here is on a timer and nothing is random.

   THE TIMING AGREES. Mode 0x12's parts are sync 0x2b and sync 0x2d,
   and sync 0x2d's runner is Water_Run - so the handler runs exactly
   while the water does, and in no other part.

   AND SO DOES THE SEGMENT. img 0x1677 loads DS from g_WaterBufA,
   which is the buffer Water_Step is about to WRITE - so a drop lands
   in the frame being computed rather than the one being read.

   Per channel, four bytes apart:

       if g_DropLife[n] == 0: skip                      img 167c
       g_DropLife[n]--                                  img 1684
       di = (g_DropPos[n] >> 9) & 0xfffe                img 1689
       word [0x2d00 + n*0xa00 + di - 0xa0] = 0xfc00     img 1696

   ON THE 160x100 WORD GRID THE BASE IS ROW 35 + 8n, COLUMN 80 -
   eight rows evenly eight apart straight down the CENTRE COLUMN
   [verified]:

       channel  0    1    2    3    4    5    6    7
       row     35   43   51   59   67   75   83   91

   BUT THE STEP CAN CARRY A DRIP OFF ITS OWN ROW. The offset is only
   masked to 16 bits, so a column displacement past 160 words lands in
   a later row instead of being clamped. Read out of a running demo,
   row 35 held two live ripples at columns 57 and 106 and none at 80
   [observed]. Clamping the column to its row is the tempting fix and
   it is not what the demo does.

   0xFC00 AS A SIGNED WORD IS -1024 - A DENT, NOT A BUMP. A falling
   drop pushes the surface down, and getting the sign wrong would give
   eight rising columns instead of eight impacts.

   SHR EDI, 9 IS AN UNSIGNED 32-BIT SHIFT, img 0x168f, and img 0x1693
   then keeps DI's low 16 bits - so a negative position shifts as a
   large unsigned and WRAPS the column. Reproduced, not corrected.

   The counter is set to 0x10 at img 0x0d26, so a drop pokes for
   sixteen ticks and stops. img 0x225f is the sample setup that fills
   the step, and the note decoder calls it at img 0x0d2d immediately
   after arming the counter - so the two always move together.

   The step is the standard S3M rate, img 0x228e:

       period = the table at img 0x97e1, indexed by note & 0xf
       t      = (period * 0x20ab0) >> (note >> 4)     unsigned
       q      = t / the instrument's c2speed at +0x20
       step   = (0xda7600 / q) << 2
