PCDEMOS 64k                                            Analysis Note


                      EXCURSION: THE BEAMS


Abstract

   Beams_Render at 1000:317a and its runner Beams_Run at 1000:260f -
   parts sync 0x28 and sync 0x29.

   Rotating parallel stripes of light, produced by FEEDBACK rather
   than by drawing: the screen is its own state, and each pixel is
   pushed toward bright or dark depending on two bits of a plane
   equation.

   IT IS NOT A ROTOZOOM, which is what the opening lines look like.
   There is no texture and no source image anywhere in the routine.

1.  The misread worth recording

   Beams_Render opens with exactly the same four operations
   Roto_Render does:

       ECX = g_RotoZoom
       BX  = g_RotoAngle & 0x7fc
       bp  = (sin * zoom) >> 8
       si  = (cos * zoom) >> 8

   and it uses the same two globals, which the same animation handlers
   drive. On that evidence it reads as a second rotozoom.

   IT IS NOT ONE. Roto_Render sets ES to the 256x256 texture at
   segment 0x2a79 and samples it; Beams_Render sets DS to 0xA000 and
   ES to the work buffer and never reads a texture at all. The sin and
   cos pair is being used as a ROTATED GRADIENT, not as a sampling
   step.

   The giveaway is which segments a routine loads, not which maths it
   starts with.

2.  The plane equation

   bp becomes the per-column step and si the per-row step of one
   16-bit accumulator [verified]:

       img 31b3   the start is -(160*bp + 100*si), built from shifts:
                  si*4 + si*32 + si*64 is 100*si, and bp*32 + bp*128
                  is 160*bp
       img 31d3   per row,    dx += si  then bx = dx
       img 31ee   per column, bx += bp

   which is

       bx = (x - 160) * bp + (y - 100) * si     mod 0x10000

   SO bx IS ZERO AT THE SCREEN CENTRE, and the 16-bit wrap is what
   turns a single plane into a repeating pattern.

   The period in pixels is 0x10000 / step, and with g_RotoZoom between
   0x40 and 0x100 the step runs to 0xfff, so [verified by
   transcription]:

       +-------------+------------------+
       | g_RotoZoom  | stripe period    |
       +=============+==================+
       | 0x40        | 64 px            |
       | 0xa0        | 28 px            |
       | 0x100       | 16 px            |
       +-------------+------------------+

3.  The two-bit test

       img 31da   TEST BX, 0xC000

   Only the top two bits are looked at. They are clear for exactly one
   quarter of a 16-bit range, so A QUARTER OF EACH STRIPE PERIOD IS
   THE BRIGHT BAND and three quarters are ramping down. Measured on
   the transcription: 25 per cent of pixels in the bright band, with
   another five or so per cent still above half brightness in the
   trail behind it.

   Testing two bits rather than comparing against a constant is why
   the duty cycle is a quarter and not something tunable. It is not a
   parameter anywhere.

4.  Feedback, and the fixed point

       bright, img 31f9:   pixel = (pixel + 0x3f) >> 1
       dark,   img 31e0:   pixel -= 4, and values below 4 are left
                           alone

   The bright rule converges exponentially; the dark rule is linear.
   So a stripe lights up fast and decays slowly, which is what leaves
   a trail behind the sweep.

   (x + 0x3f) >> 1 HAS ITS FIXED POINT AT 62, NOT 63: (62 + 63) >> 1
   is 62. The bright band therefore saturates one short of the band
   top, exactly the way Pal_BuildMain's ramps stop one short of the
   DAC maximum - see subsystems/palette.txt section 2.

   Every write goes to BOTH DS, which is 0xA000, and ES, which is the
   work buffer, img 0x31e9 and 0x31eb. The work buffer is the state
   and the screen is a copy, WHICH IS WHY Beams_Run HAS NO
   Vga_Present. It is the only renderer in the demo that writes the
   framebuffer and keeps its own history there.

5.  The register trick

   img 0x31d1 sets AH = 0xc8 as the ROW COUNTER and the loop body uses
   AL as the pixel. Nothing in the body touches AH, and

       img 31fe   SHR AL, 1

   is a byte shift precisely so the row counter above it survives.
   Writing `shr ax, 1` would destroy the loop. The same applies to
   `add al, 0x3f` at img 0x31fc, which cannot carry into AH because AL
   is at most 0x3f going in.

6.  The two parts

   The script changes the sweep and the three palette phases, and
   NOTHING CLEARS THE WORK BUFFER BETWEEN THEM [verified] - img 0x083e
   clears all 64 KB of it before the first part and the second part
   has no clear of its own, so sync 0x29 starts from sync 0x28's
   trails:

       img 081c   phases 0x400, 0x400, 0x400
       img 0855   g_RotoAngleRate = +0x20     then sync 0x28
       img 086c   phases 0xff80, 0xf980, 0xf680
       img 0881   g_RotoAngleRate = -0x20     then sync 0x29

   SO THE BEAMS SWEEP ONE WAY AND THEN BACK. A full turn is
   0x800 / 0x20 = 64 ticks, 0.64 s at g_Speed 100 - fast.

   Meanwhile the mode 0xf handler at img 0x15c4 bounces g_RotoZoom
   between 0x40 and 0x100 by flipping g_RotoZoomVel to +4 below the
   floor and -4 above the ceiling - a triangle wave, so the stripe
   spacing breathes from 16 px to 64 px and back while they turn. See
   subsystems/animation.txt.

   img 0x084b zeroes g_RotoZoom before sync 0x28, so the first stripes
   are infinitely wide and narrow in as the velocity ramps up.

7.  The transcription

   src/xcursion/xcursion_beams.c is Xcur_BeamsRender, with
   Xcur_PalBuildRgbRamps in xcursion_pal.c and the two parts as scenes
   18 and 19 of xcursion_demo.c.

   THE PLANE IS EXACT. Checked against a paused demo 56 animation
   ticks into the first part: the start value comes out 0x394 both
   ways, the row accumulator 0xdea7 at row 168, and the plane 0x7c65
   at column 218 of it [observed]. Mode 0xf agrees too - angle 0x700
   and zoom 220 at that tick, the zoom being 4 * 55 with the velocity
   starting from its image static of 0.

   THE SCREEN DOES NOT FOLLOW FROM THAT, because it is feedback: every
   pixel is a function of the whole history since the clear, so it
   depends on HOW MANY RENDERS happened and at which angle. Beams_Run
   waits a tick per render exactly as Roto_Run does, which would be
   two renders an animation pass if a pass kept up - but a pass here
   is 64000 pixels against the rotozoom's 16000, and it does not.
   Measured: g_Speed 100, so 50 animation passes a second, against
   about five video frames per render pass or 14 a second - ABOUT ONE
   RENDER PER THREE AND A HALF PASSES, and that fraction is machine
   speed rather than anything in the code.

   src/ renders once a frame, which is one per two passes - the
   nearest ratio its tick structure holds, and nothing is tuned to
   close the rest of the gap. The visible consequence is trail length:
   fewer renders per turn makes a longer, fainter trail behind each
   stripe.

   sim/xcursion/parts.py has beams_render and sim/xcursion/demo.py
   part 18 is the effect with mode 0xf driving it. V reverses the
   sweep, which is the only difference between the two parts.

   THE PALETTE IS THE DEMO'S. Pal_BuildRgbRamps at 1000:3d1e, which
   mode 0xf calls every tick, rebuilds indices 0 to 63 as three linear
   ramps whose slopes are cosines of three phases advancing at 3, 7
   and 9. pal_build_rgb_ramps transcribes it, so the beams drift
   through colour exactly as the binary says and nothing here is a
   stand-in.

8.  The palette

   Pal_BuildRgbRamps at 1000:3d1e writes the 64 entries the beams
   paint, from g_PaletteBase at cs:0xc7a4 - exactly the 0 to 62 range
   section 3 describes.

   Each channel is a ramp whose slope is (cos(phase) + 0x1000) >> 5,
   and the three phases advance at 3, 7 and 9 a tick, so the hue
   combination never repeats quickly.

   ONLY THE HIGH BYTE of each 16-bit accumulator reaches the palette,
   img 0x3d78 - the same 8.8 trick as Glow_SpawnStep.

9.  Open questions

   o Nothing in src/ closes the render-rate gap in section 7, and
     nothing should without a reason that is in the binary. If the
     trails ever read as too short, that ratio is the first thing to
     look at.
