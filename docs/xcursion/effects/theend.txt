PCDEMOS 64k                                            Analysis Note


                    EXCURSION: THE END SEQUENCE


Abstract

   The last three animation handlers - modes 0x1a, 0x1b and 0x1c at
   img 0x1c23, 0x1c67 and 0x1c8f - which run the demo out from the
   credits to the final fade.

   All three carry THE SAME THREE-INSTRUCTION LIGHT BLOCK, verbatim,
   and it is the only place in the whole demo that animates the light.
   Everywhere else g_LightPitch, g_LightYaw and g_LightRoll are set
   once by the script and left.

1.  Mode 0x1a - the THE END letters

   Its part is sync 0x4b, whose runner is TheEnd_Run at 1000:25c7 and
   whose model is 0x5c47 - 120 verts and 94 faces, the largest in the
   image [verified]:

       g_LightPitch += 4      g_ObjPitch += 8
       g_LightYaw   += 8      g_ObjYaw   += 6
       g_LightRoll  += 0xc    g_ObjRoll  += 4
       g_ObjTransZ -= 0x100
       if g_FadeLevel != 0: g_FadeLevel += 0x30, Pal_Fade

   TWO INDEPENDENT ROTATIONS AT DIFFERENT RATES. The letters turn at
   8/6/4 and the light at 4/8/0xc, so the highlight sweeps across them
   rather than sitting still - and because the rates share no common
   factor with each other the pattern does not repeat quickly.

   g_ObjTransZ falls 0x100 a tick, so the letters approach the camera
   for the whole part - from 0x1e000, NOT the 0x20000 img 0x52a4 sets.
   Mesh_FaceNormals at img 0x52de overwrites it three instructions
   later and never puts it back, so the tumble ends at 0x6100 and the
   next part's 0x6000 is no jump in size. formats/mesh.txt section 5.

   The fade here is INWARD: the level climbs while it is non-zero, so
   the part fades up from whatever the credits left and then holds.

2.  Mode 0x1b - the plasma, and the white flash

   Its part is sync 0x4c, img 0x5314, and the cut into it is the one
   place in the end sequence where the picture jumps:

       g_FadeLevel = 0x4000             img 0x5314 - FULL WHITE
       Pal_Fade
       g_AnimMode = 0x1b
       g_ObjTransZ = 0x6000             img 0x5324
       g_ObjPitch = g_ObjYaw = g_ObjRoll = 0        img 0x532e
       Vga_ClearScreen

   THE LETTERS ARE SNAPPED SQUARE, NOT SLOWED INTO PLACE. There is no
   counter, no deceleration and no test on the angles anywhere: three
   word stores zero all of them outright, and the white flash set one
   instruction earlier is what hides the jump. The same store pulls
   g_ObjTransZ back to 0x6000, nearer than the walk-in of mode 0x1a
   ever brought it, so the letters also grow at the cut [verified].

   The handler is six instructions:

       the same light block, 4 / 8 / 0xc
       if g_FadeLevel != 0: g_FadeLevel -= 0x40, Pal_Fade

   A fade OUT at 0x40 a tick, and nothing else. 0x4000 at 0x40 a tick
   is 256 ticks, which is most of the part, so the whiteout bleeds
   away across it rather than snapping back. The light keeps tumbling
   either way.

   The background is built once, at entry, and never again:

       Plasma_Generate into 0x6a79, rough 1, bias 0x2000, mask 0x3fff
       img 0x5367 - 204 rows of 320, al = (plasma >> 3) + 0xe0

   The plasma is read at its own 256-byte row pitch and written at
   the screen's 320 - that is all the `sub si, 0x40` at img 0x5384
   is. The shift leaves 0 to 31 and the add lands them in 0xe0 to
   0xff, which is the dark red ramp img 0x3f9b put at 224. 204 ROWS,
   NOT 200, so the fill runs 1280 bytes past the picture and into the
   rest of the segment; section 4 is what reads that slack back out.

   Its runner is TheEnd_Run's SECOND ENTRY POINT, img 0x25e2 - the
   same Mesh_Draw / Vga_Present / Demo_WaitTick loop as sync 0x4b
   without the Buf_Clear at the top. So the plasma survives from
   frame to frame and the letters are redrawn over the same pixels
   every time. They can be, because nothing moves them any more: only
   the light moves, so each frame re-shades them in place.

3.  Mode 0x1c - the driven oscillator

   The last part, sync 0x52, after which Demo_Timeline jumps to
   Demo_Shutdown [verified]. Its entry is img 0x5394: both water
   buffers cleared, g_WaterBufA = 0x6a79, g_WaterBufB = 0x7a79, the
   damping at img 0xa59f raised from 4 to 6, and NOTHING REBUILDS THE
   PICTURE - it is whatever sync 0x4c left in the work buffer, plasma
   and letters both, and from here it is only ever displaced.

       g_EndOscPhase += 0x20
       g_EndOscAmp++
       bx = g_EndOscPhase & 0x7fc
       eax = (g_SinTable[bx] * g_EndOscAmp) >> 0xb
       ds = g_WaterBufB
       word [0x3f20] = ax
       the same light block, 4 / 8 / 0xc
       if g_MusicPos >= 0x50: g_FadeLevel -= 0x18, Pal_Fade

   ONE WORD, AT ONE FIXED OFFSET, WITH A RAMPING AMPLITUDE. That is
   the whole of it. The grid is 160 by 100 signed words, so 0x3f20 is
   word 8080 - row 50, column 80, the exact centre of the field.

   IT DRIVES THE BUFFER THE SOLVER READS, not the one it writes - the
   opposite of the dripping scene's drops at img 0x1696. By the time
   the handler runs, Water_Step has already swapped, so g_WaterBufB
   is the frame just computed and the poke lands in the neighbours
   the next step will average.

   g_EndOscAmp is incremented every tick with no ceiling and
   multiplies the sine, so the disturbance grows for as long as the
   part runs. A single driven point whose swing increases.

   The phase advances 0x20 a tick, so a full turn is 0x800 / 0x20 = 64
   ticks, 0.64 s at g_Speed 100.

   The wave spreads far more slowly than the solver's one cell a step:
   integer truncation kills the wavefront, so at 73 steps the
   disturbance is 14 cells wide and at 350 it has reached the screen
   edges [verified, against a live run at matched g_EndOscAmp].

   ITS LOOK IS MACHINE SPEED, and this is the part of the demo where
   that shows most. The drive turns once per 64 TICKS and the solver
   steps once per FRAME, so the drive's period measured in SOLVER
   STEPS is 64 times the frames-per-tick the machine achieves - and
   that period is what decides both the ripple's wavelength and how
   far it carries.

   IT IS NOT A GENTLE DEPENDENCE. A point source driven slowly
   relative to its medium barely radiates - it bulges the field
   around itself and the energy damps where it stands - while a
   faster one launches travelling rings. Between 45 steps a cycle and
   31 the far field does not change by a half, it changes by an order
   of magnitude. Measured on row 49 at g_EndOscAmp 813, at the
   screen's left edge 80 cells from the drive:

       the demo             25       [observed]
       at 48 frames/tick   -43       rings, matching amplitude
       at 70 frames/tick     0       dead - the corners never move

   THE REFERENCE RUN HOLDS ABOUT 48 FRAMES A SECOND THROUGH THIS PART
   against the 100 Hz timer - 13 solver steps in 19 emulated video
   frames [observed] - because the frame draws a mesh, solves 16000
   cells and refracts 64000 pixels. The light parts of the same run
   hold the full 100. So the port gives this part its own frame rate,
   XCUR_END_WATER_HZ, rather than the XCUR_FRAME_HZ the rest uses:
   see subsystems/animation.txt section 1.1.

   WHAT A PERIOD MACHINE MANAGED IS NOT RECORDED ANYWHERE. 100 is
   what the timer asks for and what no measured run of this part
   reaches; 48 is what one does.

4.  Water_Refract - the picture displaced

   img 0x2a99, the last renderer in the demo, driven by TheEnd_RunWater
   at 1000:2358: Mesh_Draw, Water_Step, this, Demo_WaitTick. THERE IS
   NO Vga_Present in that loop, because this writes 0xa000 itself and
   reads the work buffer as its source. Those two are the same buffer
   nowhere else in the end sequence.

   Per field cell, img 0x2ab3:

       ax = height >> 3
       bx = di + ax + (ax << 6) + (ax << 8)   =   di + 321 * ax

   321 IS 320 + 1, so the sample moves one row down and one pixel
   right per unit of height: the displacement runs along the screen
   diagonal, and a ripple shears the picture rather than stretching
   it in one axis. Three shifts and three adds, no trigonometry.

   img 0x2ac7 subtracts 0x600 when bx is 0xfd00 or above, and
   0x10000 - 0xfa00 IS 0x600 - SO IT IS A MODULAR WRAP BY THE PICTURE
   SIZE, not a clamp. A sample that ran off the top comes back in at
   the bottom. 0xfa00 to 0xfcff is left alone and reads past the
   picture into the segment's slack, which is why section 2's fill
   runs 204 rows.

   One cell covers a 2x2 screen block, and the lower row comes from
   its own read 0x140 further on rather than from doubling the upper
   one - so the block keeps the source's vertical detail.

5.  The light, and why it matters

   g_LightPitch, g_LightYaw and g_LightRoll are written in exactly
   seven places in the image [verified]: four script sites in
   Demo_Entry, one in Demo_Timeline, and the three identical
   `add 4 / add 8 / add 0xc` blocks in these handlers. Mesh_Draw reads
   them at img 0x47c4, 0x47ce and 0x47d8.

   So for the whole demo up to sync 0x4b the light is a constant that
   the script resets per part. Only in the end sequence does it move.

   THAT IS WORTH KNOWING BEFORE "FIXING" ANY LIGHTING. The light
   sweeping over an object is sometimes an artifact of Mesh_Draw
   rotating the normal twice - see formats/mesh.txt - and sometimes,
   here, exactly what the demo asked for. The two cases look similar
   on screen and have different causes.

6.  The transcription

   src/xcursion/xcursion_demo.c has anim_tick26 and scene_theend for
   mode 0x1a - the two rotations, the z walk and the fade climb, with
   TheEnd_Run's clear-draw-present-wait around them. The part is
   img 0x5290: the letters start at z 0x20000 with the camera angle
   zeroed, the sky bias at 0x1c and the y offset at 0x2300.

   ITS PALETTE IS BUILT IN THE PREVIOUS PART'S TAIL, img 0x5287,
   after Scroller_Run returns - nothing in this part builds one. The
   fade the letters rise out of is the negative level mode 0x19 drove
   past zero.

   THAT PALETTE IS CYCLIC WITH A PERIOD OF SIXTEEN [verified]. img
   0x3f1e writes every entry FOUR TIMES - at DI and at DI+0x30, +0x60
   and +0x90, sixteen entries apart - so 1 to 64 are four copies of
   one ramp:

       1 ..  8   (0, g, 0x3f)  g rising  0 to 49 by 7   blue -> cyan
       9 .. 16   (0, g, 0x3f)  g falling 56 to 7 by 7   cyan -> blue

   The letters' shades land in 8 to 50, so they cross that cycle
   three times and BAND blue, cyan, blue as they turn. A single
   64-entry ramp gives one smooth wash and no banding, which is what
   a transcription that misses this looks like.

   img 0x3f9b then puts a dark red ramp at 224 and leaves 65 to 223
   as the previous palette had them.

   anim_tick27 / scene_endplasma carry mode 0x1b and img 0x25e2, with
   end_background for the 204-row fill; anim_tick28 / scene_endwater
   carry mode 0x1c and img 0x2358, and Xcur_WaterRefract in
   xcursion_water.c is img 0x2a99.

   THE WORK BUFFER IS A SEPARATE 64 KB ARRAY in those two parts, where
   the rest of the port draws straight into the framebuffer. It has to
   be: section 4 reads the one while writing the other, and it reads
   past 64000.
