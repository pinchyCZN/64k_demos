PCDEMOS 64k                                            Analysis Note


              EXCURSION: THE BATTLE, THE SHOTS, THE FLAG


Abstract

   Battle_Run at 1000:264b and the four routines it drives: two craft
   with independent state, eight 3D projectiles, and a blur-and-decay
   glow in the fire ramp.

   The projectiles answer the laser question: THEY ARE MESHES, out of
   an eight-slot table, with the weapon model chosen by the music
   position, and INSTRUMENT 9 IN THE MODULE PULLS THE TRIGGER -
   section 2.1. The flag answers the other: it is MODEL 0x5be7, a pole
   and a banner, drawn in Car_Run, and ITS GEOMETRY IS STATIC - it
   does not wave.

   Six models exist in the image, not four. Section 5 lists them all,
   and section 8 has the three flight models that move the two craft.

1.  Battle_Run

   Three parts call it - img 0x05c5 at sync 0x19, img 0x064e at sync
   0x1b, img 0x06f2 at sync 0x21 [verified]. Per frame:

       img 264b    clear g_WorkSeg, 0x3e80 dwords
       img 265b    Points_Render - the stars
                   ... the scene, order below ...
       img 268e    Vga_Present
       img 2691    Input_Poll
                   loop until g_MusicPos reaches the target

   IT DOES NOT WAIT FOR A TICK. img 0x2691 is Input_Poll at
   1000:0ace, not Demo_WaitTick at 1000:0abc, so this part renders as
   fast as the machine allows while the flight models stay on the
   timer. The moire does wait; this does not.

   THE DRAW ORDER FLIPS ON g_AnimMode, img 0x265e, and the glow moves
   with it [verified]:

   +---------------------------+---------------------------+
   | mode 9 and 0xa            | mode 0xb                  |
   +===========================+===========================+
   | Battle_DrawCraftA         | Battle_DrawCraftB         |
   | Glow_Render BX=0x2d60,    | Glow_Render BX=0x9c,      |
   |   only once g_MusicPos    |   unconditionally         |
   |   reaches 0x1a            |                           |
   | Shots_Draw                | Shots_Draw                |
   | Battle_DrawCraftB         | Battle_DrawCraftA         |
   +---------------------------+---------------------------+

   So in mode 0xb craft A is drawn last and is therefore in front, and
   the glow box moves from x=96 y=36 to x=156 y=0. BX is a flat offset
   into the 320-wide buffer, not a coordinate pair.

   THE ORDER FLIP IS A PAINTER'S-ALGORITHM DECISION THE FLIGHT MODEL
   FORCES, not a style choice. In modes 9 and 0xa craft B chases A from
   behind, so A is farther and goes down first; in mode 0xb B is placed
   one forward vector AHEAD of A, so B is farther and goes down first.
   Section 8.

   The two craft have SEPARATE STATE BLOCKS AND SEPARATE VERTEX
   SEGMENTS, which is what lets both be on screen at once [verified]:

   +---------------------+---------+----------+--------------+---------+
   | Routine             | Model   | Verts to | Position     | Angles  |
   +=====================+=========+==========+==============+=========+
   | Battle_DrawCraftA   | 0x585f  | 0x3a79   | g_Obj1X/Y/Z  | g_Obj1* |
   | 1000:26a0           | 34v 45f |          |              |         |
   +---------------------+---------+----------+--------------+---------+
   | Battle_DrawCraftB   | 0x572f  | 0x6a79   | g_Obj2X/Y/Z  | g_Obj2* |
   | 1000:26e8           | 17v 25f |          |              |         |
   +---------------------+---------+----------+--------------+---------+

   Craft B CARRIES THE ONLY TRUE CULL IN THE OBJECT PIPELINE: img
   0x2701 returns without drawing when its z is negative. Everywhere
   else Mesh_TransformVerts merely clamps a vertex to the near plane,
   so a craft behind the camera would smear rather than vanish.

   Battle_RunNoShots at 1000:2efa is the same scene with no Shots_Draw
   and one craft - sync 0x23 and 0x25.

   g_Obj2Pitch, g_Obj2Yaw and g_Obj2Roll are SHARED with the car - the
   mode 0x18 handler steers the car through the same triple. See
   effects/car.txt section 6.

   Segment 0x6a79 is shared with Planet_BuildMap's sphere table. That
   is why Planet_BuildMap is called twice, img 0x02d2 and img 0x4e34,
   instead of once at startup: craft B's transform destroys the table.
   See effects/planet.txt.

1.1.  The part chain, from sync 0x15

   Six parts between the moire and the aftermath [verified]. The
   first two are not Battle_Run at all - they are Planet_Render with
   the ball suppressed, one craft against the stars:

   +-------+------+------+--------------------+------------------------+
   | At img| Mode | Sync | Runner             | What it is             |
   +=======+======+======+====================+========================+
   | 045d  |  8   | 0x15 | Planet_Render      | craft A, opening WHITE |
   | 04e0  |  8   | 0x16 | Planet_Render      | cut to craft B, far    |
   |       |      |      |                    | out to one side        |
   | 0548  |  9   | 0x19 | Battle_Run         | the approach           |
   | 05cb  | 0xa  | 0x1b | Battle_Run         | the Lissajous pass     |
   | 0654  | 0xb  | 0x21 | Battle_Run         | locked formation, and  |
   |       |      |      |                    | A does the shooting    |
   | 06f5  | 0xc  | 0x23 | Battle_RunNoShots  | the aftermath          |
   | 0750  | 0xd  | 0x25 | Battle_RunNoShots  | THE WARP OUT - 1.2     |
   +-------+------+------+--------------------+------------------------+

   EACH PART ENDS ON ITS OWN TARGET and they are not evenly spaced -
   0x15, 0x16, 0x19, 0x1b, 0x21, 0x23. Reading the sync column one row
   down puts modes 0xa and 0xb a whole pattern late, which leaves the
   approach running through the pass and the pass through the
   formation.

   Battle_RunNoShots at img 0x2efa is Battle_Run with Shots_Draw gone,
   one craft out of the shared object slot, and THE GLOW DRAWN
   UNCONDITIONALLY rather than gated on the order.

   Verified against the running demo [observed]: at order 0x1b and
   again at 0x1c, all six of craft A's and craft B's coordinates match
   src/ byte for byte - mode 0xb recomputes both from the forward
   vector every tick, so nothing accumulates. At order 0x1a, where
   mode 0xa integrates instead, A matches exactly and B's Lissajous
   pair is within three ticks of phase.

   THE FIRST TWO OPEN AT THE FADE'S CEILING. img 0x04c9 and img 0x04e0
   set g_FadeLevel to 0x3d80, a bias of 0x3d on every DAC component,
   so the screen is white; mode 8 then walks it down by 0x60 a tick.
   The moire before them ends by washing out to white at order 0x13 -
   effects/moire.txt section 1.1 - so the two meet with no cut.

   img 0x0474 PARSES CRAFT B'S MODEL and img 0x0481 craft A's. That is
   the only place either is read.

   Only the last two Battle_Run parts clear anything: img 0x05d5 and
   img 0x0676 call Shots_ClearGlowField, img 0x05d8 and img 0x0654
   call Shots_Clear. The sync-0x19 part clears neither, which is why
   its first glow frames blur the colour map's leftovers - section 3.

1.2.  The warp out

   img 0x0750, mode 0xd at img 0x147e, and it ends the scene
   [verified].

   THE STRETCH IS g_MeshZScale AND NOTHING ELSE. Mesh_TransformVerts
   scales every vertex's z by it at img 0x498f before the rotate, so a
   growing scale draws the craft longer and longer along its own axis.
   The warp streak IS the model, not a trail behind it. 0x100 is the
   neutral value - it matches the << 3 that x and y get - and the part
   sets exactly that on entry.

   AND THE SAME SCALE IS THE SPEED. img 0x1497 rotates
   (0, 0, -(zscale << 4)) and the result drives both the star camera
   at img 0x14b3 and the craft at img 0x1507, so the stars streak for
   the same reason the ship stretches. One variable does both.

   The growth COMPOUNDS, img 0x1521: plus 4, then plus a sixteenth of
   the new value, every tick once the row counter passes 0x20. Before
   that it decays by a thirty-second. Past z 0x20000 a third rule
   damps it by an eighth.

   Then the ending, img 0x1573:

       g_ObjTransZ = g_ObjTransX = 0xfffd8000
       g_SparkLife = 0x80 ; g_SparkShift = 8
       g_FadeLevel = 0x4000 and Pal_Fade
       Glow_Burst

   0xfffd8000 IS A PARKED SENTINEL, NOT A POSITION. img 0x14ed tests
   it and skips the entire motion block, so once the craft warps it
   stays gone. Reading it as somewhere the ship flew to makes the
   ending look like a craft falling off the far plane instead of
   vanishing.

   What is left is the burst, and it is BLUE because the part takes
   its own palette: Pal_BuildWarp at 1000:3c1d, seven bands with
   Glow_Render's 0x60..0xbf window landing on black-blue-cyan-white
   where Pal_BuildMain puts a fire ramp. Same renderer, different
   colour.

   THE BURST ALSO CHANGES CHARACTER PERMANENTLY. g_SparkLife and
   g_SparkShift are 0x20 and 6 in the image and this is the only place
   that writes them - to 0x80 and 8, a longer-lived and slower burst -
   and nothing puts them back.

2.  The shots

   Shots_Draw at 1000:27ad [verified]. Eight slots, each a full 3D
   object through Mesh_Draw.

   THE SLOT TABLE IS AT SEGMENT 0x4a79 OFFSET 0x1770, 8 slots of 0x12
   bytes, img 0x27d0 to 0x2815:

       +0     dword   x       -> g_ObjTransX
       +4     dword   y       -> g_ObjTransY
       +8     dword   z       -> g_ObjTransZ
       +0xc   word    pitch   -> g_ObjPitch
       +0xe   word    yaw     -> g_ObjYaw
       +0x10  word    roll    -> g_ObjRoll

   A SLOT WITH X == 0 IS INACTIVE, img 0x27d3, tested as a dword. The
   free-slot marker is a position, not a flag byte, so a shot can
   never sit exactly on the world x origin.

   The table ends at 0x1800, which is exactly where
   Shots_ClearGlowField begins. The boundary is deliberate.

   THE WEAPON CHANGES WITH THE MUSIC, img 0x27b4 [verified]:

   +------------------+--------+-------------------------------------+
   | g_MusicPos       | Model  | What it is                          |
   +==================+========+=====================================+
   | below 0x1c       | 0x5b81 | 6 verts, 8 faces. A double cone     |
   |                  |        | 47 long and 4 across - a bolt.      |
   |                  |        | EVERY FACE ATTRIBUTE 0x60, so it    |
   |                  |        | is drawn in the fire ramp           |
   +------------------+--------+-------------------------------------+
   | 0x1c and above   | 0x5a8a | 14 verts, 20 faces. A pointed nose  |
   |                  |        | at z=-34, a body, swept wings at    |
   |                  |        | x +/-8 and tail fins. Attributes    |
   |                  |        | 0xc0 (greyscale) and 0x80 (red to   |
   |                  |        | yellow) - a solid-bodied weapon     |
   +------------------+--------+-------------------------------------+

2.1.  Where they come from

   THE TAILS SPAWN THEM AND THE MUSIC TRIGGERS THEM [verified].

   The sequencer's row decoder raises two flags off instrument
   numbers, img 0x0d03 and img 0x0d0e:

       instrument 0x10 -> g_FlashFlag  (img 0xa88d), the plasma's
                          white pulse - effects/warp.txt section 3
       instrument 0x09 -> g_FireFlag   (img 0xa88c), THE GUN

   So the shooting is on the beat, by the same mechanism as the
   plasma's flash and not by a timer.

   WHICH CRAFT FIRES DEPENDS ON THE PART. The modes 9 and 0xa tail at
   img 0x1dbe spawns from g_Obj2 - CRAFT B - and flashes the screen by
   0x600. The mode 0xb tail at img 0x1e3f spawns from g_Obj1 - CRAFT A
   - with ITS ROLL FORCED TO ZERO, img 0x1e70, and flashes by 0x300.

   Either way the slot is a round robin of eight through g_ShotNext at
   img 0x9923, and the shot carries the firing craft's own position
   and attitude, so it leaves along the nose.

   AND THE TRIGGER CHANGES AT ORDER 0x1c, img 0x1e25. At or above it
   the mode 0xb tail reads g_NewPatternFlag at img 0xa88e instead,
   which Music_NextPattern raises once per pattern at img 0x2234 - so
   A's rate drops from one a note to one a pattern and the shooting
   thins out rather than stopping. Both flags are cleared on a spawn,
   so a note left pending across the switchover cannot fire twice.

2.2.  How they fly, and the hit test

   img 0x1e9b, run by both tails every tick [verified]:

       rotate (0, 0, -0x20) by the slot's own angles and add it
       UNSHIFTED - Vec_Rotate3 returns x64, so the step is 64 times
       what the literal suggests
       roll += 0xb                             img 0x1ed9, a spin
       kill if x or y leaves +/-0xa0000
       kill if z > 0x2e000

   THE Z BOUND IS UNSIGNED - img 0x1f12 is `ja` - so a shot whose z
   goes negative is killed by that same test instead of flying on
   behind the camera. The x and y bounds are signed.

   IN MODE 0xb ONLY there is a hit test, img 0x1f14 onward: a box
   0x1000 on a side centred on craft B, lo = B - 0x800 and hi = lo +
   0x1000. It is AXIS-ALIGNED IN WORLD SPACE and ignores B's
   attitude - a box around a point and nothing more. A hit calls
   Glow_Burst, adds 0x600 to the fade and kills the shot; img 0x1f85
   is both that kill and the out-of-bounds one.

   So the only part where a shot can actually strike is the one where
   craft A is shooting.

2.3.  The fade, which is the flash

   THE SCREEN PULSES BECAUSE THE SHOT STEP BLEEDS THE FADE OFF, img
   0x1f94, and that is the last thing both tails do [verified]:

       if (signed) g_FadeLevel <= 0:  done
       g_FadeLevel -= 0x60, then Pal_Fade

   Every spawn throws the level UP - 0x600 from the modes 9/0xa tail,
   0x300 from mode 0xb's, another 0x600 on a hit - and this takes it
   back down 0x60 a tick. So a shot is a bright flash that falls off
   over about ten ticks, and between shots the screen sits near its
   own level.

   WITHOUT IT THE LEVEL ONLY EVER CLIMBS. The screen then settles at a
   pale grey and never pulses, and the glow's box reads as a coloured
   square parked on screen instead of a muzzle flash - which is the
   whole visual signature of the scene lost to one missing
   subtraction.

   The test is JLE on a WORD, so it stops at zero from either side.
   Mode 8's own copy at img 0x124e is JZ and stops only on exactly
   zero, and mode 0xc's at img 0x1468 is JZ with a step of 0x30.

   Measured against the running demo at four points [observed]:

   +-------+-------+------------+------------+
   | Order | Mode  | g_FadeLevel| src/       |
   +=======+=======+============+============+
   | 0x1a  |  0xa  |   0x1560   |   0x16e0   |
   | 0x1b  |  0xb  |   0x02a0   |   0x0240   |
   | 0x1c  |  0xb  |   0x17a0   |   0x1740   |
   | 0x22  |  0xc  |   0x0ff0   |   0x0fc0   |
   +-------+-------+------------+------------+

   The 0x2a0 against 0x17a0 one order apart is the pulse itself: low
   between shots, high on one. Each row is within one to four ticks of
   its step.

   0x5b81's geometry is the decisive evidence that these are the
   lasers. Vertex 1 is the origin, vertex 6 is at z=-47, and vertices
   2 to 5 are a diamond of radius 2 at z=-25; the eight faces are four
   triangles to each apex. A 47-by-4 spindle rendered in a black-to-
   red-to-yellow ramp is a glowing bolt and nothing else.

2.4.  Where the craft sits on screen

   Mesh_TransformVerts projects to

       sx = ((x << 4) / (z >> 3)) + 0xa0
       sy = ((y << 4) / (z >> 3)) + 0x48 + g_SkyBias

   and 0x48 is 72, not 100. THE MESH IS ONLY CENTRED WHEN g_SkyBias IS
   0x1c, which is what img 0x0292 - the planet part - sets. That is
   the LAST write to it in Demo_Entry: the only other writers anywhere
   are img 0x52ef in Demo_Timeline and Voxel_LatchCamera, which no
   part after the landscape calls. So 0x1c stands for the planet, the
   star cut, the whole battle and the warp out [verified], and read
   live at order 0x16 it is 0x001c [observed].

   Points_Render uses a flat + 0x64 instead, img 0x3241, so THE STARS
   ARE CENTRED WHATEVER g_SkyBias IS. A craft 28 pixels above a
   centred starfield is the signature of having zeroed it somewhere.

   And because Glow_Render's box is a fixed screen offset whose centre
   is the screen's - 0x2d60 is row 36 column 96, so rows 36..163 of
   128 - a craft displaced that way puts every muzzle flash and every
   hit splat on empty space. The two symptoms have one cause.

3.  The glow

   Glow_Render at 1000:2736 [verified]. A 128x128 field at segment
   0x4a79, blurred and decayed, drawn into the fire band.

   Per cell, img 0x2751 to 0x2787:

       AX = [SI-1] + [SI-0x80] + [SI+1] + [SI+0x80]
            with ADC into AH, so the 10-bit sum does not wrap
       AX >>= 2                        the average - a box blur
       DH = AL
       if AX != 0:
           DH--                        THE DECAY
           AL = (AX >> 1) + 0x60       into the fire ramp at 96
           clamp to 0xbf               191, the top of the ramp
           write the pixel
       [DI] = DH                       to the OTHER buffer

   THE DECAY IS WHAT MAKES IT DIE. A box blur conserves its total and
   would settle into a steady glow; the DEC at img 0x2771 takes one
   off every non-zero cell per frame.

IT IS NOT DOUBLE-BUFFERED, and this is the trap in the routine.
   img 0x2791 loads g_GlowDst into AX and XCHGs it with g_GlowSrc, so
   g_GlowSrc takes the destination's value and THE XCHG'S OTHER HALF
   IS DROPPED IN AX. g_GlowDst at img 0x98b1 has no writer anywhere in
   the image - its only two references are the reads here - so from
   the SECOND call on both point at 0x1880 and the blur reads its own
   output: the taps behind the cursor are this frame's values and the
   ones ahead are last frame's, which drags the glow along the scan.
   Treating the pair as a working double buffer gives a symmetric blob
   and the wrong effect.

   THE BUFFERS LIE INSIDE THE VOXEL'S COLOUR MAP. Segment 0x4a79 is
   the 256x256 colour map Terrain_BuildShade fills - effects/voxel.txt
   section 2 - and 0x1880 and 0x5900 are offsets into it. Nothing
   clears them before the sync-0x19 part reaches order 0x1a, so the
   first glow frames blur what the landscape left there and a faint
   square fades out over about a second. Measured at the moire, one
   part earlier: the bytes at 0x1880 run 0x0e to 0x31 [observed], and
   src/ reproduces them byte for byte from the generator chain plus
   Terrain_TintWater.

   Shots_ClearGlowField at 1000:279b clears 0x20a8 dwords from 0x1800
   - 0x82a0 bytes, both 0x4000 buffers AND the 32-slot particle table,
   ending exactly on the table's last byte.

   The row stride is assembled as 0x80 columns then BX += 0xc0, which
   is the 320 screen stride, img 0x2789.

4.  The flag

   MODEL 0x5be7, drawn by Car_Run at img 0x248a - the TINY flag in the
   car scene [observed].

   THERE ARE TWO FLAGS IN THE DEMO and only this one is a mesh. The
   big waving flag with VISTA on it, one scene earlier, is
   Flag_Render at 1000:2504 displacing a text panel - effects/flag.txt
   - and is unrelated to this model. Both readings stand; see
   effects/scenes.txt section 3.4.

   11 verts, 6 faces, all quads [verified]:

   +---------+-------+------------------------------------------------+
   | Faces   | Attr  | What                                           |
   +=========+=======+================================================+
   | 0 to 3  | 0x60  | a square column rotated 45 degrees, 21 tall    |
   |         |       | and 2 across - THE POLE. Verts 1 to 8          |
   +---------+-------+------------------------------------------------+
   | 4 and 5 | 0x20  | one quad and its reverse winding - the two     |
   |         |       | sides of THE BANNER. Verts 9, 10, 11 and 4:    |
   |         |       | out to x=9, between y=-9 and y=-16             |
   +---------+-------+------------------------------------------------+

   Attribute 0x60 puts the pole in the fire ramp at 96, so it reads
   dark red to yellow; 0x20 puts the banner in the blue band at 32.
   See subsystems/palette.txt section 2.

   IT DOES NOT WAVE, and this is the one claim worth being careful
   about - the waving one is a different scene and a different
   mechanism, described at the top of this section. A waving flag
   needs the vertices deformed per frame, and
   NOTHING IN THE IMAGE WRITES TO THE MODEL DATA REGION - checked by
   scanning every cs-relative store in the first 0xb000 bytes. The
   model's only motion is the camera's, and its placement is fixed:
   img 0x2469 puts it at the craft's own target less 0xb800 in z and
   0x5200 in x, with pitch 0, YAW FIXED AT 0x200 and roll 0.

   Car_Run draws THREE objects per frame, each skipped when
   Mesh_SetupTransform returns a camera-space z of 0x28000 or more, so
   each pops in as the camera closes:

       1.  0x585f  the craft, from g_9484/88/8c and g_9943/45/47
       2.  0x5be7  the flag, at the fixed offset above
       3.  0x94f4  the car, from g_9478/7c/80 and g_995b/5d/5f.
                   g_947c is the ground height Terrain_HeightAt
                   returned at img 0x51c1 - which is what puts the car
                   ON the terrain rather than through it

6.  The transcription

   sim/xcursion/parts.py has glow_render, and MODEL_BASES now carries all
   seven entries with MODEL_NAMES beside it. sim/xcursion/demo.py part 15
   is the battle and M cycles every model, so the flag and both

7.  Open questions

   o Text_BuildBanner at 1000:33a0 against Text_BuildMask at
     1000:330f. The two are near-identical glyph blitters differing in

8.  The flight models - modes 9, 0xa and 0xb

   One handler per part, dispatched out of g_AnimDispatch at
   cs:0x982d - see subsystems/animation.txt. Modes 9 and 0xb begin
   with Battle_Pursue, and so do modes 8 and 0xc. MODE 0xa DOES NOT -
   it is the one exception, which is why its peel-away rotate at img
   0x131a runs on a roll latch nothing in the handler sets.

8.1.  Battle_Pursue, 1000:1ce8

   CRAFT B CHASES CRAFT A, and modes 8 and 0xc use it too [verified]:

       latch A's pitch, yaw and roll
       rotate (0, 0, -0x400) by them       -> A's FORWARD VECTOR
       negate it; add A's z to the z part  -> a point on A's heading
       subtract B's position               -> the vector from B to it
       SHIFT RIGHT 4 AND ADD to B

   One sixteenth of the remaining gap per tick. It never arrives and
   never overshoots, needs no velocity stored and no clamp - the
   cheapest pursuit curve there is.

   The camera moves too: the forward vector >> 6 is added to g_CamX,
   g_CamY and g_CamZ, so the view drifts along A's heading.

   ON RETURN EAX, EBX AND EDX HOLD THE FORWARD VECTOR >> 6. The mode
   0xb handler depends on that and shifts them back left rather than
   recomputing, so this is not a void function however it looks.

8.2.  Mode 9, img 0x1264 - the approach

       Battle_Pursue
       if A.z < 0x1e000: A.z += 0x60       A CLAMP, NOT A WRAP
       A.pitch += 3 ; A.yaw += 2 ; A.roll += 4
       B.pitch = A.pitch ; B.yaw = A.yaw ; B.roll -= 2

   A flies in from far off and halts at z = 0x1e000. CRAFT B COPIES A'S
   PITCH AND YAW OUTRIGHT and rolls the other way, so the two are
   banked against each other without B carrying an attitude model of
   its own.

8.3.  Mode 0xa, img 0x12a5 - the pass

       B.x     = 2 * sin(cs:0x998f)
       B.yaw   = (B.x >> 7) + 0x400
       B.y     = 2 * sin(cs:0x9991)
       B.pitch = B.y >> 7
       cs:0x998f += 4 ; cs:0x9991 += 8
       A.roll += 3 ; B.roll -= 2 ; g_CamZ += 0x400
       if A.roll >= 0x708:
           A.pitch -= 8
           A.position += rotate(0, 0, -8) by A's pitch and yaw,
                         less 0x200 in z

   B FLIES A LISSAJOUS FIGURE. The two angles advance at 4 and 8, a
   1:2 ratio, which is what makes the path a figure rather than a
   circle.

   AND IT BANKS BY READING ITS OWN POSITION. Yaw and pitch are just
   the position shifted right 7. No velocity is kept and none is
   needed: the derivative of a sine is another sine a quarter turn on,
   so a shifted position is a bank angle that leads the curve by the
   right amount for free.

   A is stationary for the first part of the pass and then peels away
   along its own heading once its roll passes 0x708.

   THE TWO PHASE ACCUMULATORS ARE PLASMA_GENERATE'S OWN VARIABLES.
   cs:0x998f and cs:0x9991 are g_PlasmaC0 and g_PlasmaC1, written and
   read by Plasma_Generate at img 0x3fff to 0x4081 [verified]. Mode 0xa
   borrows them as Lissajous phase because no plasma is being generated
   during the battle. The names are correct for Plasma_Generate and
   actively misleading here; do not rename them to suit this handler.

8.4.  Mode 0xb, img 0x1351 - the locked formation

       Battle_Pursue, then recover the forward vector by << 6
       A.position = -(forward >> 2), with 0x8800 added to z
       B.position = A.position + forward
       A.roll -= 4 ; B.roll += 2
       A.pitch += (0xff60 - A.pitch) >> 6

   B sits EXACTLY ONE FORWARD VECTOR AHEAD OF A, rigidly - which makes
   B the farther of the two and is the reason Battle_Run flips its
   draw order on this mode and only this one.

   A's pitch eases toward 0xff60 by a sixty-fourth of the remaining
   distance, the same shift-and-add curve Battle_Pursue uses for
   position. The demo uses that one idiom for every smooth approach it
   has.
