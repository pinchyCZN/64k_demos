PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                   EXCURSION: THE MAPPED SPHERE


Abstract

   Planet_Render at 1000:2925 and Planet_BuildMap at 1000:42d8: a
   texture-mapped sphere over a starfield with a 3D object in front.

   Six parts call it, more than any other renderer in the demo, and in
   four of them THE BALL IS SUPPRESSED - the same function serves as
   the plain space scene. That is what made it hard to recognise.

   The texture is the 256x256 diamond-square field the landscape uses
   as its heightmap, drawn through the land and water bands, so one
   planet carries the terrain colours without a byte of extra data.

1.  The table

   Planet_BuildMap fills SEGMENT 0x6a79, the general scratch buffer,
   with 128x128 words - one per screen sample, each a packed texture
   offset, or 0xf380 for "off the ball" [verified]. 0x6a79 is a segment
   value, so it is linear 0x6a790 and nothing to do with image offset
   0x6a79.

   It starts by building its own square table, 90 entries at
   cs:0xaf7c [verified]:

       img 42d8    g_SqrTable[k] = (k+1)^2,  k = 0 to 89

   The +1 keeps the centre sample off zero, and it is why
   Sqr_NearestRoot returns an offset one entry past the answer.

   The radius falls out of one subtraction [verified]:

       img 42ef    AX = g_SqrTable[63] - g_SqrTable[31]
                      = 64*64 - 32*32
                      = 3072
       img 42fb    Sqr_NearestRoot -> g_PlanetRadiusSq
       img 4334    read back as the nearest square, 3025

   SO THE SPHERE HAS RADIUS 64 AND IS CLIPPED AT z = 32. The disc on
   screen is radius 55 inside a 128-wide box, and the visible cap
   subtends 60 degrees - cos = 32/64.

   Per sample, img 0x430b to 0x43aa [verified]:

       r^2 = (|cl-64|+1)^2 + (|ch-64|+1)^2       two table reads
       if 3025 <= r^2:  [di] = 0xf380, done      img 43a1
       z   = sqrt(64*64 - r^2)                   Sqr_NearestRoot
       u   = ((64 - ch) * 64 + z) / z + 64       img 4357-4370
       v   = ((64 - cl) * 64 + z) / z + 64       img 4373-438e
       [di] = u | (v << 8)                       img 439c

   The parameterisation is LATERAL OVER Z - the tangent, not the true
   spherical arcsine. It still compresses toward the limb, which is
   what makes it read as a ball and not a lens [verified by
   transcription]:

       +------------------+--------------------------+
       | Where            | Texels per 16 pixels     |
       +==================+==========================+
       | at the centre    | 17                       |
       +------------------+--------------------------+
       | at the rim       | 26                       |
       +------------------+--------------------------+

   Both coordinates are negated, so the texture is mapped THROUGH the
   centre - mirrored in both axes. The half-divisor added before each
   divide is round-to-nearest.

   9033 of the 16384 samples are inside the ball, 55.1 per cent
   [verified by transcription].

2.  The renderer

   Planet_Render draws three things in order [verified]:

       img 2925    clear g_WorkSeg, 0x3e80 dwords = 320x200
       img 2935    POINTS_RENDER - the stars go down first
       img 293d    ES = SEGMENT 0x6a79   the offset table
       img 2942    FS = SEGMENT 0x2a79   THE TEXTURE
       img 2957    BP = g_PlanetTexPan
                   ... the ball ...
       img 299b    MESH_DRAW - the object goes in front
       img 299e    Vga_Present

   Segment 0x2a79 is the 256x256 field Plasma_Generate writes and
   Voxel_Render reads as its heightmap - see effects/voxel.txt. The
   planet surface IS the landscape heightmap, sampled from a different
   starting offset.

   The colour, img 0x2978 [verified]:

       texel >> 2, then - 0x20, then & 0x3f

   which lands in palette 0 to 0x3f: Pal_BuildMain's land band at 0 to
   0x1f and water band at 0x20 to 0x3f. See subsystems/palette.txt. So
   the ball comes out brown-and-blue like the terrain, roughly half
   each [verified by transcription].

   PAL_BUILDGREYLOW BEFORE THE SYNC 0x34 CALL REWRITES 0 TO 0x1F AS
   GREY. The same ball, the same table, the same texture - and a grey
   moon.

2.1.  It ends on a row, not just an order

   img 0x29a4 to 0x29ba - the loop takes TWO conditions and keeps
   going while EITHER holds [verified]:

       order < g_MusicPosTarget          img 0x29ad
       row   < g_9917                    img 0x29ba

   So a part running this renderer does not stop at an order
   boundary. It runs on until the row counter inside the current
   pattern reaches g_9917, which both the sync 0x0c planet and the
   star cut after it set to 0x16.

   THAT IS WHAT TIMES THE PART AFTER IT. The rotozoomer inherits the
   counter at 22 rather than 0, and mode 5 stops working at 24, so it
   gets three rows rather than twenty-four - see effects/roto.txt
   section 4. Ending on the order alone hands the next part a counter
   of zero and its effect runs four times too long.

3.  The approach

   g_PlanetApproach at cs:0x9464 is reloaded PER ROW at img 0x2961 and
   advanced 0x400 per column; img 0x2967 skips every column whose
   running value reaches 0x38000 [verified]. So it is a column count,
   not a perspective scale - the ball never changes size.

   THE CUTOFF COUNT AND THE SCREEN ORIGIN ARE THE SAME NUMBER, which
   is why no write ever wraps across a row:

       origin x = 224 - (approach >> 10)          img 2947
       columns  = (0x38000 - approach) >> 10      img 2967
       both equal N

   Column i lands at x = N - i, and the first 11 table columns are the
   sentinel, so writes run from x = N-11 down to x = 1. The ball enters
   at the left edge, is whole from N = 118, and lands centred at x =
   160 when approach is 0 [verified by transcription]:

       +-------------+---------+---------------------------+
       | approach    | columns | drawn extent              |
       +=============+=========+===========================+
       | 0xfa000     | 0       | nothing at all            |
       +-------------+---------+---------------------------+
       | 0x30000     | 32      | x   1.. 21                |
       +-------------+---------+---------------------------+
       | 0x24000     | 80      | x   1.. 69                |
       +-------------+---------+---------------------------+
       | 0x1a800     | 118     | x   1..107  - whole       |
       +-------------+---------+---------------------------+
       | 0x0         | 128     | x 107..213  - centred     |
       +-------------+---------+---------------------------+

   Four of the six parts set it to 0xfa000, skipping all 128 columns.
   THE BALL IS SIMPLY NOT THERE in those parts and Planet_Render is the
   plain starfield-and-object scene.

4.  Which part draws what

   Every call site, with the mode and sync target in force [verified]:

   +------+------+-------+--------------+-----------------------------+
   | img  | sync | mode  | approach     | What is on screen           |
   +======+======+=======+==============+=============================+
   | 02e9 | 0x0c |  4    | 0, the       | THE PLANET. Centred, pan    |
   |      |      |       | static value | 0x8000, under Pal_BuildMain |
   |      |      |       |              | - brown and blue            |
   +------+------+-------+--------------+-----------------------------+
   | 03b1 | 0x0d |  4    | 0xfa000      | stars and the object        |
   +------+------+-------+--------------+-----------------------------+
   | 04da | 0x15 |  8    | 0xfa000      | stars and the object        |
   +------+------+-------+--------------+-----------------------------+
   | 0542 | 0x16 |  8    | 0xfa000      | stars and the object        |
   +------+------+-------+--------------+-----------------------------+
   | 4dcf | 0x33 |  8    | 0xfa000      | stars and the object, and   |
   |      |      |       |              | IT FADES IN FROM WHITE      |
   +------+------+-------+--------------+-----------------------------+
   | 4e94 | 0x34 | 0x14  | 0, set at    | THE MOON. Centred, pan      |
   |      |      |       | img 4e0e     | 0xc0, Pal_BuildGreyLow      |
   +------+------+-------+--------------+-----------------------------+

   THE MOON'S SURFACE IS THE PLASMA TURNED INTO A SLOPE AND BACK
   [verified]. Its part generates a cloud into 0x2a79 with mask 0x3ff
   and bias 0x200 - a flatter one than any other part asks for - runs
   Terrain_BuildShade over it into 0x4a79, and then img 0x4e64 copies
   that back as (v << 2) + 0x80.

   THAT TRANSFORM AND THE RENDERER'S CANCEL. Planet_Render finishes
   every texel with (t >> 2) - 0x20 at img 0x297b, which undoes it
   exactly, so THE INDEX REACHING THE SCREEN IS THE SHADE - and
   Terrain_BuildShade clamps that to 1 .. 0x1e. Live, the moon's
   pixels are 0x06 to 0x14 [observed].

   Which is why Pal_BuildGreyLow at img 0x3e79 only writes THIRTY-TWO
   ENTRIES and stops: 0 to 0x1f is the whole range the moon can
   produce. It greys exactly that band and leaves the craft's own
   entries, 0xce and up, as Pal_BuildMain left them.

   The part does NOT set the shade scale or divisor - cs:0x986d and
   cs:0x986f carry the opening's 0x1000 and 0x53 [verified]. It sets
   only the field size and origin, to the same full 256 by 256 the
   opening uses. effects/voxel.txt section 1.2.

   THE SYNC 0x33 PART IS THE ONLY ONE OF THE SIX THAT FADES IN, and
   it fades FROM WHITE, not from black [verified]. img 0x4dbb sets
   g_FadeLevel to 0x3d80 - POSITIVE, just under Pal_Fade's +0x4000
   clamp, so the bias it adds washes every entry to full - and mode 8
   walks it back down to 0 by 0x60 a tick at img 0x124e. Every other
   fade in the demo climbs from a negative level, so reading this one
   as the usual fade gives a part that starts black and ends white.

   That fade belongs to this part and not to the tunnel exit before
   it: the exit ends on its own black, and nothing between img 0x0a2a
   and img 0x4dbb touches the level.

   It is also the first part of Demo_Timeline, which Demo_Entry
   reaches by a JMP at img 0x0a37 rather than a call - so the sync
   0x32 tunnel is the last part of one chain and this is the first of
   the next. effects/timeline.txt section 2.

   Both balls START centred and whole, and both then MOVE: the mode 4
   animation handler at img 0x101e runs in the sync 0x0c and 0x0d
   parts and does

       inc g_PlanetTexPan                   the surface rotates
       g_PlanetApproach += 2 * the rotated x of the craft's heading

   so the planet turns one texel of pan a tick and drifts across the
   screen BECAUSE THE CRAFT IS FLYING, not on a timer of its own
   [verified]. See subsystems/animation.txt section 2.3.

   The two balls differ in their starting g_PlanetTexPan - 0x8000
   against 0xc0, so different regions of the same field - and in the
   palette.

   g_PlanetApproach is statically 0 in the image, which is what puts
   the planet on screen at sync 0x0c without any part writing it
   [verified]. Nothing re-zeroes it until img 0x4e0e, so once sync 0x0d
   sets 0xfa000 the ball stays away for the whole middle of the demo.

   The sync 0x34 moon has mode 0x14, whose handler at img 0x1702 does
   g_PlanetTexPan -= 0x100 EVERY FOURTH TICK [verified]. So the moon
   turns too - backwards, one texture row per four ticks, against the
   planet's forward one texel a tick. Two different rates and two
   different directions out of the same renderer.

   This also settles the four parts at sync 0x0d, 0x15, 0x16 and 0x33
   that effects/timeline.txt listed as unexplained uses of an
   unidentified renderer: they are the space and battle scenes, stars
   plus a craft, drawn by the planet routine with its planet turned
   off.

4.1.  The sync 0x0c part's block, in full

   img 0x0253 to 0x02e2, and NOTHING HERE IS INHERITED - the part
   sets its own craft, its own light and its own bias before calling
   the renderer [verified]:

   +------------------+---------+------------------------------------+
   | Global           | Value   | At img                             |
   +==================+=========+====================================+
   | transform x, y   | 0       | 0x0253, 0x025b                     |
   | transform z      | 0x2e000 | 0x0265                             |
   | obj pitch/yaw/   | 0       | 0x026f, 0x0276, 0x027d             |
   |   roll           |         |                                    |
   | g_CamAngleNext   | 0       | 0x028b                             |
   | g_SkyBias        | 0x1c    | 0x0292                             |
   | g_GroundRamp     | 0x1c    | 0x0299                             |
   | g_LightPitch     | 0       | 0x02b4                             |
   | g_LightYaw       | 0xfe00  | 0x02bb                             |
   | g_LightRoll      | 0       | 0x02c2                             |
   | g_AnimMode       | 4       | 0x02c9                             |
   | g_MusicPosTarget | 0x0c    | 0x02db                             |
   +------------------+---------+------------------------------------+

   THE CRAFT STARTS DEAD AHEAD AND COMES AT THE CAMERA. Mode 4 adds
   the rotated z of a -0x10 step to the transform every tick, and
   Vec_Rotate3 returns times 64, so at yaw 0 that is -1024 a tick
   against a starting 0x2e000 - about 184 ticks to reach the eye.

   The light is NOT the opening's: yaw 0xfe00 here against 0xff00
   there, with pitch and roll both zero.

4.2.  The starfield has its own camera

   Points_Render subtracts g_StarCamX, g_StarCamY and g_StarCamZ at
   img 0x9919, 0x991b and 0x991d before projecting, and MODE 4 IS
   WHAT FLIES THEM - img 0x1072 and img 0x1077 add half the rotated
   x and a quarter of the rotated z plus 0x200. Nothing else writes
   them, so leaving them at zero gives a correct but frozen field.

   EVERY SUBTRACTION THERE IS 16-BIT and wraps - img 0x3222, 0x3227
   and 0x322c are `sub` on word registers, and the shift at img
   0x3231 is `shr`, unsigned. A star and the camera are each a
   signed word, so their difference can leave 16 bits; doing it in
   32 bits and shifting arithmetically sends whole regions of the
   field off screen at once, where the demo wraps them round and
   keeps the count steady.

   The two cutoffs at img 0x3241 and img 0x324d are `jae`, so they
   are unsigned over the 16-bit result and need no separate test for
   a negative coordinate.

5.  The transcription

   src/xcursion/xcursion_planet.c has all four routines in C, and
   BOTH OF THE SPHERE MAP'S DIVIDES ARE IDIV - img 0x4370 and img
   0x438c - so they truncate toward zero. C's / is right; the Python
   below floors and so differs over the half of the sphere where
   0x40 - c goes negative, the same class of divergence recorded in
   ../formats/mesh.txt section 3.1.

   sim/xcursion/parts.py has planet_build_map and planet_render, and
   sim/xcursion/demo.py parts 13 and 14 are the planet and the moon. A and S
   drive g_PlanetApproach so the slide in section 3 can be watched.

   Nothing in either is ours. The position, size, texture coordinates,
   colour mapping and column cutoff are all the demo's, and the two
   parts use the demo's own pan values and palettes. The object
   ROTATION RATE is ours, as everywhere else - see formats/mesh.txt.

6.  Open questions

   o Which object is in front in each of the six parts. Mesh_Draw reads
     g_MeshFaces, and effects/timeline.txt section 3 places the stores,
     but the sync 0x0c and 0x33 parts set no model of their own and
     inherit whatever the previous part left.

   o Sqr_NearestRoot saturates above 8100 - 90 entries. Both callers
     stay under 4096, so it is not reached here.
