PCDEMOS 64k                                            Analysis Note


                      EXCURSION: THE MOIRE


Abstract

   Moire_Render at 1000:2818, part sync 0x14. Lines that squeeze
   together and expand - and NOTHING IN THE ROUTINE PLOTS A LINE.

   The lines are the mod-256 wrap of an accumulating ramp. Each pixel
   adds a smooth increment to itself every frame; because neighbouring
   columns differ by 1 in that increment, after n frames they differ
   by n, and an 8-bit accumulator bands at 256/n pixels.

   So the effect's whole content is one subtraction and one 8-bit
   store. It is the cheapest thing in the demo and among the most
   striking.

1.  The routine

   It is its own runner - img 0x286f loops the whole function until
   g_MusicPos reaches the target, so there is no separate Run
   function. Called once, at img 0x0457, with target 0x14 [verified].

   Its g_AnimMode is 7, whose handler at img 0x11dd only fades. That
   is all this part needs, because the phase is advanced inside the
   renderer rather than by a handler.

       DS = 0x2a79   THE ACCUMULATOR. It clobbers the texture there
       ES = 0xa000   the screen, written directly - no work buffer
       img 2818  g_RenderDisabled skips the render and just waits

   g_RenderDisabled at img 0xa87d is NOT an effect parameter. It is
   F1's detail switch out of Input_Poll at img 0x0afb, which runs the
   demo at 25 Hz with rendering and the animation modes turned off;
   every other key clears it. It is 0 in the image, so a
   reconstruction without an F1 key renders unconditionally.

   g_MoirePhase at img 0x9921 is read and written by this routine and
   by nothing else [verified], so it starts from its image static of
   0.

   per pixel, img 0x2847 to 0x285e:

       increment = sin5(rowPhase) + (200 - row) - col + sin5(colPhase)
       field[di] = (field[di] + increment) & 0xff
       screen[di] = field[di]

   ADD AL, [DI] at img 0x2852 IS A BYTE ADD. The accumulation is mod
   256 and the carry never reaches AH, which is why the wrap is clean.

   The three phases all advance:

       img 2859   the column phase, 8 per pixel
       img 2860   the row phase, 8 per row
       img 2866   g_MoirePhase, 2 per FRAME

   The two are seeded differently, and that asymmetry matters. SI, the
   row phase, is loaded from g_MoirePhase ONCE at img 0x282d, before
   the row loop, and then advances 8 per row - so it accumulates down
   the screen. BX, the column phase, is RE-SEEDED from g_MoirePhase at
   the top of every row, img 0x2837, so every row starts its column
   sweep from the same angle.

1.1.  The part, and the fade

   img 0x042d, in order [verified]:

       Pal_BuildBluePurple                        img 0x042d
       g_FadeLevel = 0xc000, Pal_Fade             img 0x0430
       ZERO ALL 64 KB of segment 0x2a79           img 0x043a
       g_AnimMode = 7                             img 0x044a
       g_MusicPosTarget = 0x14                    img 0x0450
       Moire_Render                               img 0x0457

   SAME PALETTE AND SAME FADE FLOOR AS THE PLASMA WARP BEFORE IT, so
   the moire rises out of black with no cut and no palette change -
   see effects/warp.txt section 4 for the ramp.

   THE ACCUMULATOR IS ZEROED FIRST, img 0x0447, 0x4000 dwords over the
   field the warp was just sampling. Entering on a field that still
   holds a plasma accumulates from that instead of from flat and the
   bands never resolve.

   Mode 7 at img 0x11dd is two lines and they go opposite ways
   [verified]:

       order != 0x13 : if the level is not 0, += 0x100, then Pal_Fade
       order == 0x13 : += 0x60, then Pal_Fade

   Below the last order the level climbs from 0xc000 by 0x100 and
   STOPS DEAD at zero, which is the fade in - 64 passes. At order 0x13
   it keeps climbing past zero by 0x60 a pass, and a positive bias is
   Pal_Fade brightening, so THE PART ENDS BY WASHING OUT TO WHITE
   rather than dimming.

2.  Why there are lines at all

   Nothing draws an edge. The field is 8-bit and the increment is
   smooth, so the value at a pixel after n frames is about n times its
   increment, TAKEN MOD 256 - and the places where that sum crosses a
   multiple of 256 are where the colour jumps from 255 to 0.

   Those jumps are the lines. They are an artifact of integer overflow
   being allowed to show, which is why the effect costs nothing: there
   is no edge test, no threshold and no second buffer.

3.  Why they squeeze, and why they expand again

   img 0x285d decrements DX once per column, which is the -col term.
   Neighbouring columns therefore differ by exactly 1 in their
   increment, so they differ by n after n frames, and the mod-256
   banding period is 256/n columns.

   Measured on the transcription, band width across the centre row
   [verified by transcription]:

   +--------+-------------+--------+-------------+
   | frame  | band width  | frame  | band width  |
   +========+=============+========+=============+
   |    1   |   320 px    |   32   |    8.4 px   |
   |    2   |   160 px    |   64   |    4.2 px   |
   |    4   |    64 px    |  120   |    2.2 px   |
   |    8   |    32 px    |  200   |    3.4 px   |
   |   16   |  16.8 px    |  256   |    4.5 px   |
   +--------+-------------+--------+-------------+

   SO IT SQUEEZES AND THEN EXPANDS, which is what the demo shows
   [observed].

   THE TURN IS NOT A CLEAN WRAP OF THE ACCUMULATOR, and that was the
   obvious wrong answer. If the increment were exactly C - col, the
   per-column step would reach 256 at frame 256 and the bands would
   vanish and restart. They do not: the width bottoms out near frame
   120 and widens smoothly after it.

   What turns it is the phases. g_MoirePhase advances 2 a frame and
   feeds both the row and column sine lookups, so the increment is
   never exactly C - col and the effective step drifts off any integer
   multiple. The spacing breathes instead of resetting.

   The (200 - row) term gives each row a different offset, which is
   what makes the bands diagonal rather than vertical.

4.  The sine read one byte in

       img 2840   MOV DX, word ptr CS:[SI + 0x7f5f]
       img 284d   ADD AX, word ptr CS:[BX + 0x7f5f]

   g_SinTable is at img 0x7f5e and holds DWORDS. A word read at +1 is
   bytes 1 and 2 of a little-endian dword, which is bits 8 to 23 - so
   THE ODD OFFSET IS A FREE SHIFT RIGHT BY 8 [verified]:

       angle 0x000   dword      0   word at +1      0
       angle 0x200   dword   4095   word at +1     15
       angle 0x600   dword  -4095   word at +1    -16

   A 5-bit sine of +/-15, with the sign preserved and no shift
   instruction. It is not a misaligned read, and "correcting" it to
   0x7f5e would multiply the sine term by 256 and destroy the effect.

   At +/-15 against a ramp that spans 320, the sines bend the bands
   rather than forming them.

5.  The palette, which has no red in it

   THE MOIRE DOES NOT RUN UNDER Pal_BuildMain, and the ordering is
   what settles it [verified]:

       img 042d   Pal_BuildBluePurple
       img 0437   Pal_Fade
       img 0457   MOIRE_RENDER
       img 045d   Pal_BuildMain

   Pal_BuildMain runs AFTER the moire's only call site, so the part
   carries the palette installed at img 0x042d and none of
   Pal_BuildMain's fire ramp reaches it.

   Pal_BuildBluePurple at 1000:3cac, six bands into g_PaletteBase:

   +----------+-----+--------+----------+--------+-----------------+
   | Indices  | Num | R      | G        | B      | Reads as        |
   +==========+=====+========+==========+========+=================+
   |   0.. 63 |  64 | 0      | 0        | n      | black -> blue   |
   |  64.. 95 |  32 | n      | 0        | 0x3f   | blue -> purple  |
   |  96..127 |  32 | 0x3f   | n        | 0x3f   | purple -> white |
   | 128..159 |  32 | 0x3f   | n down   | 0x3f   | white -> purple |
   | 160..191 |  32 | n down | 0        | 0x3f   | purple -> blue  |
   | 192..255 |  64 | 0      | 0        | n down | blue -> black   |
   +----------+-----+--------+----------+--------+-----------------+

   The 32-entry bands step by 2 so they still cover 0..62.

   NO ENTRY IN IT HAS R ABOVE B [verified by transcription], so
   nothing in the palette can read as red or orange - R only ever
   rises while B is already full, which gives purple. That is why the
   demo shows this effect in blue, purple and a grey-white.

   AND IT IS CYCLIC: index 0 and index 255 are both black. THE
   SYMMETRY IS THE POINT. Section 2's whole mechanism is a mod-256
   accumulator, so a palette whose ends did not meet would put a hard
   edge at every wrap. This one runs out to white and back, so the
   wrap is invisible and the bands read as smooth rings.

6.  The transcription

   src/xcursion/xcursion_moire.c is Xcur_MoireRender, with Xcur_Sin5
   in xcursion_tables.c and the part's block as scene 9 of
   xcursion_demo.c.

   IT IS EXACT. Against a live run caught partway through a frame, the
   accumulator at offset 0 is the C's 26th render and at offset 0x8000
   its 25th - the split the in-flight row and column predict
   [observed].

   sim/xcursion/parts.py has sin5_at and interfere_render, and
   sim/xcursion/demo.py part 21 is the effect; J resets the
   accumulator so the squeeze can be watched from the start. The field
   is the demo's accumulator and every term is the demo's; nothing
   here is a stand-in.

   The palette is Pal_BuildBluePurple's - section 5.

7.  Open questions

   o THE DEMO'S RENDER AND ANIMATION RATES ARE NOT EXACTLY 2 TO 1.
     Measured on one frame of a live run: g_MoirePhase 0x32 is 25
     renders while g_FadeLevel 0xf000 is 48 mode-7 passes, so the
     ratio is 1.92 and not 2 [observed]. The two are clocked
     independently - Timer_Isr increments a render counter every tick
     and runs the animation on every other one - so the ratio is a
     property of how long a frame takes on the machine, not of the
     code. The reconstruction fixes it at 2, which costs about one
     pass in fifty over this part.

   o What follows at img 0x045d: Pal_BuildMain, g_AnimMode = 8 and a
     pointer at img 0x98af set to 0x6a79. Not read.
