PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                      LITHIUM: THE TUNNEL


Abstract

   Scene 2.11's second half, straight after the glass shatters.

   THE ROCK IS NOT IN THIS SCENE, but it IS in a tunnel. Phase four
   of maybe_Scene_BumpShatter draws the tunnel, the flying glass and
   the rock together - effects/rock.txt - and this runner follows it
   with the tunnel alone.

   THE TUNNEL HAS NO MESH. It is rebuilt from the sine table every
   frame: a circle of points swept along a path, and the path is
   three angles advancing at fixed rates. Nothing about it is stored
   in the asset segment.

   THE CURVE IS 4:2:1. The three heading angles step by 0x100, 0x80
   and 0x40 a ring, so the path traces a Lissajous figure - smooth,
   non-repeating within a scene, and free of any spline or control
   points.

   All [verified] from the three routines.

2.  Tunnel_StepPath, the curve

       eax, ebx, ecx = (0, 0, 0x100)         a step straight ahead
       Vec_RotZ, SAR 8
       Vec_RotX, SAR 0xc
       Vec_RotY, SAR 0x10
       g_TunnelPath{X,Y,Z} += the rotated step
       g_RotAngleX += 0x100
       g_RotAngleY += 0x80
       g_RotAngleZ += 0x40

   A FIXED STEP ROTATED BY A TURNING FRAME. The heading is not
   stored as a direction; it is three Euler angles that advance by
   constant amounts every ring, so the path curves continuously.

   THE RATES ARE 4:2:1, which is what makes the shape read as a
   tunnel winding rather than a circle: the three axes come back into
   phase only after a long run.

   The same three angles are what Tunnel_Build rotates each ring by,
   so the rings stay perpendicular to the path without any extra
   work.

3.  Tunnel_Build, the rings

       DS = 0x2ad2;  si = cs:[0x88af] << 4        the vertex cursor
       g_TunnelPath{X,Y,Z} = the camera position, SAR 0xc then << 8,
           with z negated
       g_RotAngle{X,Y,Z} = 0
       g_TunnelFirstRing times:  Tunnel_StepPath
       then per ring, per vertex:
           eax = g_SinTable[bx + 0x800]            COSINE
           ebx = g_SinTable[bx]                    sine
           ecx = 0
           Vec_RotZ, SAR 0xa
           Vec_RotX, SAR 0xc
           Vec_RotY, SAR 0x11
           add g_TunnelPath{X,Y,Z}
           store at [si], [si+2], [si+4]

   A RING IS A CIRCLE. The cosine and the sine are one table read
   0x800 apart - subsystems/mesh3d.txt section 4 - so a vertex is
   (cos t, sin t, 0) carried into the path's frame.

   THE FIRST RINGS ARE WALKED AND DISCARDED. g_TunnelFirstRing steps
   the path forward before any vertex is written, which is how the
   geometry behind the camera is skipped without a clip test.
   maybe_Scene_BumpShatter sets that pair to -4 and 11.

   THE THREE SHIFTS DIFFER - 0xa, 0xc and 0x11 - so the ring is not
   simply rotated into place but scaled unequally on each axis, and
   the bore is an ellipse that changes shape along the run.

   3.1.  Sixteen vertices, two triangles a quad

      The vertex loop runs bx from 0 to 0x2000 in steps of 0x200, so a
      ring is SIXTEEN VERTICES - 0x2000 bytes is the whole 2048-entry
      sine table and 0x200 is a sixteenth of it.

      The texture coordinates are taken at img 0x055f:

          u = (g_RotAngleX >> 3) & 0xff
          v = (bx >> 3) & 0xff

      g_RotAngleX advances 0x100 a ring, so u advances 0x20 and the map
      repeats every eight rings along the bore; v advances 0x40 a vertex,
      so it repeats FOUR TIMES around the circumference.

      The faces follow at img 0x05a0, two triangles per quad and sixteen
      quads per ring:

          v0 = cx + a
          v1 = cx + ((a + 1) & 0xf)
          v2 = v1 + 0x10
          triangle 1 = v0, v1, v2
          triangle 2 = v2, cx + a + 0x10, cx + a

      THE & 0xf IS WHAT CLOSES THE BORE, wrapping the sixteenth quad onto
      the first with no seam vertex. img 0x05d0 subtracts 0x11 and masks
      with 0xf, and the mask also discards the ring base in CX because
      that is always a multiple of 0x10.

      Fifteen rings give 240 vertices and 448 triangles [verified by
      transcription].

4.  maybe_Scene_Tunnel

   1000:040b, from the main script at 1000:02a3 - directly after
   maybe_Scene_BumpShatter, which is the glass.

       cs:[0x8f80] = 0x36b;  g_TickHandler = 0x6b7f
       loop:
           Obj_LoadOrientA;  cs:[0x88af] = 0
           cs:[0x8fa6] = 0x6ad2
           Tunnel_Build
           the camera angles, SAR 4
           Scene_ProjectVerts;  Scene_DrawFacesReset
           Screen_BlitFull(0x4ad2);  Demo_PollKeys
       until the music position reaches 0x1e, then 0x1f

   THE POSITION IS SPLIT COARSE AND FINE. Tunnel_Build seeds the
   path from (pos >> 0xc) << 8 at img 0x04a3, while the runner sets
   g_CamPosX and g_CamPosZ from (-(pos + 1) & 0xfff) >> 4 at img
   0x0435 and 0x0449. The two sum to pos/16, so the geometry re-seeds
   every 0x1000 while the camera offset wraps - which is how the
   flight stays in small numbers however far it goes.

   IT IS DRAWN WITH THE SPHERE MAP. cs:[0x8fa6] is 0x6ad2, the same
   chrome environment map the rock and the running figure use -
   subsystems/texture.txt.

   IT BLITS THE WHOLE SCREEN rather than a dirty rectangle, because
   the tunnel covers all of it every frame - subsystems/raster.txt
   section 6.

5.  Tick_Tunnel, and why it never ends

   1000:6b7f, installed by the runner at 1000:0415.

   NOTHING IS APPENDED AND NOTHING IS FREED. The whole bore is
   regenerated from ring 0 every frame, so "endless" is a WINDOW
   SLIDING over a curve, not a mesh being extended.

   Every sixteenth tick:

       g_TunnelFirstRing++
       g_TunnelLastRing++

   Tunnel_Build pre-steps the path g_TunnelFirstRing times and throws
   those rings away - img 0x04e3 - then emits last minus first. So
   the count stays at 15 while the window walks forward: A RING
   APPEARS AT THE FAR END AND ONE DROPS OFF THE NEAR END. Being a
   thousand rings along costs only the pre-step loop and not one byte
   of storage.

   In the same sixteenth tick it recomputes the camera's velocity:

       g_RotAngle2{X,Y,Z} = g_SlotPitch, g_SlotYaw, g_SlotRoll
       eax, ebx, ecx = (0, 0, 0x100)
       Vec_RotZ2, SAR 8;  Vec_RotX2, SAR 0xc;  Vec_RotY2, SAR 0x10
       negate eax and ebx
       cs:[0x9013], [0x9017], [0x901b] = the result
       g_SlotPitch += 0x100;  g_SlotYaw += 0x80

   THAT IS Tunnel_StepPath'S ARITHMETIC EXACTLY - the same step, the
   same three rotations, the same shifts, the same 0x100 and 0x80
   increments - on the second angle set instead of the first. The
   camera walks the identical curve the tunnel is built along, one
   ring per 16 ticks, in lockstep with the window.

   IT DOES NOT ADVANCE THE ROLL. Tunnel_StepPath adds 0x40 to its
   third angle every ring; nothing here adds to g_SlotRoll, so the
   camera does not twist with the rings.

   Between those, every tick:

       g_SlotFlight{X,Y,Z} += the velocity
       g_SlotHome* += (g_SlotFlight* - g_SlotHome*) >> 5
       Pal_Animate;  Pal_Upload

   which is the smooth motion within one ring, and the colour cycling.

   g_RecordMode is the 16-tick counter here and the record/replay flag
   in Flight_Step - one byte, two jobs.

6.  How the eye stays on the window

   Two terms advance as the scene runs and they CANCEL.

   The pre-step pushes the window forward by ring(first), and the
   position term pulls it back by the same amount, so the window sits
   a bounded distance ahead of the eye for ever.

   6.1.  Why they match

      The camera's velocity IS the path's step - Tick_Tunnel rebuilds
      it from the same (0,0,0x100), the same three rotations and the
      same shifts, section 5. So:

          the camera integrates that step EVERY TICK
          the window advances one ring every SIXTEEN ticks
          the coarse/fine split divides the position by SIXTEEN

      Sixteen ticks of camera motion, divided by sixteen, is exactly
      one ring step. THE `& 0xf` AND THE `>> 4` ARE THE SAME NUMBER,
      and that is the whole lock. Change either and the tunnel slides
      away from the viewer at the difference.

   6.2.  The three sign choices

      Each axis cancels by a different route, and every one of them
      looks arbitrary until this is the reason:

      +------+--------------------------------+---------------------+
      | Axis | What is negated                | Where               |
      +======+================================+=====================+
      | z    | the PATH START, -((p>>0xc)<<8) | img 0x04bc, neg cx  |
      | x, y | the VELOCITY, neg eax / ebx    | img 0x6c67          |
      +------+--------------------------------+---------------------+

      On z the path start carries the minus, so coarse plus fine
      comes to 255 - pos/16 and cancels the pre-step directly. On x
      and y the velocity carries it instead, so the position
      accumulates the opposite of the path's own drift and pos/16
      cancels ring(first) the other way about.

   6.3.  Measured

      Transcribing both and running them together, the window's first
      ring in VIEW space over 12,000 ticks and 745 rings:

          x   -519 ..  517
          y   -520 ..  519
          z   -294 ..  775

      BOUNDED, NOT DRIFTING. The residual is the home position easing
      after the flight position by a 32nd plus the truncation in the
      split; it does not accumulate. sim/lithium/parts.py.

7.  Where the camera looks

   THE ORIENTATION IS THE THREE DECREMENTS IN Tick_Tunnel, and they
   reach the renderer by a route that hides them: they go to the
   EASED angle arrays, not the live ones.

       cs:[0x8af9] -= 0x10      ->  -0x100 a ring
       cs:[0x8b27] -= 0x08      ->  -0x80  a ring
       cs:[0x8b55] -= 0x0c      ->  -0xc0  a ring

   Obj_LoadOrientA, called at the top of the scene's loop at img
   0x041e, copies those three into g_SlotAngleX, Y and Z at 0x8a6f,
   0x8a9d and 0x8acb - and THOSE are what Scene_ProjectVerts rotates
   the world by, at img 0x2e96, 0x2eaf and 0x2ec8.

   X AND Y ARE THE EXACT NEGATION of Tunnel_StepPath's +0x100 and
   +0x80 a ring, so the camera turns into the bend precisely as fast
   as the bore turns away from it. Without them the eye keeps
   pointing down +z, and the first bend puts the wall in front of it.

   Z IS NOT. The path advances its third angle by 0x40 a ring and
   this takes 0xc0, so THE VIEW ROLLS 0x80 A RING relative to the
   tube. A perfect inverse would be 0x4 a tick; the demo uses 0xc.

   7.1.  There is no distance term

      The camera is exactly g_CamPosX, g_SlotPosY, g_CamPosZ, with
      nothing added. Scene_ProjectVerts ADDS the position BEFORE it
      rotates - img 0x2e84, then 0x2e96 - so an offset along the view
      axis is not available to a scene even if one were wanted: it
      would turn with the view instead of pushing the eye back.

   7.2.  The winding

      The bore is seen from INSIDE, and the authored winding is the
      one that survives: of 448 faces, 334 pass the backface test as
      authored against 99 reversed. The 84 rejected are the far wall
      seen through the near one.

8.  Open questions

   o The scene's length is a MUSIC cue - 1000:048d returns when the
     order reaches 0x1f - so how long it runs depends entirely on
     where the module has got to, and that makes it a measure of the
     music clock rather than of anything here.

     g_TunnelFirstRing IS A TICK COUNTER FOR THAT. It starts at -4
     when the bump scene hands over, advances one per sixteen ticks
     and is never reset, so it spans the shatter and the tunnel
     together. Measured against the original: at the point the fade
     out has run 59 ticks it reads 35, which is 624 ticks since the
     handover. The shatter phase is 293 of those [verified], so the
     TUNNEL ITSELF RUNS 331 TICKS.

   o Why cs:[0x8f80] is 0x36b here. Scene_DrawFaces rejects a face
     whose summed depth is at or below it - img 0x32aa - and most
     scenes set a different value, but what the number is derived
     from is not known.
