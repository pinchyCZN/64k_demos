PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                   LITHIUM: THE VISIBLE SEQUENCE


Abstract

   What the demo looks like, in order, and which code each scene is
   likely to be. The sequence is [observed] - it was written down
   while watching the demo run, not read out of the binary.

   THIS DOCUMENT DECIDES WHAT TO READ NEXT. It is not evidence about
   what the code does, and nothing in it may be implemented from.
   CLAUDE.md rule 12: match the original's CODE, never a capture of
   it. A colour named here is a reason to go and find the palette
   builder, never a palette to write down.

   Fourteen scenes, and four of them draw the same voxel surface.

1.  How to read this

   Every scene below carries two separate things, and they must not be
   confused:

   +------------+--------------------------------------------------------+
   | Seen       | [observed]. What appeared on screen. Reliable          |
   |            | about WHAT, unreliable about HOW - "chrome" is         |
   |            | a look, and three techniques produce it                |
   +------------+--------------------------------------------------------+
   | Candidate  | [guess] unless marked otherwise. A routine or          |
   |            | a landmark worth opening first. A wrong                |
   |            | candidate costs one function read; a wrong             |
   |            | name in the database misleads every read after it      |
   +------------+--------------------------------------------------------+

   Nothing in section 2 has been confirmed against the code. The
   per-scene work is to turn a candidate into a [verified] entry in
   ../README.txt and a document in this directory.

2.  The sequence

   2.1.  Scene 1 - warping chrome surface

      Seen: an environment-mapped surface, chrome-looking, with the
      surface itself warping. Palette cycling runs throughout scenes 1
      to 3.

      IT IS THE ROCK MESH AT FOUR TIMES SIZE [verified]. There is no
      height field and no surface: maybe_Scene_RockIntro loads 0x41ea
      at g_ObjScaleShift 0xe, close enough that only its curvature is
      on screen, and g_EnvMapAngle spins the reflection under it.
      ./rockintro.txt sections 2 and 4.

   2.2.  Scene 2 - a chrome rock moves in

      Seen: a rock with the same chrome surface enters over scene 1.

      IT IS THE SAME MESH AGAIN, AT 0x10 INSTEAD OF 0xe [verified].
      The runner draws a second copy from slot 8 once its Z falls
      inside -0x1f4 .. 0x2cec, so what arrives is the same 130-vertex
      model the "surface" already is - ./rockintro.txt section 2.
      Mesh_Transform rotates each per-vertex normal into view space
      and biases it by 0x80 to index the sphere map -
      ../subsystems/mesh3d.txt section 5.

      XCURSION solves the equivalent problem differently, in
      maybe_Mapped_Render on a parameterised sphere rather than a mesh
      - ../../xcursion/effects/planet.txt.

   2.3.  Scene 3 - the rock leaves, VISTA appears

      Seen: the rock exits and the word VISTA appears.

      Candidate [verified in part]: "VISTA" is in the image, at
      1000:90ad, in the credit table that starts at 1000:90a1 -
      ../README.txt section 7. So this scene draws from that table
      rather than from a bitmap.

   2.4.  Scene 4 - LITHIUM zooms away

      Seen: the word LITHIUM, starting close and receding to far.

      Candidate: "LITHIUM" is the FIRST entry of the same table, at
      1000:90a1 [verified]. A word that recedes is a scaled blit per
      frame, so the scene wants whatever scales the font.

   2.5.  Scene 5 - white fade into a voxel planet surface

      Seen: a fade through WHITE, not black, into a voxel-like planet
      surface with the camera sweeping over it.

      Candidate [verified]: the fade is Pal_Upload at 1000:3dcc with
      g_PalFade positive. Demo_Main stores 0x0100 into g_PalFade at
      1000:0265, and Pal_Upload adds g_PalFade >> 2 to every DAC
      component and clamps at 0x3f - so +256 drives every component to
      full and the screen goes WHITE. The black fades use 0xff80 at
      1000:024e and 0xffc0 at 1000:02ed. That is the fade mechanism
      settled; which store belongs to which scene is not.

      THE SURFACE IS NOT A VOXEL RENDERER, though it looks like one.
      Terrain_Build at 1000:4b3c emits it as a GRID MESH rebuilt every
      frame from a 128x128 heightmap, through the same polygon
      pipeline as the ships - terrain.txt. It comes back in scenes 8,
      9 and 12, and Terrain_Build is called from exactly four scene
      runners, which is the check.

      XCURSION solves the same shot the other way, with a real
      y-buffer span fill in Ground_Render -
      ../../xcursion/effects/ground.txt.

   2.6.  Scene 6 - demo credits, lower left

      Seen: credits for the demo appear in the lower left over the
      surface.

      Candidate [verified in part]: the credit records at 1000:90b9
      onward read as alternating role and name - MUSIC / TOX,
      MUSIC / EWACT, FONTS / JANGLER, CODE / TEO - which is exactly
      what a credit roll needs.

      The glyphs are likely the ROM 8x8 font, whose pointer
      Demo_Main captures into g_RomFont8x8Off and g_RomFont8x8Seg at
      1000:0110 with INT 10h AX=1130 BH=3 [verified].

   2.7.  Scene 7 - stars and a sun with lens flare

      Seen: a starfield and a sun with a lens-flare effect.

      Sky_SunAndStars at 1000:425b and Flare_Render at 1000:4476,
      both called every frame of maybe_Scene_SurfaceFlyover's loop -
      effects/sky.txt. IT IS NOT A SEPARATE RUNNER. The stars and the
      sun are drawn throughout 2.5 to 2.9 and what reads as its own
      shot is the camera tilting up.

      The flare elements do sit along the line from the sun through
      screen centre, at multiples built from shifts and adds.

   2.8.  Scene 8 - the camera chases a starship

      Seen: the camera follows a starship, shaped much like
      XCURSION's, flying low over the surface.

      Candidate: the voxel surface of scene 5 plus a mesh. XCURSION
      replays a RECORDED flight track for exactly this kind of shot -
      ../../xcursion/effects/flight.txt - so a table of camera
      keyframes is worth looking for before assuming a flight model.

   2.9.  Scene 9 - the tower, with two ships circling

      Seen: the camera rises to a large tower structure with THREE
      RINGS, two gold starships flying around it. The planet surface
      is light blue or cyan, the tower purple.

      Candidate: the same voxel surface and mesh renderer as scenes 5
      and 8. The three rings say the tower is one mesh with repeated
      geometry. The model format is now known, and the largest model
      the script loads is 664 vertices and 1,323 faces, which is the
      only one big enough to be this - ../formats/mesh.txt section 4.

      THE CYAN-AND-PURPLE PAIRING NOW HAS A MECHANISM. One sphere map
      serves every surface, and a face attribute of 0 or 1 adds 0x40
      or 0xc0 to each texel, moving it into a different 64-entry band
      of the palette - ../subsystems/raster.txt section 4. So gold
      ships against a cyan surface with a purple tower is three bands
      of one palette, not three textures, and it is why the demo has
      only one Pal_Upload.

   2.10.  Scene 10 - bump-mapped morphing surface

      Seen: a bump-mapping effect whose surface morphs. Palette
      cycling runs through scenes 10 and 11.

      Candidate: the 0x80-filled buffer again, now read as a normal
      field. If scenes 1 and 10 share that buffer, they share a
      renderer, and the 64 KB fill at 1000:016b is the evidence that
      one such buffer exists at all.

   2.11.  Scene 11 - a rock breaks the surface, then a tunnel

      Seen: a rock enters and shatters the bump-mapped surface like
      glass; the rock then travels down a tunnel.

      Candidate: the tunnel is the strongest match in the whole
      sequence to something already understood. XCURSION builds a
      tunnel from two tables that together form one 16-bit texture
      index, and its depth illusion comes from the byte truncation -
      ../../xcursion/effects/tunnel.txt section 3.

   2.12.  Scene 12 - the battle, and the tower falls

      Seen: the planet surface returns with a battle - three enemy
      craft against two gold starships. The laser blasts and
      explosions look like XCURSION's. The tower is hit and topples
      over with a flame effect.

      SIX CRAFT IN TWO DESIGNS, three abreast each, plus six
      tetrahedral projectiles - effects/battle.txt section 6. Eight
      of the fourteen slots fly a recorded track of 300 entries;
      five of those fire. TWO ARE CAMERA RIGS, never rendered,
      orbiting in opposite directions and exchanged on every music
      position change.

      THE LASERS AND EXPLOSIONS ARE TRAIL BLOBS, not meshes:
      Trail_Draw at 1000:26a4 renders the 64-record ring as additive
      sprites, and maybe_Scene_TowerFall calls it at 1000:21cd -
      effects/trail.txt. That is a different mechanism from
      XCURSION's, which draws lasers as 8-slot meshes -
      ../../xcursion/effects/battle.txt.

      One bit of each particle's life counter decides whether it
      falls under gravity and burns its own colour into the top
      palette band, or drifts weightless and merely brightens what it
      crosses.

      The firing itself is the tail of Flight_Step: a tracer every
      eighth tick, and fifty particles where it meets the terrain -
      effects/trail.txt section 5. maybe_Scene_Battle resets EIGHT
      slots in its prologue and draws nine objects, the terrain, the
      sky and the flare in one loop.

      THE TOWER IS ONE MESH IN ONE SLOT AND IT TOPPLES WITH THE
      FLIGHT MODEL. Tick_TowerFall points Flight_StepRoll at it - a
      constant 0x40 of roll a tick with nothing easing it back -
      and winds its speed up as it goes, so there is no animation
      and there are no keyframes. It lands when its two horizontal
      coordinates reach a fixed grid cell, throws fifty burning
      particles, and after three impacts the camera starts shaking.
      effects/towerfall.txt.

   2.13.  Scene 13 - greetings, with trails

      The zoom is Image_BlitScaled at 1000:16d5, called from
      maybe_Scene_Greetings at 1000:146b - the SAME routine
      maybe_Scene_RockIntro uses for the LITHIUM title of 2.4.

      Seen: shout-outs to other groups, the text zooming in and out
      with trails behind it.

      Candidate [verified in part]: the roughly thirty group names
      from 1000:9149 onward, packed and NUL-terminated rather than
      padded like the credits above them - ../README.txt section 7.

      THE NAMES IN THAT TABLE ARE NOT ASCII. X means Y, W means X and
      Q means W, so MUSICPLAXER is MUSICPLAYER and QE APPRECIATE is WE
      APPRECIATE - ../README.txt section 7. Any group name containing
      W, X or Y is unreliable until the font's glyph list is read.

      Trails mean the previous frame is not cleared but faded or
      shifted, so this scene identifies itself by what it does NOT do
      to the framebuffer.

   2.14.  Scene 14 - the running chrome man

      Seen: the finale. A running man built out of chrome balls, who
      runs from the lower left towards the upper right and towards the
      camera.

      Candidate: the same sphere mapping as scenes 1 and 2 -
      ../subsystems/mesh3d.txt section 5 - on a set of balls whose
      positions come from an animation table. A walk cycle is
      keyframed, so there should be a table of joint or ball positions
      per frame, and its size will be obvious once found.

      Each ball would be one Mesh_Transform call with its own
      g_CurPos*, which costs nothing extra: the pools are shared and
      only grow, so a crowd of spheres is the cheap case for this
      pipeline.

3.  What the sequence already says about the code

   Three things fall out of the sequence without reading anything new.

   o SCENES SHARE RENDERERS, and the counting proves it. There are 15
     sites loading 0xa000 into AX [verified], and FIVE of them are
     inside Demo_Main itself - 1000:0248, 0279, 02a6, 0324 and 035d -
     where the script hands the framebuffer segment to 1000:2aa1. That
     leaves TEN inside other functions against FOURTEEN scenes, so at
     least some scenes reuse a renderer. The voxel surface appearing in
     scenes 5, 8, 9 and 12 is the obvious case.

   o THE PALETTE IS ONE ROUTINE, NOT SEVEN. XCURSION has seven palette
     builders; Lithium has a single OUT 0x3c8 site, inside Pal_Upload
     [verified]. So every colour in every scene above comes from one
     uploader reading g_PalSource with a g_PalFade bias, and the
     per-scene colour differences are differences in g_PalSource. That
     makes the cyan-and-purple of scene 9 and the cycling of scenes 1
     to 3 and 10 to 11 a question about what writes g_PalSource, which
     is a much smaller question than XCURSION's.

   o THE TEXT SCENES ARE ONE TABLE. Scenes 3, 4, 6 and 13 all draw
     strings from g_CreditTable at 1000:90a1
     [verified that the strings are there]. Four scenes, one data
     structure, and decoding its glyph order unblocks all four.

4.  What to read next

   The ENGINE is read: the timeline, the 3D pipeline, the rasteriser,
   the texture and palette generators, the terrain, the recorded
   tracks and the music replayer all have documents. WHAT IS LEFT IS
   THE SIX SCENES WITH NO MECHANISM AT ALL - section 5 says which.

   In this order, because each answer makes the next cheaper.

   1. THE TEXT RENDERER. Four scenes need it - 2.3, 2.4, 2.6 and 2.13
      - and all four draw from g_CreditTable, so one routine unblocks
      them. The ROM 8x8 font pointer Demo_Main captures into
      g_RomFont8x8{Off,Seg} is where to start.

   2. EVERY ROUTINE THE SCRIPT CALLS IS NOW NAMED. 1000:040b is
      maybe_Scene_Tunnel, 1000:10f7 is maybe_Scene_Finale,
      1000:25a0 is maybe_Scene_Approach and 1000:5526 is
      Tex_RenderObjectTile, which renders three objects flat into a
      128 x 128 texture and shows the viewer nothing.

      What remains open about them is WHICH SCENE each serves, not
      what each does.

   3. The remaining texture segments. 0x6ad2 is generated by
      Tex_BuildSphereMap. 0x5ad2 holds whatever picture is current -
      the title image, a radial texture, or a snapshotted frame -
      and 0x7ad2 is the bump map's height field, but
      maybe_Flare_Occlusion reads 0x7ad2 too, in a scene with no bump
      map. subsystems/texture.txt section 6, effects/bump.txt,
      effects/sky.txt.

   4. The glyph list behind g_CreditTable, which decodes the
      substitution ../README.txt section 7 describes.

5.  The running order, and which runner is which scene

   DEMO_MAIN IS A LINEAR SCRIPT AND ITS CALL ORDER IS THE RUNNING
   ORDER [verified by disassembly]. Only three music waits are at the
   top level; every other runner waits on the music itself.

       01fe  maybe_Scene_RockIntro
       0208  Font_Unpack
       0245  Tex_RenderObjectTile
       025f  Demo_RunUntil(8)
       0262  maybe_Scene_SurfaceFlyover
       0297  FUN_1000_503a                 a mesh load, see below
       029a  FUN_1000_5334
       029d  Tex_BuildThreeGlows
       02a0  maybe_Scene_BumpShatter
       02a3  maybe_Scene_Tunnel
       02bd  FUN_1000_503a                 a second mesh load
       02c0  FUN_1000_5334
       02c3  Tex_BuildGlowNoise
       02c6  Height_BuildCone
       02da  Tex_RenderObjectTile          a second call
       02dd  Tex_SnapshotFrame
       02ea  Demo_RunUntil(0x1f)
       030b  Scene_Shatter
       031e  Demo_RunUntil(0x20)
       0321  maybe_Scene_Approach
       0336  maybe_Scene_Battle
       0350  maybe_Scene_TowerFall
       035a  FUN_1000_1396
       03d1  FUN_1000_10f7
       03ed  FUN_1000_70c8                 the shutdown path

   FOUR OF THE SCRIPT'S CALLS DRAW NOTHING. 1000:029d is
   Tex_BuildThreeGlows, 1000:02c3 is Tex_BuildGlowNoise and 1000:02c6
   is Height_BuildCone - they fill the texture buffer and the bump
   map's height field - and 1000:02dd is Tex_SnapshotFrame, which
   renders one frame into the same buffer. subsystems/texture.txt
   section 6. So the gap between the credit roll and the shatter holds
   fewer scenes than its call count suggests.

   1000:0665 DRAWS TEXT AND PARTICLES, NOT A ROCK AND A TUNNEL. Its
   head renders the string "VOTE" into the dust mask and seeds the
   particle pool from it - effects/dust.txt section 5 - and its body
   drives a shatter. Which scene that is remains open: it runs at
   0x02a0, directly after the credit roll, and no scene in section 2
   is described as text or particles.

   1000:150b COVERS THE TITLES AS WELL AS THE ROCK. It calls
   Image_UnpackRle, which expands the full-screen logo "Vista
   Lithium" out of the asset stream [verified by decoding it] - so
   2.3 and 2.4, the VISTA and LITHIUM titles, belong to this runner
   rather than to a runner of their own. effects/text.txt section 5.

   THE ORDER OUTRANKS A MESH LOAD AS EVIDENCE. 1000:21eb is the only
   runner that loads the 664-vertex tower, which suggests the shot
   that introduces it - but it runs AFTER the battle, so it is the
   tower being destroyed. Its tick handler cues a white flash off a
   track position, which fits.

   +---------------+----------------------------+--------------------+
   | Runner        | Named                      | Scene              |
   +===============+============================+====================+
   | 1000:150b     | maybe_Scene_RockIntro      | 2.2, 2.3 and 2.4   |
   | 1000:18a3     | maybe_Scene_SurfaceFlyover | 2.5 to 2.9         |
   | 1000:0665     | maybe_Scene_BumpShatter    | 2.10 and 2.11      |
   | 1000:2496     | Tex_SnapshotFrame          | not a scene        |
   | 1000:1c4f     | maybe_Scene_Battle         | 2.12               |
   | 1000:21eb     | maybe_Scene_TowerFall      | 2.12, the tower    |
   |               |                            | falling            |
   | 1000:040b     | maybe_Scene_Tunnel         | 2.11, the tunnel   |
   | 1000:0c59     | Font_Unpack                | not a scene        |
   | 1000:10f7     | maybe_Scene_Finale         | 2.14               |
   | 1000:1396     | maybe_Scene_Greetings      | 2.13               |
   | 1000:2919     | Scene_Shatter              | 2.11, the glass    |
   | 1000:25a0     | -                          | a ship, no terrain |
   | 1000:5526     | -                          | three chrome       |
   |               |                            | objects, called    |
   |               |                            | TWICE              |
   +---------------+----------------------------+--------------------+

   1000:1c4f is the battle on two independent counts: it is the only
   runner that loads the four-vertex bolt, and its tick handler is the
   block with eight consecutive Track_Play calls.

   A TEXT SCENE NEVER CALLS Scene_ProjectVerts, so 2.3, 2.4, 2.6 and
   2.13 are driven from runners that are not in the ten-caller set at
   all. The greetings of 2.13 come from 1000:13db, which sets
   g_CreditPtr and installs Tick_Greetings - text.txt section 4.

   1000:1396 is the greetings on a direct reading: 1000:13db sits
   inside it and is the only site that sets g_CreditPtr and installs
   Tick_Greetings - text.txt section 4.

   1000:10f7 is the last runner Demo_Main calls before Snd_Shutdown,
   and Demo_Main loads FOUR MORPH KEYFRAMES of a 10-vertex shape
   immediately before it - ../formats/mesh.txt section 4.1.

   EVERY SCENE NOW HAS A MECHANISM. The last one without was 2.7,
   and it turned out not to need a runner of its own - effects/sky.txt.

   The lesson the scene list keeps teaching is that A RUNNER IS NOT A
   SCENE. 1000:150b covers four of them, 1000:18a3 covers five, and
   1000:0665 covers two across four phases, while four of the script's
   calls draw nothing at all.

6.  Open questions

   o The scene count. Fourteen scenes are described above and the user
     watching counted the same run once. A second viewing may split or
     merge some, and the boundaries between scenes 1 to 3 and between
     10 and 11 are the least certain because the transitions are
     continuous.

   o Whether the chrome of scenes 1, 2 and 14 is one technique or
     three. "Chrome" is a look. An environment map, a phong lookup and
     a plain reflection table all produce it.

   o Whether scene 11's tunnel is entered by the rock or merely
     cross-faded to. The description says the rock goes through it,
     which would make the two one scene with a shared camera.

   o How the tower topples.

   o Where the demo's own timing comes from. XCURSION ends every part
     on a music order position and has no retrace wait; Lithium has no
     MOV DX,0x3da site at all [verified], which is consistent but not
     proof - ../README.txt section 5.
