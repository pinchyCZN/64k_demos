PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                       LITHIUM: THE FLIGHT MODEL


Abstract

   What turns a recorded mouse path into a flying craft. Flight_Step
   at 1000:59b7 runs once per slot per tick, immediately after every
   Track_Play, in all three track handlers.

   IT IS NOT ONLY THE FLIGHT MODEL. Its tail, from 1000:5dc0, is the
   WEAPON: it fires a tracer every eighth tick and spawns fifty
   explosion particles where the tracer meets the terrain -
   effects/trail.txt section 5.

   TWO THINGS ABOUT IT ARE WORTH KNOWING BEFORE THE DETAIL. Roll is
   never recorded - it is derived from the yaw rate and levels itself.
   And the track's two-bit packed field is a THROTTLE, which is the
   whole of what the author's keyboard contributed to a recording
   besides the mouse.

   All [verified] from Flight_Step unless marked.
   tracks.txt is the recording; objects.txt is the parameter bank.

1.  Flight_Step

   SI is the slot times four.

       ax = g_SlotPitchAcc - g_SlotPitch         the pitch error
       bx = g_SlotYawAcc   - g_SlotYaw           the yaw error
       ax >>= 5;  bx >>= 5                       eased by a 32nd
       g_SlotRoll  -= bx
       g_SlotRoll  -= bx
       g_SlotYaw   += bx
       g_SlotPitch += ax
       bx += bx;  g_SlotRoll -= bx
       g_SlotRoll += (g_SlotRollRest - g_SlotRoll) >> 3

       if g_SlotThrottle == 1:
           rotate (0, 0, g_SlotSpeed) by pitch, then by yaw
           >>= 7 and add to g_SlotFlight{X,Y,Z}
       if g_SlotThrottle == 2:
           the same with the speed negated

   THE TRACK RECORDS RATES AND THIS TURNS THEM INTO FLIGHT.
   Track_Play accumulates the recorded deltas into g_SlotPitchAcc and
   g_SlotYawAcc; the attitude then chases those accumulators at a
   thirty-second per tick, which is what makes a hand-flown mouse path
   come out smooth rather than jittery.

   The easing is the same shift-and-add idiom that appears everywhere
   in this corpus - the palette fades, g_FocusEased, the cross-fade.

2.  Roll comes free

   g_SlotRoll is never written by the recording. Flight_Step
   subtracts the eased yaw rate from it FOUR TIMES - twice, then twice
   more via bx += bx - and then eases it back toward g_SlotRollRest by
   an eighth.

   SO A TURN BANKS AND LEVELS ITSELF WITH NO INPUT. Four times the
   yaw rate is a hard bank, and the pull back toward the rest angle is
   what stops it winding up.

   XCURSION does the same trick the other way round, deriving yaw from
   roll - ../../xcursion/effects/flight.txt. Both demos get banked
   turns out of one axis of input.

3.  The throttle

   g_SlotThrottle at 1000:8db0 holds the two-bit field Track_Play
   unpacks from byte 2 of each track entry:

       0    coast - the attitude still changes, the position does not
       1    forward
       2    reverse

   THAT IS WHAT THE RECORDED KEYBOARD WAS FOR. tracks.txt records the
   six-bit key index and this two-bit mode as unexplained; this is the
   consumer of the two-bit half.

   The speed itself is g_SlotSpeed at 1000:8e46, which Obj_Reset sets
   to 0xa, so a slot coasts at a fixed rate and the recording only
   says whether it is moving.

4.  Vec_Rotate3_2

   1000:3799, a full three-axis Euler rotation on the SECOND angle
   set: Z by g_RotAngle2Z, then X by g_RotAngle2X, then Y by
   g_RotAngle2Y.

   Each stage is the same g_SinTable sin/cos pair the single-axis
   helpers use, but with SAR 6 on every product and another SAR 6
   after each stage - so unlike Vec_RotZ, which leaves its products at
   full scale for the caller to shift, this one stays in range as it
   goes. That is what lets Flight_Step hand it a velocity and get a
   usable vector back with no shifting of its own.

   Flight_Step calls it twice, setting one angle and zeroing the other
   two each time, so the velocity is rotated by pitch and then by yaw.

5.  Flight_StepRoll, the other integrator

   1000:61de. The same idea as Flight_Step's throttle section, with
   two differences that matter:

       Flight_Step      -> g_SlotFlight{X,Y,Z},  SAR 7
       Flight_StepRoll  -> g_SlotHome{X,Y,Z},    SAR 0xb

   SAR 0xb is a sixteenth of SAR 7, so this one creeps. And it adds
   0x40 to the slot's roll every tick with nothing easing it back - a
   full revolution every 32 ticks, which is a constant barrel roll
   rather than a banked turn.

   THAT IS WHY IT EXISTS. Tick_TowerFall points it at the tower's
   slot, where a constant rotation about one axis is a building
   turning over - effects/towerfall.txt section 3. Tick_PlayEight
   also calls it.

   IT ALSO CLOSES THE LINK BETWEEN FLYING AND BEING DRAWN.
   Obj_LoadOrientA and B copy g_SlotHome* into g_SlotPos*, and
   Obj_Select copies those into what Mesh_Transform reads, so a slot
   driven by this routine reaches the screen.

6.  The link to the drawn position and the drawn orientation

   The last three lines of Flight_Step, at 1000:5d59:

       g_SlotHomeY += (g_SlotFlightY - g_SlotHomeY) >> 5
       g_SlotHomeX += (g_SlotFlightX - g_SlotHomeX) >> 5
       g_SlotHomeZ += (g_SlotFlightZ - g_SlotHomeZ) >> 5

   THAT IS THE WHOLE CONNECTION between flying and being drawn.
   Flight_Step's throttle integrates into g_SlotFlight*; these ease
   g_SlotHome* after it by a 32nd; Obj_LoadOrientA and B copy Home
   into g_SlotPos*; Obj_Select hands those to Mesh_Transform.

   THE LAG IS DELIBERATE. The drawn craft trails its own flight
   position, so a hard turn makes the model swing wide and settle.

   AND THE ORIENTATION TRAILS THE SAME WAY, four times faster -
   1000:5d9b, immediately after:

       g_SlotAngleAX += (g_SlotPitch - g_SlotAngleAX) >> 2
       g_SlotAngleAY += (g_SlotYaw   - g_SlotAngleAY) >> 2
       g_SlotAngleAZ += ((g_SlotRoll - g_SlotAngleAZ) >> 2)
                         >> g_RollEaseShift

   THAT IS WHAT TURNS THE CAMERA, and it is easy to miss because the
   flight model writes the attitude into SET B while Obj_LoadOrientA
   hands SET A to Mesh_Transform. Nothing else writes set A for a
   flying slot, so leaving this out gives a camera that follows its
   recorded path while barely rotating - which looks like a wrong
   flight model and is not one.

   ROLL TAKES AN EXTRA SHIFT from g_RollEaseShift at 1000:9026. The
   flyover sets it to 2 at 1000:18a9 and clears it on the way out at
   1000:1bd8, so the bank follows at a sixteenth there where pitch
   and yaw follow at a quarter.

   Two angles are eased the same way a quarter at a time, and just
   before them comes a GROUND CLAMP:

       if g_RecordMode == 0:
           eax = Terrain_HeightAtSlot
           if g_SlotFlightY < eax:  g_SlotFlightY = eax

   It is SKIPPED WHILE RECORDING, so the author could fly through
   terrain laying down a track and the replay would still ride the
   surface.

6.1.  Measured against the emulator

   THE THREE ADDRESSES COMMAND 0x18 ADDS ITS OFFSET TO ARE SLOT 4'S
   FLIGHT POSITION. cs:[0x895d], cs:[0x8c6d] and cs:[0x8995] are
   g_SlotFlightX, Y and Z plus four, so the command positions itself
   relative to the lead ship rather than to the world -
   ../subsystems/objects.txt is the bank, and every array in it is
   indexed by slot times four.


   Two dumps of the whole parameter bank at consecutive entries to
   Tick_SurfaceFlyover. Slot 0 is the only one flying
   there - both ships coast with throttle 0 - and every field it
   touches comes out of the transcription exactly [verified]:

   +------------------+----------+---------+-----------+
   | field            | before   | demo    | the port  |
   +==================+==========+=========+===========+
   | pitch            | 11       | 12      | 12        |
   | yaw              | -65      | -70     | -70       |
   | roll             | 129      | 130     | 130       |
   | flight x delta   |          | +14     | +14       |
   | flight z delta   |          | +287    | +287      |
   | flight y         | 4160     | 4160    | 4160      |
   +------------------+----------+---------+-----------+

   The roll is the telling one: it is derived four times over from the
   eased yaw rate and then pulled back an eighth, and 129 to 130 only
   comes out right if all five steps are in the right order.

   flight y holds still because the ground clamp pins it.
   Terrain_HeightAtSlot at 1000:4107 indexes the map the way
   Terrain_Height does, but it NEGATES the byte about sea level and
   shifts left 7 with a bias of 0x40 rather than shifting left 3 -
   object space runs the other way up and sixteen times coarser. The
   live byte under the camera is 0x60, and -(0x60 - 0x80) << 7 plus
   0x40 is 4160 exactly [verified].

6.2.  The command chain, and the chase cam

   Flight_Step TESTS THE TRACK'S SIX-BIT FIELD THIRTEEN TIMES,
   between the throttle at 1000:59fe and the ground clamp at
   1000:5d40: commands 1 to 8, 0xa, 0x17, 0x18, 0x19 and 0x1a, each
   an independent compare that falls through to the next
   [verified]. It is a small flight-control vocabulary, which is what
   the recorded keyboard was for - ./tracks.txt.

   WHICH ONES THE THREE TRACKS ACTUALLY USE, counted over their first
   400 records [verified, decoded from the asset segment]:

   +----------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | camera, slot 0 | 0x18 ninety-nine times, 3 fifty-four, 2    |
   |                | six, and 0xe, 0xf, 0x10 and 0x17 once each |
   | ship, slot 4   | 6 forty-one times, 8 five times            |
   | ship, slot 0xc | 0xb twice, 0xd, 0x13 and 0x15 once each    |
   +----------------+--------------------------------------------+

   THE REST OF THE VOCABULARY, all of it short [verified]:

   +-----------+------------------------------------------------+
   | 1         | raises 1000:8f63, the flyover's exit flag      |
   | 2 and 3   | strafe right and left, roll +4 and -4          |
   | 4 and 5   | flight y plus or minus speed << 4              |
   | 6 and 7   | g_SlotRollRest minus or plus 0x20              |
   | 8         | level g_SlotRollRest to the nearest 0x2000,    |
   |           | eased a sixteenth, snapping when the step is 0 |
   | 0xa..0x17 | SET THE SPEED to ((cmd - 9) * 2) - 1, so 1 to  |
   |           | 27 in fourteen steps                           |
   | 0x19      | g_SlotFiring = 1                               |
   +-----------+------------------------------------------------+

   SO COMMAND 1 IS HOW THE SCENE ENDS. The flyover's loop at
   1000:1bce leaves when 1000:8f63 is set, and nothing else in the
   runner raises it - the shot's length is in its own camera track,
   not on a clock or an order boundary.

   SO THE SHOT IS A CHASE AND COMMAND 0x18 IS WHAT MAKES IT ONE.
   1000:5c64:

       rotate (0, 0, -0x60) by this slot's pitch, then by its yaw
       >> 6, and ADD SLOT 4'S FLIGHT POSITION
       store that as this slot's flight position

   It does not integrate, it OVERWRITES - so for a quarter of its
   records the camera is pinned 96 units behind the lead ship along
   its own facing. A port that only integrates the throttle drifts
   away from the recorded path within seconds.

   THE TRAIL DISTANCE IS 0x60 AND IT IS WRITTEN 0xffffffa0.
   1000:5c89 is MOV ECX, 0xffffffa0, which is the byte 0xa0 sign
   extended - value -0x60, not -0xa0. Read at face value the camera
   sits five thirds too far back, and nothing in the flyover reaches
   command 0x18 until a thousand ticks in, so the symptom is a
   camera that holds the recorded path for most of the scene and
   then slides off it mid-shot. The rotated offset is verified
   against EAX, EBX and ECX at 1000:5cad, which hold
   (303168, 32448, -249408) before the three shifts.

   Command 3, the camera's other frequent one, is a strafe:
   roll -= 4, then (-speed, 0, 0) rotated by pitch and yaw, >> 7, and
   ADDED to the flight position. Command 0x19 just sets g_SlotFiring.

   THE CLAMP READS THE GENERATED MAP AND NOTHING ELSE WILL DO. The
   asset segment holds zeros where the heightmap goes until
   Terrain_Generate fills it - ./terrain.txt section 5 - and a zero
   byte pins Terrain_HeightAtSlot at -16320, far below any real
   ground, so the camera sinks straight through the surface. That is
   the whole of a bug that looked like bad flight integration.

   WITH THE CHAIN AND THE RIGHT MAP IN PLACE THE CAMERA IS EXACT.
   Compared against the emulator at a point matched on BOTH the track
   pointer and g_SlotTrackTick - 40 records in and one tick past the
   last - every field agrees: pitch 209, yaw -1381, roll -33,
   pitch_acc -128, yaw_acc -1344, speed 10, roll_rest 2 and the
   flight position (188958, -8912, 86688) [verified].

   AND IT HOLDS DEEP INTO THE SHOT. At flyover tick 485, with the
   camera swung round to yaw -2709 and roll 987, all sixteen compared
   fields agree exactly - both angle sets, both positions, the
   accumulators, the speed and the roll rest [verified].

   COMPARE WHAT IS DRAWN, NOT ONLY WHAT IS COMPUTED. An earlier pass
   checked the attitude, the accumulators and the flight position and
   found them exact while the set-A easing was still missing
   altogether - so the numbers agreed and the camera on screen did
   not turn. Set A is what reaches Mesh_Transform and it has to be in
   the comparison.

   MATCHING THE RECORD IS NOT ENOUGH TO COMPARE. A record lasts six
   ticks, so two states can share a track pointer and still be up to
   five ticks apart, and the attitude eases a thirty-second per tick
   throughout. Comparing on the record alone shows a few per cent of
   apparent error in pitch, yaw and position that is nothing but that
   offset; g_SlotTrackTick has to match too.

7.  Open questions

   o Throttle value 3 is untested by the routine, so it coasts like 0.

   o Command 0x1a, at 1000:5cdd, which loads DI with 0x10 and is the
     only one of the thirteen not transcribed. No track seen so far
     uses it.

   o Whether any track uses commands 0x04, 0x09 or 0x1a. The eight
     battle tracks use none of them - effects/battle.txt section 3 -
     and the other scenes' tracks have not been decoded.
