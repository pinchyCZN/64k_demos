PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                 LITHIUM: THE STARS, SUN AND FLARE


Abstract

   Scene 2.7 of effects/scenes.txt. It is NOT A SCENE RUNNER: the
   stars, the sun and the flare are drawn every frame of
   maybe_Scene_SurfaceFlyover's one loop, and what the viewer reads as
   a separate shot is the camera tilting up.

   THE STARFIELD COSTS NOTHING TO STORE. 1024 stars are regenerated
   every frame from a PRNG reseeded with two fixed constants, so the
   same positions come out each time. Held as coordinates they would
   be 6 KB of a 64 KB intro.

   THE OCCLUSION TEST IS ONE PIXEL. The sun is projected, the back
   buffer is read at that exact address, and the flare is drawn only
   if the pixel still holds the background fill.

   All [verified] from the routines unless marked.

1.  The routines

   The first three are called once a frame, in this order, from
   maybe_Scene_SurfaceFlyover at 1000:1b50, 1b53 and 1b56.

   THE FIRST OF THEM NEVER RUNS. Both of maybe_Sky_DrawPoint's call
   sites gate on g_SkyPointsOn at 1000:8f5a - the flyover at
   1000:1b48 and maybe_Scene_Battle at 1000:21bd - and nothing in the
   image writes that byte. It ships as 0 and reads 0 in the emulator
   throughout the battle, so the routine is dead code and the port
   leaves it out [verified].

       Sky_SunAndStars        1000:425b   the sun, then 1024 stars

2.  The sun is a direction

   Sky_SunAndStars starts from a CONSTANT VECTOR:

       eax, ebx, ecx = (-0x800, -0x12c, -0x400)
       rotate by g_SlotAngleY, then g_SlotAngleX, then g_SlotAngleZ,
           SAR 9 after each
       return if ecx < 0x3e8
       perspective divide, + 0xa0 and + 0x64
       g_SunScreenX, g_SunScreenY = the result

   IT IS NEVER TRANSLATED BY THE CAMERA POSITION, only rotated - so
   the sun sits at infinity and does not drift as the camera flies.
   That is the whole of the sky model.

   g_SunScreenX and g_SunScreenY are set to 0xff80 first, which is the
   mark Flare_Render tests to decide the sun is not on screen.

3.  The occlusion test

   Still inside Sky_SunAndStars, after the projection:

       clamp the position to the screen
       bx = y*320 + x
       DS = 0x4ad2
       g_SunVisible = ([bx] == 0x80) ? 0 : 0x8000

   SO 0x8000 MEANS BLOCKED and 0 means clear.

   0x80 IS THE BACK BUFFER'S FILL VALUE - Tex_SnapshotFrame and
   1000:5526 both fill 0x4ad2 with 0x80808080 - so a pixel still
   holding it means nothing has been drawn over the sun this frame.

   ONE BYTE READ IS THE ENTIRE VISIBILITY QUERY. There is no depth
   buffer in the demo and none is needed: the terrain and the ships
   are drawn before this runs, so their pixels are already present
   wherever they cover the sun.

   THE RESULT IS NOT USED DIRECTLY. Tick_TowerFall eases
   g_FlareEnergy toward it by a quarter each tick -
   effects/towerfall.txt section 5 - and Flare_Render gates on that,
   so a sun sliding behind a ridge dims the flare over several frames
   instead of switching it off.

4.  The free starfield

       g_RndState0, g_RndState1 = 0xcd57, 0xb6b5
       cx = 0x400
       per star:
           cs:[0x8f59] = Rnd_Next                  a shade
           ecx, ebx, eax = three Rnd_Next, sign-extended to 32 bits
           ebx -= 0x5000
           eax >>= 4;  ebx >>= 5;  ecx >>= 4
           rotate by the same three angles, SAR 9 after each
           skip if ecx < 1
           perspective divide

   RESEEDING WITH A CONSTANT IS WHAT MAKES THIS WORK. The generator
   is deterministic, so the same 1024 positions are produced every
   frame and the starfield holds still while the camera turns.

   The cost is zero bytes. The price is that the layout cannot be
   authored - it is whatever Rnd_Next yields from 0xcd57, 0xb6b5 -
   and that the stars must be re-projected every frame rather than
   stored once.

   ebx is biased by -0x5000 and shifted one bit further than the
   other two axes, so the field is flattened and pushed below the
   camera rather than being an even cube.

5.  The flare

   Flare_Render, once the sun is on screen and g_FlareEnergy is
   below 0x7f00 - that is, the sun is not yet fully blocked:

       g_FlareColour = g_FlareBrightness;  g_FlareSize = 0x50
       Flare_DrawSun
       ax = 0xa0 - g_SunScreenX
       bx = 0x64 - g_SunScreenY

   (ax, bx) IS THE VECTOR FROM THE SUN TO THE CENTRE OF THE SCREEN,
   and every element sits at a multiple of it. THIRTEEN of them, in
   this order [verified from the call sites]:

   +----+-----------+--------+----------------------+-------------+
   | #  | Call site | Colour | Multiple of (ax,bx)  | Sun at      |
   |    |           |        |                      | (110,130)   |
   +====+===========+========+======================+=============+
   | 1  | 1000:4517 | 0x01   | 2 + 1/4 + 1/8        | (228,  58)  |
   | 2  | 1000:4537 | 0x80   | 2                    | (210,  70)  |
   | 3  | 1000:456b | 0xc0   | -1/2 + 1/8 + 1/32    | ( 92, 140)  |
   | 4  | 1000:458b | 0xac   | 1/2                  | (135, 115)  |
   | 5  | 1000:45bb | 0x9c   | 1 + 1/2 - 1/8        | (179,  89)  |
   | 6  | 1000:45cd | 0xe4   | 1 + 1/2 - 1/8        | (179,  89)  |
   | 7  | 1000:45f7 | 0xd6   | 1/2 + 1/16           | (138, 113)  |
   | 8  | 1000:462d | 0xce   | 1 + 1/2 - 1/8 - 1/16 | (176,  91)  |
   | 9  | 1000:4657 | 0xe4   | 1/2 - 1/16           | (132, 117)  |
   | 10 | 1000:467f | 0xe4   | 1/2 + 1/4            | (147, 107)  |
   | 11 | 1000:4697 | 0xf6   | 1                    | (160, 100)  |
   | 12 | 1000:46c3 | 0xf0   | 1 + 1/8 + 1/32       | (167,  95)  |
   | 13 | 1000:46ed | 0xdc   | 1 + 1/2 + 1/8        | (191,  81)  |
   +----+-----------+--------+----------------------+-------------+

   EVERY MULTIPLE IS SHIFTS AND ADDS. There is no multiply and no
   table of offsets - each element's position is open-coded, which is
   why the routine is 0x27d bytes of straight line rather than a loop
   over a table.

   THE SHIFTS COMPOUND WITHIN A BLOCK. Each element restores the
   vector from the stack, but the SARs inside one block apply to the
   running value, so a block's second shift sees the first one's
   result - reading them as independent fractions of the vector gets
   every multiple wrong. Element 1 is 2 + 1/4 + 1/8 and not
   2 + 1/8 + 1/16 for exactly that reason, the 0x44f8 shift acting on
   the already doubled value.

   THE SHIFTS ARE ARITHMETIC, so a negative component rounds away
   from zero: -15 >> 2 is -4. Element 13 lands on (191,81) rather
   than (191,82) because of it.

   THE NEGATIVE MULTIPLE puts element 3 on the far side of the sun
   from the centre, which is what stops the flare reading as a
   one-sided smear. ELEMENT 11 IS THE SCREEN CENTRE exactly, being
   the whole vector.

   ELEMENTS 5 AND 6 SHARE A POSITION - 1000:45ac pushes it and
   1000:45be takes it back - drawn one after the other in different
   colours, which is a ring inside a ring. 9 and 10 only share a
   colour.

   Flare_Render sets g_FlareSize to 0x50 before the sun, but each
   element recomputes it from its own colour - section 6.1 - so they
   differ in size as well as in colour and position.

   AN ELEMENT IS A RING, NOT A DISC. Flare_DrawElement draws only
   where the sprite lies within 0x20 ABOVE the threshold, and the
   strength is ((0x20 - that) & 0x1f) >> 2 - brightest one step above
   it and gone 0x1f above. The cone falls off with radius, so that
   band is an annulus whose bright edge is its outside. Only the sun's
   own disc is filled.

6.  The additive blit

   This is the demo's ONLY additive write, and it is not a span
   routine - which is why it is absent from the five g_SpanRoutine
   values of subsystems/raster.txt.

   Flare_DrawSun at 1000:4754, per pixel:

       al = ES:[si] - g_FlareColour
       skip the pixel if that borrowed or came out zero
       ax = (-ax) & 0xff                     0x100 minus the excess
       dh = ES:[si + 0x4600]                 the ANGLE plane, 6.3
       di = g_FlickerTable[dh] - ax + 0x40,  floored at 0
       ax = di + g_FlareRamp[ax]             NOT [di] - see below
       clamp to 0xff, then >>= 2             0 .. 0x3f
       ah = [bx]                             THE DESTINATION PIXEL
       dh = (ah & 0x3f) + al
       if dh >= 0x40:  dh = 0x3f             SATURATE
       [bx] = dh | (ah & 0xc0)               restore the band

   THE TOP TWO BITS ARE PRESERVED AND THE LOW SIX ARE ADDED TO. A
   pixel here is a 6-bit index inside a 64-entry band chosen by its
   high bits - subsystems/raster.txt section 4 - so the flare
   brightens whatever it covers without changing that pixel's colour,
   and saturating at 0x3f is what stops it carrying into the next
   band and flipping the colour outright.

   g_FlareRamp IS NOT INDEXED BY THE FLICKERED VALUE. 1000:47b0 is an
   XCHG, so the two swap: the ramp is read at 0x100 - (sprite -
   colour), the sprite's own height above the threshold, and the
   flickered index is only the base it is added to. Reading the
   paraphrase as ramp[di] gives a plausible picture and the wrong
   pixels.

   THE SHIMMER IS g_FlickerTable, the smoothed random walk
   Flicker_Step maintains - effects/flicker.txt - indexed through the
   angle plane of section 6.3.

   Flare_DrawElement at 1000:47f5 does the same thing at 0 to 7
   instead of 0 to 0x3f, so an element tints by at most an eighth of
   a band and only the sun's own disc really glares.

   6.1.  One sprite, every size

      Flare_ClipSetup at 1000:49df:

          bp = 0x50 - g_FlareSize
          si = bp + (bp << 5) + (bp << 7)       ie. bp * 161

      bp*161 is bp*160 + bp - A ROW AND A COLUMN of a 160-wide
      buffer - so the source starts at (0x50-size, 0x50-size), the
      centred sub-square of side 2*size inside Flare_BuildSprite's
      160 x 160 cone.

      THERE IS NO SCALER ANYWHERE. The cone is a linear ramp from its
      centre, so a tighter crop is a smaller disc that is correctly
      shaped and brighter at its rim. Flare_DrawElement sets
      g_FlareSize from its own brightness and gets the right sprite
      for free.

   6.2.  Looking at the sun washes the screen out

      Sun_GlareFade at 1000:46f3, called before anything is drawn:

          if the sun is off screen:  g_PalFadeTarget2 = 0;  return
          bx = ((0x104 - g_SunScreenY) >> 1) * 160
             + (g_SunScreenX >> 1)
          cl = ES:[bx]
          g_PalFadeTarget2 = ((0x8200 - g_FlareEnergy) >> 3)
                             / (0x104 - cl)

      IT WRITES THE GLOBAL FADE TARGET. Tick_Default eases g_PalFade
      toward it and uploads the palette, so the whole screen bleaches
      when the sun is high and recovers when the camera looks away.
      Nothing in the fade path is specific to the sun; only the
      target is.

      The halved coordinates address the same 160-wide buffer the
      sprite lives in.

   6.3.  Flare_BuildSprite: the cone, the ramp, the angle plane

      1000:5363, run once in the transition, filling segment 0x7ad2.

      THE CONE, 1000:5378. A 160 x 160 byte image, 251 at the centre
      falling linearly to 0 at the rim:

          eax = sq[|x-0x50|] + sq[|y-0x50|],  then << 2
          si  = Math_Sqrt(eax)                A BYTE OFFSET
          v   = 0xff - si/4 - si/8 - si/64 - si/512, floored at 0

      The square table is the one Math_BuildSquareTable leaves at
      1000:c8b2 and Math_Sqrt returns the byte offset of the nearest
      entry, so si is about 8r - the four shifts start at 2 and not at
      0 for that reason.

      THE RAMP, 1000:53e3. 256 words at g_FlareRamp:

          ax = 0x1194 / (k + 1) - 0x11
          if |(0x100-k) - 0xb0| < 4:  ax += (4 - that) * 8
          if ax > 0xff:  AL = 0xff

      THE LAST CLAMP WRITES AL ALONE, so an entry whose quotient
      passed 0xff keeps its high byte: entry 0 is 0x11ff, entry 1
      0x08ff. It is invisible because Flare_DrawSun clamps the whole
      word, but a transcription that clamps here is not the same
      table. The bump peaks at entry 0x50, 0x46 against its
      neighbours' 0x3f and 0x3d.

      THE ANGLE PLANE, 1000:541f. 512 wedges from (160,100) out to
      radius 256, each a 512th of a turn, filled through Poly_Fill
      with a ONE-BYTE texture - 1000:5446 writes the single texel the
      zeroed u and v will read, so a textured filler does a flat
      fill. 1000:54df then copies 160 x 64 from (80,68) to 0x6400.

      SO THE PLANE IS AN ANGLE MAP, and the 0x4600 that Flare_DrawSun
      adds is exact: the sprite's centre byte 0x3250 plus 0x4600 is
      0x7850, which is the sweep's centre byte. The flicker table is
      therefore indexed by ANGLE AROUND THE SUN, and Flicker_Step
      closes its table into a ring - effects/flicker.txt - precisely
      because an angle wraps.

      THE VALUE IS THE COUNTER'S LOW BYTE against 512 wedges, so the
      ramp runs 0 to 255 TWICE around the circle and the shimmer has
      two-fold symmetry [verified by sampling the demo's own buffer].

      THE SUN'S OWN SPRITE READS OUTSIDE THE PLANE. At size 0x50 it
      spans all 160 rows while the plane covers only rows 48 to 111,
      so its top rows index the cone as though it were an angle and
      its bottom rows read the zeros past 0x8c00.

   6.4.  The shimmer is not frame-deterministic

      Flicker_Step runs from the TICK, which is the timer interrupt,
      while Flare_DrawSun reads the table from the frame. The two are
      asynchronous: dumping g_FlickerTable either side of one call to
      Flare_DrawSun shows 214 of its 256 entries changed [observed].

      SO THE SUN'S DISC IS DRAWN PARTLY WITH ONE TABLE AND PARTLY
      WITH THE NEXT, and the same frame rendered twice does not come
      out the same. Any byte comparison of the sun has to say which
      table state it is against; the elements read no table and are
      exact.

7.  The port

   Sky_SunAndStars is transcribed - src/lithium/lithium_sky.c - and
   called where the original calls it, after the geometry, because the
   back buffer's 0x80 fill is the whole of its visibility test.

   WHAT IS RIGHT: the plot rules, and as it turns out everything
   else. The plot rules first - A star only lands where the buffer
   still holds 0x80, its colour is (shade >> 3) + 0x88, and the sun's
   occlusion reads one clamped byte. The star count on screen is the
   right order - 99 isolated pixels against the demo's 57 - and the
   untouched area falls from 64,000 to 51,249 where the demo reaches
   36,185, the balance being the flare.

   AND SO IS THE PROJECTION. Broken inside Sky_SunAndStars at the
   three SAR 9 boundaries - 1000:4288, 0x42a1 and 0x42ba - all three
   rotations agree to the bit [verified]:

       after Y   -603136  -1228800   9347072
       after X  -4825088  16389968  73525008
       after Z -116547274 70949286 588197888

   and the divide then gives (110, 130), which is what 1000:8f42 and
   0x8f44 hold the instant after the store. Given the demo's own
   angles the transcription reproduces that exactly.

   THE STARS MATCH TOO: fed the same angles, 125 of the 1,024 land on
   screen, 101 of those on a pixel the demo left in the star range,
   and 97 with the identical shade [verified]. The rest are positions
   the demo rejected because terrain covered them.

   TWO WAYS TO MISMEASURE THIS, both of which happened first:

   o g_SunScreenX AND Y ARE OVERWRITTEN BY Flare_Render. Read at the
     end of the frame they hold (191, 81), which is an element's
     position and not the sun's. Read them at 1000:4301, right after
     the store, or not at all.

   o A STAR IS ONE PIXEL, so comparing star POSITIONS between two
     runs needs the camera angle matched exactly, not to within a
     tick or two. Two ticks of drift moves every star and the match
     rate falls to zero while nothing is wrong.

   THE FLARE IS PORTED - src/lithium/lithium_flare.c - and verified
   against the demo's own memory at four levels [verified]:

   +----------------------------+------------------------------------+
   | the 0x7ad2 sprite segment  | 40,960 bytes identical - the cone, |
   |                            | the angle plane built through our  |
   |                            | own Poly_Fill, and the zeros above |
   | g_FlareRamp                | 256 of 256 words, AL-clamp         |
   |                            | artifacts and the 0x50 bump        |
   | the 13 elements            | 9,350 pixels, NOT ONE DIFFERENT    |
   | the sun's disc             | 14,105 pixels, 13,807 identical    |
   +----------------------------+------------------------------------+

   THE SUN'S RESIDUAL IS THE INTERRUPT, not the arithmetic - section
   6.4. Against the table as it stood BEFORE the call, rows 50 to 117
   are exact and it diverges below; against the table AFTER, rows 118
   down are exact. The 20 rows between were drawn while Flicker_Step
   was part way through rewriting the table.

   THE FLARE DOES NOT YET APPEAR IN OUR OWN RUN. The sun never
   reaches the screen in the first 900 ticks of the flyover because
   the camera diverges from the demo's before it turns that way -
   ./flight.txt. Nothing about the flare is waiting on that; it is
   the camera.

8.  Open questions

   o What maybe_Sky_DrawPoint's point was meant to be. The addresses
     cs:[0x88f5] and cs:[0x892d] are g_SlotPosX and g_SlotPosZ at
     slot 0xc, one of the bolt slots, and it is rotated by only TWO
     camera angles - no pitch - which nothing else in the demo does.
     Section 1 is why it does not matter: the gate is never set.

   o The two further callers of Flare_ClipSetup, at 1000:48d4 and
     1000:4974, are Blob_DrawKeepBand and Blob_DrawBurn, the trail's
     blitters - effects/trail.txt section 4.
