PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                       LITHIUM: THE BUMP MAP


Abstract

   A height field refracting a picture. Each pixel's horizontal and
   vertical slope is packed into a 16-bit offset, and the picture is
   read at that displacement instead of straight on - so a flat area
   samples itself and a slope pulls the sample sideways.

   NO MULTIPLY AND NO LIGHTING MODEL. The gradient pair IS the
   address, which is the whole trick: one table lookup per pixel,
   four pixels at a time in 32-bit registers.

   What makes the bumps is the dust - effects/dust.txt. The particles
   never draw a pixel; they stamp an 8x8 brush into the field, and
   this is what makes the field visible.

   All [verified] from the three routines unless marked.

1.  The pieces

   All three run every frame of phase 2 of maybe_Scene_BumpShatter.

       1000:0806   stamp every particle into the field   inline
       1000:08af   relax the field                       inline
       1000:0ad1   Bump_Render                           a function

   The three segments involved stay fixed:

       0x7ad2   the height field, 320 x 200 bytes - the same buffer
                effects/sky.txt uses for the flare sprite, reused
       0x5ad2   the picture being refracted - the same buffer the
                title image and the text renderer compose into,
                effects/text.txt section 5
       0x4ad2   the back buffer, or 0xa000 direct

2.  Stamping the field

   1000:0806, over the 1000 particles of effects/dust.txt.

       DS = 0x7ad2, ES = 0x2ad2, SI = 0x3820
       per particle:
           bx = drawn_x >> 7
           dx = drawn_y >> 7
           bx += dx*0x40 + dx*0x100              ie. dx*320 + bx
           skip if bx <= 0x140
                                                 BX IS 16-BIT and the
                                                 sum WRAPS before this
                                                 test - a particle
                                                 below the screen
                                                 lands elsewhere, it
                                                 does not vanish
           BP = g_DustBrushA, or g_DustBrushB when g_BumpInterlace
                is zero
           8 rows of 2 dwords:
               ABANDON THE PARTICLE if bx >= 0xf780
               add the brush dword to [bx], EACH BYTE SATURATING
               at 0xff
               bx += 0x140

   THE UPPER BOUND IS TESTED PER BRUSH ROW, NOT PER PARTICLE. img
   0x0842 is the top of the row loop that DH counts down, so a brush
   whose origin is near the bottom is drawn part-way and then
   dropped - it is neither clipped to the last row nor wrapped.
   Writing all eight rows regardless puts height into rows 198 and
   199, which the relaxation cannot reach [verified byte-exact
   against the emulator over all 65,536 bytes of the field].
               bx += 4, bp += 4
           bx += 0x138                           8 + 0x138 = 320
           si += 0xc

   SEVEN FRACTIONAL BITS. Dust_ResetFromPoint's launch point
   0x5000, 0x3200 shifts down to 160, 100 - the centre of a 320 x 200
   screen - which fixes the particle coordinate format.

   THE ADD SATURATES RATHER THAN WRAPPING. Overlapping particles pile
   into a brighter bump instead of rolling over to zero, and that is
   why a thousand points read as a surface rather than as noise.

   The brushes are 8 x 8 bytes in the CODE SEGMENT at 1000:a031 and
   1000:a071, immediately before g_SinTable. Both are ROUND SPLATS,
   symmetric on both axes [verified]:

       g_DustBrushA                    g_DustBrushB
       00 00 01 02 02 01 00 00         00 00 01 01 01 01 00 00
       00 02 03 04 04 03 02 00         00 01 02 02 02 02 01 00
       01 03 07 06 06 07 03 01         01 02 02 03 03 02 02 01
       02 04 06 08 08 06 04 02         01 02 03 04 04 03 02 01
       02 04 06 08 08 06 04 02         01 02 03 04 04 03 02 01
       01 03 07 06 06 07 03 01         01 02 02 03 03 02 02 01
       00 02 03 04 04 03 02 00         00 01 02 02 02 02 01 00
       00 00 01 02 02 01 00 00         00 00 01 01 01 01 00 00

   B IS ABOUT HALF A'S AMPLITUDE, not a different size - both are
   8 x 8 and both peak at the centre, at 8 and at 4. The 07 either
   side of the 06 in A's third row is the only break in an otherwise
   smooth falloff.

3.  Relaxing the field

   1000:08af, after the stamping and before the render.

       DI = 0x140, BP = 0xc7 rows
       per pixel, 0x140 a row:
           ax = [di-1] + [di+1] + [di+0x140] + [di-0x140]
           ax >>= 2
           if ax != 0: ax--
           [di] = al

   A FOUR-NEIGHBOUR AVERAGE WITH A DECAY OF ONE. The average spreads
   a stamp outward a pixel a frame; the decrement drains it. So a
   bump rises where particles are and dies where they have left, with
   no per-particle lifetime anywhere.

   It starts one row in and stops one row short, which is why the
   +/- 0x140 reads need no bounds check.

   THE HALF IT WALKS IS CHOSEN BY g_FieldToggle, img 0x08a3 - THE
   SAME BYTE Bump_Render steps. So the half relaxed this frame is the
   half the render did not draw, and the two alternate together.
   Driving the relaxation from any counter that moves more slowly
   leaves one half never drained: it saturates, and the result is a
   grain and a row banding the original has not got.

   HOW DENSE THE FIELD GETS DEPENDS ON THE FRAME RATE. The stamp
   runs once a FRAME and the particles move once a TICK, so a
   machine that draws more frames per tick lays more brush down per
   unit of movement. Measured at 35,000 DOSBox cycles the field runs
   42 to 81 per cent non-zero; a port drawing every tick will sit
   above that, and the difference is the frame rate rather than the
   stamp - ../README.txt section 11.2.

   THE ROWS IT CANNOT REACH ARE NEVER STAMPED, so nothing collects
   there. The relaxation covers 0xc7 rows from row 1, leaving the
   top row and everything from row 200 down with no drain at all -
   and the stamp's own test at 0x0842 accepts an origin as low as
   row 197, whose eight-row brush would reach past the buffer. That
   never happens: the field's bottom ten rows read ZERO at three
   points across the phase, and the active band moves UPWARD
   through it, rows 33 to 160 and then 0 to 141 [verified, dumped
   from 0x7ad2 in the emulator].

   WHAT KEEPS THEM EMPTY IS THE MASK, not a bound. A particle chases
   a target sampled from the letter mask - ./dust.txt section 2 -
   and the mask has nothing near the bottom of the screen, so no
   origin is ever generated down there. A transcription whose dust
   reaches those rows will grow a band the demo does not have, and
   the fault is then in the particle targets rather than in the
   stamp or the relaxation. Do NOT add a drain to compensate.

4.  Bump_Render

   1000:0ad1.

       DS = 0x7ad2, ES = 0x5ad2, FS = 0x4ad2 or 0xa000
       DI = 0xf8bc, walking UPWARD
       SI = (g_BumpLightX >> 8) + (g_BumpLightY & 0xff00)

       per four pixels, 0x50 times a row:
           eax = [di+4]                 four heights
           edx = [di-0x13c]             the same four, one row up
           ebx = eax rotated one byte, carrying CL in
           edx -= eax                   the VERTICAL gradient
           eax -= ebx                   the HORIZONTAL gradient
           eax += eax;  edx += edx
           eax &= 0xfefefefe;  edx &= 0xfefefefe
           for each of the four:
               bl = its dx byte, bh = its dy byte
               pixel = ES:[bx + si]
           FS:[di+4] = the four pixels

   SI IS SWEPT ACROSS THE SCREEN, NOT HELD. It drops by 2 every four
   pixels at 0x0b6d and is restored by the ADD SI, 0xa0 at 0x0b7d, so
   the picture is MAPPED over the surface at half a texel per column.
   Within a group of four the left pair reads SI and the right pair
   SI+1, which is the half.

   VERTICALLY IT DEPENDS ON THE INTERLACE. The interlaced path takes
   the 0x0ba4 tail and drops SI by 0x100 for every row it draws; the
   full-rate path instead bumps g_FieldToggle at img 0x0b89 and goes
   straight back round at img 0x0b94 while that is odd, LEAVING SI
   ALONE - so there the light descends one texel row per TWO screen
   rows. Measured: 198 rows drawn against 99 subtractions of 0x100
   [verified]. Sweeping it every row on the full-rate path moves the
   light down the picture twice as fast as the original.

   THAT IS WHY MORE THAN ONE HIGHLIGHT APPEARS. The base offset
   travels about 160 texels across and 100 down over a frame, far
   enough that opposite corners of the screen sit near different
   glows of the three in the picture, and the surface carries a
   large-scale lighting ramp on top of the per-pixel displacement.
   Sampling from one fixed offset instead gives a single hotspot.

   THE HEIGHTS ARE READ FOUR BYTES AHEAD OF THE PIXELS THEY SHADE.
   img 0x0b20 reads [DI+4], img 0x0b48 steps DI down by four and img
   0x0b74 writes [DI+4] with the NEW DI - so the group written is the
   one four bytes below the group read, and the vertical neighbour at
   [DI-0x13c] is one row up from the READ. The relief therefore sits
   four columns to the left of the field that made it.

   IT COVERS ROWS 1 TO 198. DI starts at 0xf8bc and the loop runs
   while DI >= 0x280 at 0x0bac, which with the four-byte offset above
   puts the writes at 0x140 to 0xf8bf - all of row 1 through row 198
   bar its last three columns. ROWS 0 AND 199 ARE NEVER WRITTEN. Row
   199 is also the one row the interlaced relaxation never reaches,
   section 5, so it accumulates stamps with nothing to drain them;
   drawing it puts a saturated lumpy strip along the bottom that the
   demo does not have.

   THE TWO SUBTRACTIONS ARE 32-BIT AND THEIR BORROWS CROSS BYTES.
   Only the DOUBLING's carry is removed, by the 0xfefefefe at img
   0x0b3e clearing the low bit of each byte - which is the bit a
   left shift carries in. Computing the four slopes independently
   therefore disagrees with the original on about a quarter of the
   pixels, by one or two levels each [verified].

   THE HORIZONTAL NEIGHBOUR IS THE ONE AT THE HIGHER ADDRESS. img
   0x0b2c exchanges CL with BL and img 0x0b2e rotates right a byte,
   which shifts the four heights along and carries the PREVIOUS
   group's first height in as this group's fourth neighbour. So the
   slope is height minus the byte after it, and CL threads that
   chain across groups and across rows - it is never reset.

   THE GRADIENT PAIR IS THE ADDRESS. bx is assembled as (dy, dx), so
   a byte of slope on each axis becomes a 16-bit offset into the
   64 KB at ES. There is no dot product and no normal - the
   displacement IS the shading, because the picture is read somewhere
   else than it is written.

   MASKING WITH 0xfefefefe IS WHAT KEEPS THE FOUR BYTES SEPARATE.
   After the doubling, clearing each byte's low bit stops a carry
   crossing into the next pixel's slope. The doubling is the gain:
   without it a one-level step would move the sample one byte.

   The rotate with a carry-in through CL is how the horizontal
   neighbour is obtained without a second memory read - the four
   heights are shifted along by one byte inside the register.

   WHERE THE LIGHT SITS DECIDES WHETHER THE RELIEF READS AT ALL. At a
   disc's centre every flat pixel samples the maximum and a slope can
   only darken it, so the surface inverts into dark outlines on a
   flat glare; parked on the linear part of a disc's ramp a slope
   moves the sample either way and the bumps come out properly. And
   the picture is only 56 per cent lit, so a light off the discs
   leaves the whole surface black - which is what the demo's wander
   does for much of the time.

   SI IS THE LIGHT. Vec_Ease2D at 1000:6244 moves it, so the sampled
   picture drifts under the bumps and the surface appears to be lit
   from a moving source.

   AND THERE ARE THREE OF THEM. The picture this scene refracts is
   Tex_BuildThreeGlows' output - the script builds it at img 0x029d,
   immediately before the runner - so the "lights" are the three
   radial discs of subsystems/texture.txt section 6.2, and a pixel's
   slope decides which of them it happens to catch. Nothing in the
   bump code knows there are three; the count is a property of the
   picture.

   WHAT AIMS IT IS Bump_RandomiseLight at 1000:0c44, which sets
   g_BumpLightVelX and g_BumpLightVelY from Rnd_NextB >> 5. It is
   called from Tick_Default - the common tail of every handler - on
   the eighth tick of a fade, so the light changes direction on a
   slow cadence that belongs to no scene in particular.

5.  The interlace
      5.1. What the break hands over

   g_BumpInterlace at 1000:8ff8 picks the destination and halves the
   work.

       nonzero   FS = 0x4ad2, every row, and g_FieldToggle is reset
       zero      FS = 0xa000 direct, alternate rows, the field
                 chosen by g_FieldToggle

   So the expensive path writes a back buffer in full and the cheap
   one writes the screen in fields. It also picks the brush: the
   full-rate path stamps with g_DustBrushA and the interlaced one
   with the half-amplitude g_DustBrushB.

   THE SCENE CROSSES OVER AFTER 250 FRAMES. Nothing initialises
   g_RockSlideX - the only writers drain it, Tick_Dust by 0x20 a tick
   at 0x6a56 and Tick_Rock by 4, both guarded so they floor at zero -
   so it holds its LOADED value, and the image carries 0x2ee0 at
   1000:8ff9 [verified]. The test at 0x07f4 sets the flag once it
   falls below 0xfa0, which at 0x20 a tick is 250 ticks in. Only
   Tick_Dust and Tick_Rock drain it and neither runs before this
   scene installs them, so the count starts here.

   THE LOADED STATE OF THE OTHER TWO IS NEXT TO IT [verified]:

       1000:8ff7   g_DustMaskShift   0x0b    the full-height word
       1000:8ff8   g_BumpInterlace   0x00    the opening path
       1000:8ff9   g_RockSlideX      0x2ee0  250 ticks of interlace

   THE ROCK'S POSITION IS LOOKED UP, NOT FLOWN. Obj_WobblePos at
   1000:6008 reads the sine table three times off the rock's own
   attitude - the pitch, yaw and roll accumulators Tick_Dust steps
   by 0x45, 0x24 and 0x36 a tick - so it traces a Lissajous figure:

       pos x = (sin(pitch) >> 2) + g_RockSlideX     img 0x601b
       pos y = (sin(yaw)   >> 2)
       pos z = (sin(roll)  >> 1) + 0x1f40 + home z  img 0x6058

   g_RockSlideX IS THE ENTRANCE, and that is why one counter serves
   two purposes: the rock begins 0x2ee0 off to the right and slides
   in as the same drain that decides the interlace crossover empties
   it. The scene's opening shimmer and the rock's approach are the
   same clock.

   THE APPROACH IS GUARDED AND SO IT WAITS. img 0x6a64 is a JLE past
   the fold, so home z takes nothing while flight z is negative:
   seeded at -1800 and stepped 4 a tick, that is 450 ticks of a rock
   that wobbles in place before it begins to accelerate. Folding
   unconditionally drives it through the surface long before the
   shatter.

   ONLY THE ANGLES AND THE POSITION REACH THE MESH. Obj_Select at
   1000:4ffd copies g_SlotAngle X, Y and Z and the position into the
   object Mesh_Transform reads; pitch, yaw and roll stay behind in
   the slot bank as the wobble's phases and never rotate anything.

   ALL ELEVEN FIELDS VERIFIED against the emulator at the frame the
   rock enters - 293 ticks into the scene - position, the six
   accumulators, home z and flight z, exact [verified].

   THE TOP AND BOTTOM ROWS ARE CLEARED EVERY FRAME, img 0x0922 and
   0x092d, which are exactly the two Bump_Render never writes.
   Without that, whatever the previous scene left shows along both
   edges for the whole shot.

   THE ROCK IS DRAWN IN THIS SCENE, AND ONLY AFTER THE CROSSOVER.
   The test at 0x08f0 sends the interlaced path straight to the
   bottom of the loop; the full-rate path falls into img 0x0904,
   which every frame zeroes g_VertPoolCount and runs Obj_Select(4),
   Mesh_Transform, Scene_ProjectVerts and Scene_DrawFacesReset. The
   setup already pointed g_MeshSrcOff at 0x41ea with scale 0x10 at
   img 0x07a9, so that is the chrome rock of effects/rock.txt,
   arriving over the surface before it breaks.

   IT CANNOT AFFECT THE HEIGHT FIELD. The field is segment 0x7ad2
   and the rock is drawn into 0x4ad2, which is also where
   Bump_Render writes once the flag is set - so the rock lands ON
   the rendered picture, after the displacement has been resolved,
   and nothing it draws can feed back into the relaxation.

   THE SHATTER FIRES ON THE ROCK'S DEPTH. 1000:8925 is g_SlotPosZ
   for slot 4 - the array starts at 1000:8921 and steps 4 - so the
   test at img 0x08f8 against 0x88b8 is a position test on the rock
   itself, and it branches to Tex_SnapshotFrom plus Shatter_Build
   when the rock passes that depth. Z is seeded to 0x1b58 at img
   0x072a and Obj_WobblePos rewrites it every tick from the sine
   table plus g_SlotHomeZ, so what actually drives the break is
   g_SlotHomeZ: g_SlotFlightZ is seeded to -1800 at img 0x0734 and
   Tick_Dust adds 4 a tick, folding it into g_SlotHomeZ once it
   turns positive, so the approach accelerates and the surface
   breaks when the rock arrives - not on a clock or a music cue.

   THAT CROSSOVER IS VISIBLE. Before it, half the scanlines are
   repainted per frame straight to 0xa000 and the picture shimmers;
   after it, every row is drawn, the relaxation reaches row 199, and
   the stamp changes to the peak-8 brush. Holding the opening state
   for the whole scene leaves the shimmer running and the bottom rows
   with nothing to drain them.

   IT IS NOT ONLY A SAVING - IT IS WHAT GIVES THE SURFACE ITS
   RELIEF. The relaxation at 1000:08af walks 0xc7 rows when the flag
   is set and 0x63 when it is clear, skipping one each time, so in
   the scene's opening state HALF THE FIELD IS NEVER TOUCHED IN A
   GIVEN FRAME and those rows keep their full height. The average
   and the decay only reach a row every other frame.

   RELAXING EVERY ROW INSTEAD COLLAPSES THE EFFECT. Measured over
   the opening burst, the interlaced field carries about twelve
   times the total height of one relaxed in full, and the difference
   is visible as the difference between a heavy noise surface and a
   scatter of small droplets. The half-amplitude brush is paired
   with the interlace precisely because the interlace lets height
   accumulate.

5.1.  What the break hands over

   img 0x0942 to 0x09ce, once, between the last bump frame and the
   first shatter frame:

       Tex_SnapshotFrom(0x4ad2)        the frame the shards carry
       g_ShatterAssemble = 0
       Shatter_Build
       g_BumpInterlace = 1
       g_TickHandler = Tick_Rock
       Mem_ClearSegment(0x4ad2)
       Obj_Reset(0);  slot 0's flight z = 0x1000, home = 0
       g_SlotAngleA{X,Y,Z}[0] = 0x80, 0x40, 0x20
       g_PalFlash = 0;  g_PalRampShift = 2;  g_DepthNear = 0x36b
       g_TunnelFirstRing = -4;  g_TunnelLastRing = 11

   THE SNAPSHOT IS OF THE FRAME JUST DRAWN, so what breaks is the
   bump surface with the rock already on it - effects/shatter.txt
   section 5 and subsystems/raster.txt section 4.1.

   TEX_SNAPSHOTFROM ALSO AIMS THE FADE, and that is the whole of the
   next phase's lighting. img 0x27ea zeroes g_PalFadeTarget2 before
   a byte is copied. This scene runs at a target of -0xc0 with
   g_PalFlash dragging the fade back up on every beat - section 6 -
   and the handover clears the flash three instructions later, so
   without that store nothing holds the level up and the tunnel goes
   black a few seconds in. A copy routine carrying a palette write
   reads as incidental and is not.

   THE RAMP IS UNCHANGED ACROSS THE HANDOVER. Pal_Animate advances
   its own three phases, so the colour keeps cycling with no scene
   involvement; only g_PalRampShift is restated, and at the same 2
   the bump scene was already using.

   ONLY SLOT 0 IS RESET. The rock keeps the spin and the depth this
   scene left it at and simply starts being flown by a different
   handler - effects/rock.txt section 5.

   THE INTERLACE FLAG IS SET RATHER THAN CLEARED, and it is not the
   bump render that reads it afterwards: Bump_Render is not called
   again. It survives as the flag at img 0x0ae9 that points the
   render's FS at the back buffer instead of 0xa000, which is dead
   in this phase.

   THE TICK HANDLER IS ASSIGNED TWICE. Tex_SnapshotFrom installs the
   default one at img 0x27e3 before it copies a byte and img 0x0957
   overwrites it, so the snapshot call is not where a scene's
   handler can be read from.

6.  The flash, and what it is tied to

   g_PalFlash at 1000:8fe4 is set to 0x80 by this scene at img 0x0794
   and cleared at img 0x09b0, so it belongs to phase two.
   Tick_Default uses it twice, img 0x6c8e onward:

       g_FlashCountdown--
       if it reached zero and g_PalFlash != 0:
           g_FlashCountdown = 0x18
           Bump_RandomiseLight                 THE LIGHT RE-AIMS
           raise g_PalFade to g_PalFlash
       if the music position changed:
           g_FlashCountdown = 0x18             RESET, no re-aim
           raise g_PalFade to g_PalFlash

   0x80 IS A FULL WHITE FLASH. Pal_Upload biases every component by
   g_PalFade >> 2, so +32 saturates all 64 entries of a 0 to 0x3f
   ramp. The scene's resting target is 0xff40 - a bias of -48, nearly
   black - so the screen swings between almost black and pure white
   on every music position change.

   ONE COUNTER DOES BOTH JOBS, and that couples them. A beat resets
   the countdown WITHOUT re-aiming, so beats arriving faster than 24
   ticks hold the light still while the screen keeps flashing.

   THE SIM LEAVES THE FLASH OUT. It takes the raw cycling colours
   with no bias at all - a deliberate divergence, recorded here so
   nobody restores it: neither end of the swing is worth watching.

7.  What the scene seeds

   THE WORD IS "VOTE", and it is drawn, not stored as a bitmap. The
   setup clears 6,400 bytes at 0x2ad2:0x6700 with a REP STOSD at img
   0x0686 and then calls THE CREDIT ROLL'S OWN TEXT ROUTINE, img
   0x2d46, to write into them:

       img 0x068b   cs:[0x8f4b] = 0x6700    the pen, the destination
                                            offset inside the segment
       img 0x0692   bx = 0x92eb             the string - "VOTE", NUL
                                            terminated
       img 0x0695   ax = 0x2ad2             the destination SEGMENT
       img 0x0698   call 0x2d46             dx = 0, so full strength

   So the mask is 320 by 20 and lives immediately after the particle
   pool in the module's own segment, past the end of the module's
   22,902 bytes. Its ink is 0x0f and everything else is zero, and the
   glyphs occupy rows 2 to 15 of the 20 - which is what the phase's
   sample shift in ../effects/dust.txt section 2 is widening across
   [verified, dumped from the running demo].

   THE BUFFERS ARE CLEARED FIRST, img 0x066c and 0x0672:
   Mem_ClearSegment on 0x7ad2, the height field, and on 0x4ad2, the
   back buffer. The field starting at zero is what makes the opening
   burst read as a surface rising out of nothing.

   A MUSIC GATE SPLITS THE SETUP IN TWO. img 0x0762 is
   Demo_RunUntil(0x18) - ../subsystems/timeline.txt section 2 - and
   it sits between the two halves rather than at the scene's edge.
   Before it: the two buffer clears above, the mask, the slot
   zeroing from img 0x0665, and the rock's seeding - pos z 0x1b58 at
   img 0x072a and flight z -1800 at img 0x0734. After it, img 0x0765
   onward:

       cs:[0x8ee8], [0x8eea], [0x8eec] = 0xf8a3, 0xf6f0, 0xf41b
       g_PalRampShift = 2
       g_PalFade = g_PalFadeTarget2 = 0xff00, then Pal_Animate and
           Pal_Upload
       g_PalFlash = 0x80;  g_FlashCountdown = 0x18
       g_PalFade = 0x80;  g_PalFadeTarget2 = 0xff40
       the fade shift = 6
       g_TickHandler = Tick_Dust                at img 0x07ed

   SO THE SCENE OPENS WITH 90 TICKS OF NOTHING OF ITS OWN. Tick_Dust
   is not installed until after the gate, g_TickHandler still holds
   the Tick_Default written at img 0x0665, and Demo_RunUntil neither
   draws nor presents - so the flyover's last frame is held while the
   module covers the 90 ticks from order 0x15 to order 0x18
   [verified, measured at 111 display frames]. The dust cloud's own
   572 ticks begin only after that.

   THE SCENE CYCLES ITS OWN PALETTE, and Tick_Default does NOT.
   Only a scene's handler calls Pal_Animate - ./transition.txt - and
   Tick_Dust's tail does it every tick after driving the three
   accumulators hard: +0x73, +0x50 and +0x0b at img 0x6b1d, against
   the +0xd, +0x10 and +0x15 Pal_Animate adds for itself. So the
   surface's colours run several times faster here than anywhere
   else, and a port that only seeds the phases gets a palette that
   never moves - 256 entries of one grey, which looks exactly like a
   broken renderer and is not one.

   NO STORED PALETTE IS SELECTED EITHER. Pal_Animate GENERATES the
   ramps from those accumulators, so there is nothing to load.

   THE 7 TO 1 RELOAD OF g_DustPhase IS NOT PART OF THE REFILL. img
   0x6b0f sits on the path both branches of the music test reach, so
   it runs every tick; only the 0xfc reload is inside the refill.

   FOUR VALUES ARRIVE FROM THE IMAGE AND NOT FROM THIS SETUP, which
   never writes them [verified, read at img 0x0665 in the emulator
   and matched against the image's own bytes]:

       img 0x8ff9   g_RockSlideX        0x2ee0   250 ticks
       img 0x8ff7   g_DustPhase         0x0b
       img 0x8fea   g_DustJitterShift   0x01
       img 0x8fc7   g_RndStateB0 / B1   0xa5b5, 0xcab6

   A JITTER SHIFT OF 0 RATHER THAN 1 IS NOT A SMALL ERROR. It is the
   SAR count applied to each random before it is added to the target,
   so zero adds the whole 16-bit value and the cloud wanders twice as
   far - the stamps scatter and the surface comes out grainy.

   THE SECOND GENERATOR'S PAIR IS A LOADED VALUE TOO. Nothing in the
   demo but Rnd_NextB touches img 0x8fc7, so the bump scene still
   finds exactly what the image carries - and the image gives it the
   same pair it gives the main generator. It is not a seed anyone
   chose, and inventing one leaves the light wandering differently.

   THE PALETTE PHASES ARE SEEDED, NOT STARTED FROM ZERO. Those three
   words are Pal_Animate's accumulators - subsystems/texture.txt - so
   the cycling begins at a defined point in the ramp instead of
   wherever the previous scene left it, and g_PalRampShift of 2
   steepens every ramp at once. Both change what the surface looks
   like and neither is guessable from the effect code.

7.1.  Which of the four buffers can be compared, and how

   THE SCENE IS FOUR ARRAYS: the height field at 0x7ad2, the picture
   at 0x5ad2, the mask at 0x2ad2:0x6700 and the 1000-record pool at
   0x2ad2:0x3820. They do not all compare the same way.

   THE PICTURE AND THE MASK ARE STATIC and byte-exact - both are
   built once by the setup and never written again [verified].

   THE POOL COMPARES PER TICK, and exactly. Seeded with the main
   generator's state at the scene's entry, a port reproduces
   Dust_ResetFromPoint's output byte for byte AND the generator's
   state afterwards, then tracks the demo through 121 ticks of the
   mover with no difference in 12,000 bytes [verified]. The mover is
   the only consumer in between, so the generator is a complete
   description of the scene's randomness.

   THE FIELD CANNOT, WITHOUT MATCHING THE FRAME RATE. The stamp runs
   once a FRAME and the particles move once a TICK, so two machines
   drawing at different rates lay different amounts of brush down for
   the same motion - section 3. It is the one of the four whose
   contents depend on the host.
