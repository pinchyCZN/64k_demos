PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                       LITHIUM: THE OPENING


Abstract

   The demo's first runner, maybe_Scene_RockIntro at 1000:150b. It is
   the first scene Demo_Main calls, at img 0x01fe, and it runs until
   the module reaches order position 4.

   ONE RUNNER PRODUCES ALL THREE OF THE OPENING'S APPARENT SCENES -
   the warping chrome surface, the rock that moves in, and the VISTA
   logo. There is no dispatcher between them and no second runner.

   THE WARPING SURFACE AND THE ROCK ARE THE SAME MESH AT TWO SCALES.
   0x41ea is loaded into both slots; the difference is one shift.

   All [verified] from the runner, Tick_RockIntro and Image_BlitScaled.

1.  The runner

   1000:150b, 0x1ca bytes. Setup, then a loop from img 0x1635.

       Mem_ClearSegment(0x7ad2)
       Image_UnpackRle                   a 320x200 picture into 0x5ad2
       blur 0x5ad2 TWICE                 1000:4b15 with CX = 2
       g_PalRampShift = 1
       g_TickHandler = 0x6294            Tick_RockIntro
       g_SlotPosZ[8] = 0x6590;  g_SlotPosZ[4] = 0x1b58
       g_LogoZoom = 0x2200
       g_BlitCentreX, g_BlitCentreY = 0xa0, 0x64
       g_BlitSrcW, g_BlitSrcH = 0x50000, 0x32000
       g_DepthNear = 0x186a

   per frame, from img 0x1635:

       g_VertPoolCount = 0
       mesh 0x41ea, g_ObjScaleShift = 0xe, g_ObjMirror = 1
       Obj_Select(4);  g_ObjFaceAttr = 0x6ad2;  Mesh_Transform
       if -0x1f4 < g_SlotPosZ[8] < 0x2cec:
           mesh 0x41ea, g_ObjScaleShift = 0x10, g_ObjMirror = 0
           Obj_Select(8);  g_ObjFaceAttr = 0x6ad2;  Mesh_Transform
       Scene_ProjectVerts;  Scene_DrawFacesReset
       Image_BlitScaled                  the logo
       Screen_CrossFade
       Demo_PollKeys
   until g_MusicPos >= 4 or g_PalFade >= 0xff60

   NOTHING THE RUNNER DRAWS REACHES THE SCREEN DIRECTLY. Both the
   geometry and the title go into 0x4ad2, and Screen_CrossFade at img
   0x185a moves a THIRD buffer a quarter of the way toward it:

       blend = blend + ((back - blend) >> 2)

   writing each result to 0x7ad2 and to 0xa000 together. So the
   picture is a running average of the last several frames, which is
   where the demo's smeared, liquid look comes from - and it is why
   the runner clears 0x7ad2 at img 0x150b. That segment is the
   cross-fade accumulator here, not the planar map it is elsewhere.

   THE SHIFT IS SIGNED AND THE APPROACH IS NOT SYMMETRIC: a gap of
   -1 to -3 still steps down by one where +1 to +3 steps by nothing,
   so a pixel falls to its target but can stall just short of it on
   the way up.

2.  One mesh, two sizes

   Both slots load 0x41ea, the 130-vertex 256-face model of
   ../formats/mesh.txt section 4, under the sphere map at 0x6ad2.
   They differ in one byte:

   +--------+------------------+-------------+----------------------+
   | Slot   | g_ObjScaleShift  | g_ObjMirror | reads as             |
   +========+==================+=============+======================+
   | 4      | 0xe              | 1           | a warping surface    |
   | 8      | 0x10             | 0           | a rock               |
   +--------+------------------+-------------+----------------------+

   IT IS LITERALLY ONE MODEL TWICE, confirmed in the running demo:
   with both copies up, g_VertPoolCount reads 260 and
   g_FacePoolCount 512 - exactly double the rock's 130 and 256, with
   no second model anywhere in the pool.

   THE SMALLER SHIFT IS THE LARGER OBJECT. At 0xe the mesh is four
   times the size it is at 0x10, far enough past the near plane that
   only a patch of its curvature is on screen - so what the viewer
   reads as a warping chrome surface is the same rock, too close to
   be recognised as one.

   THE Z FUDGE IS A SCENE TERM AND IT IS NOT NEUTRAL HERE. img 0x4e44
   reads g_SlotPosZ[0] - a FIXED address, element 0, not the slot
   being drawn - and applies (-(v + 1)) >> 0xc << 8. The opening runs
   with that element at 0, so EVERY VERTEX MOVES 256 UNITS CLOSER.
   Reading it at 1000:4e33 and 0x4e44 against the running demo is
   what settled it: with the offset left out the port covered 84.5
   per cent of the screen where the demo covers 100, and the surface
   read as a recognisable rock instead of a wall of curvature.

   SLOT 8 IS DEPTH-GATED, NOT TIMED. img 0x166c skips it entirely
   unless its Z lies between -0x1f4 and 0x2cec. The Z starts at
   0x6590 and Tick_RockIntro drops it 0x20 a tick, so the second
   copy appears when it comes near and is dropped once it passes.

3.  Tick_RockIntro

   1000:6294, installed at img 0x153a.

       g_SlotAngle{X,Y,Z}[4] += 0x10, 0x0c, 0x0a
       g_SlotPosZ[8]         -= 0x20
       g_SlotAngle{X,Y,Z}[8] += 0x05, 0x07, 0x0d
       bx = (g_SlotPosZ[8] >> 1) + 0x200, & 0x1ffc
       g_SlotPosY[8] = (g_SinTable[bx] >> 1) - 0x48
       g_EnvMapAngle += 0x10
       g_LogoZoom    -= 8
       if g_LogoZoom < 0x1900:  g_BlitPhase += 0xa
       Pal_Animate;  Pal_Upload
       if g_LogoZoom < 0:
           g_BlitSrcOfs = 0xa0;  g_BlitPhase = 0xff00
           g_LogoZoom = -0x200;  g_TickHandler = 0x6337

   SIX RATES FOR TWO OBJECTS, none shared, so the two copies tumble
   on unrelated schedules and never look like one thing twice.

   THE SMALL ONE BOBS BECAUSE ITS HEIGHT IS A FUNCTION OF ITS DEPTH.
   g_SlotPosY[8] is a sine lookup indexed by its own Z, so the rock
   rises and falls as it closes rather than on a clock of its own.

   IT HANDS OFF RATHER THAN ENDING, AND THE HANDOFF IS THE WHOLE
   VISTA-TO-LITHIUM TRANSITION. Once the zoom passes zero, img 0x6315
   moves g_BlitSrcOfs to 0xa0 - the OTHER HALF of the one picture -
   and restarts the zoom at -0x200, behind the camera. Nothing else
   changes: no second image is loaded and no scene ends.

   SO THE SECOND WORD RETRACES THE FIRST ONE'S PATH BACKWARDS. The
   first grows toward the viewer and passes behind the camera; the
   second starts behind it, swells into view and recedes.

4.1.  Tick_RockIntro2

   1000:6337, 0x67 bytes, installed by that handoff.

   THE ROCKS DO NOT NOTICE IT. All six angle rates are the ones phase
   one uses, g_SlotPosZ[8] still falls 0x20 a tick, g_SlotPosY[8] is
   still the sine of its own depth, and g_EnvMapAngle still gains
   0x10. Only the logo moves differently.

       g_LogoZoom += (0x800 - g_LogoZoom) >> 9      img 0x6368
       g_BlitPhase += 9
       Pal_Animate;  Pal_Upload
       if g_MusicPos >= 6:  g_PalFadeTarget2 = 0xff00

   AN EASE OUTWARD WHERE PHASE ONE WAS A WALK INWARD. Closing a
   512th of the gap to 0x800 each tick is slow at first, so the word
   stays behind the camera for about 128 ticks before the zoom turns
   positive, then arrives enormous and shrinks away.

4.  The spinning reflection

   g_EnvMapAngle at 1000:8f76 gains 0x10 every tick, and this is the
   only place in the demo that writes it per frame.

   THAT IS WHY THE CHROME LOOKS WRONG. Mesh_Transform rotates each
   per-vertex normal into view space and biases it by 0x80 to index
   the sphere map - ../subsystems/mesh3d.txt section 5. Adding a
   constant to that angle every tick turns the REFLECTION on its own,
   independently of the geometry, so highlights crawl across a
   surface that is not turning that way. A correct environment map is
   fixed in world space and only the object moves; this one does
   both, which reads as a material that is alive.

5.  The logo

   Image_BlitScaled at 1000:16d5 draws the picture at 0x5ad2 into
   0x4ad2, scaled by g_LogoZoom about g_BlitCentreX, g_BlitCentreY.

   THE PICTURE IS THE TITLE CARD, "Vista Lithium" in a tall script
   hand, white on black across rows 13 to 176 [verified]. It is RLE
   in the image at img 0x1930f - 0x1e5f:0xad1f, the asset segment -
   and 4,646 source bytes expand to exactly 64,000. Each source byte
   is one run: bit 7 is the value, 0 or 0x7f, and bits 0 to 6 are the
   length less one. Image_UnpackRle at 1000:2d10.

   THE BLUR IS Image_Blur AND NOT Tex_Blur, and the two are a trap
   for each other. They sit side by side at img 0x4aee and 0x4b15 and
   are identical but for their vertical offsets: Tex_Blur reaches
   +-0x100 because a texture is 256 wide, Image_Blur +-0x140 because
   a picture is 320. Smoothing the title with the texture one pulls
   every row 64 bytes out of line and the letterforms come out
   mangled - legible as a shape, not as writing.

   IT IS TWO-LEVEL AND THEN BLURRED TWICE, which is what turns the
   hard stencil into the soft grey letterforms the blit actually
   scales. The runner is the only one that blurs 0x5ad2 - the blur at
   1000:4b15 runs with CX = 2 at img 0x151c. Read live, row 100 of
   the blurred buffer rises to 0x7c through a gradient, against the
   flat 0x7f it started as.

   ONE PICTURE HOLDS BOTH WORDS AND THE BLIT TAKES HALF OF IT
   [verified]. The u accumulator steps by the zoom and is shifted
   >> 0xb, while v is shifted >> 0xa - so with g_BlitSrcW at 320 << 10
   the source columns run 0 to 160, HALF the picture's width, where
   the rows run the full 200. g_BlitSrcOfs at img 0x8fd4 is 0 through
   the opening, which takes the left half: "Vista". Read off the
   running demo, the screen shows that word alone.

   SO SCENES 3 AND 4 ARE THE SAME BLIT TWICE. The picture decodes to
   "Vista Lithium" across its full width - section 5 - and moving
   g_BlitSrcOfs to 160 takes the right half instead, which is the
   LITHIUM of scenes.txt 2.4. One image, two halves, one routine.

   IT IS AN ADDITIVE BLEND, NOT A COPY: img 0x1839 adds the source
   byte into the destination and img 0x183e saturates at 0x7f, which
   is why the word appears THROUGH the chrome rather than over it. A
   source byte of 0 is skipped outright at img 0x1833.

   THE FADE IS A SINE OF g_BlitPhase. img 0x17d9 reads the table at
   img 0xa8b2 and makes (sin - 0x1000) >> 7 + 0x7f, clamped at zero,
   which it SUBTRACTS from every source byte - so the whole word
   breathes in and out rather than any pixel being keyed.

   g_LogoZoom FALLS 8 A TICK from 0x2200, so the logo grows toward
   the viewer throughout; it crosses 0x1900 at tick 288, which is
   where g_BlitPhase at 1000:8f9f starts advancing 0xa a tick, and
   when it passes zero at tick 1088 the handoff in section 3 fires.

   THE FADE IS A COSINE AND THAT IS WHAT HOLDS THE WORD BACK. img
   0x17e2 reads the table at 0xa8b2 where its base is 0xa0b2, and
   0x800 is 512 dwords - a quarter turn - so the term is cos, not
   sin. At phase 0 cos is 0x1000, the fade is a full 0x7f, and every
   source byte goes to zero: THE WORD IS INVISIBLE UNTIL THE PHASE
   MOVES, and the phase does not move until the zoom crosses 0x1900
   at tick 288.

       tick   zoom    w x h   fade   the word
          0   8704    37x23    127   invisible
        288   6400    51x32    127   invisible, and now starting
        438   5200    63x39    108   19 of 127
        588   4000    81x51     73   54
        688   3200   102x64     63   brightest, 64
        888   1600   204x128    91   36, and much larger
       1088      0      -      126   1, at the handoff

   So it rises once, peaks around tick 688, and dims as it swells
   past the camera - and because the rock enters its depth gate at
   tick 453 the two arrive together. On the sine the word would
   instead be half lit from the first frame and gone by the time the
   rock appeared [verified; the whole back buffer is byte-identical
   to the demo's at tick 91, where the word contributes nothing].

   The period is 8192 phase units - 819 ticks - not the 2048 the
   index suggests, because the mask is g_BlitPhase & 0x1ffc. Phase 1
   gives it 800, just under one turn, so there is exactly one arc.

6.  The three object flags

   Two of them are rewritten per slot inside the loop at 1000:1635 -
   g_ObjScaleShift and g_ObjMirror - and THE THIRD IS SET ONCE IN THE
   RUNNER'S HEAD at 1000:1606, so g_ObjAxisSwap holds for both slots
   and for the whole part [verified; read live as 1 at slot 0].

6.1.  g_ObjAxisSwap

       XCHG CL,BL   then   NEG BL          BL is y, CL is z

   The exchange leaves y holding the old z and the negate lands on
   that, so it is y = -z, z = y - a quarter turn about x. The other
   sense is the easy mistake and it is not visibly broken: it moves
   the environment-map highlight from the middle of the screen to the
   top. Bytes on the position pass at 1000:4d9f, words on the normal
   pass at 1000:4e82, so the negate's width differs.

6.2.  g_ObjMirror does two unrelated things

   o  1000:4ef3 negates both env-map components, reflecting the
      sphere-map lookup.

   o  1000:4f51 does XCHG AX,CX in the FACE loop, where those hold
      the first and third VERTEX INDICES - it reverses the winding
      order.

   Reversed winding is what keeps a reflected model facing outward,
   so it decides which faces survive the cull rather than how they
   are shaded. The flag therefore changes BOTH the lookup and the
   geometry, by two different mechanisms [verified].

7.  The opening fade-up out of black

   THE DEMO OPENS BLACK AND THE CHROME ARRIVES IN THE TOP RIGHT, and
   IT IS A PALETTE FADE. g_PalFade enters the runner at its stored
   0xff00 - minus 256 - and Tick_Default walks it to 0 at one a tick,
   so the part spends its first 256 ticks, four and a half seconds,
   fading up out of black [verified; -240 at tick 13 and -52 at tick
   201 on a fresh run, which is 188 over 188].

   THE INDEX BUFFER IS NOT BLACK WHILE THIS HAPPENS. At tick 13 all
   64,000 of the demo's bytes are non-zero and average 23.7 - the
   geometry is drawn and the chrome is there. What is black is the
   DAC: a fade of -240 biases every entry by -60, and a palette whose
   entries only reach 0x3f clamps nearly all of them to zero. Compare
   indices against indices and palettes against palettes here, never
   the resolved picture.

   WHY THE FADE HAS NOT ALREADY RUN by the time the scene starts,
   given the interrupt was installed long before: Demo_TickIsr tests
   g_MixRateShift at 1000:6282 and jumps straight to the tail while
   it is zero, past the replayer AND the tick handler AND the fade.
   1000:708f sets it, three ticks before the runner. So nothing in
   the tick chain runs during Demo_Main's setup at all, and the fade
   is still full when the opening begins [verified].

   Screen_CrossFade adds a second, much shorter ramp on top of it,
   starting from the blend buffer that 1000:150e clears. The blend
   moves a QUARTER of the way toward the rendered frame each pass:

       passes   1     3     5     8    11    16
       reached  25%   58%   76%   90%  96%   99%

   and because it is a proportional step the BRIGHTEST region crosses
   into visibility first. At tick 91 the back buffer's top-right
   eighth means 52.8 against 13 to 24 everywhere else [verified], so
   the chrome appears there and spreads - which is what it looks like.

7.1.  Its duration is set by the frame rate, not the tick rate

   The blend takes its quarter step once per RENDERED FRAME, and the
   original renders as fast as the machine manages while its motion
   runs on the 57 Hz interrupt. Measured in the emulator, consecutive
   frames are 4, 3 and 3 ticks apart - about 17 frames a second
   [observed; g_EnvMapAngle read at 1000:16b9 on successive passes].

   So the blend's own ramp takes about 0.65 s to reach 96 per cent
   where blending once per tick reaches it in 0.19 s. THE TIME
   CONSTANT OF THE BLEND IS A PROPERTY OF THE 1997 MACHINE and no
   constant in the image gives it - unlike the palette fade above,
   which is simply the stored 0xff00 and needs nothing measured.

   The port therefore renders the back buffer every tick - so it can
   be diffed against the emulator at any tick - and lets the blend
   consume every third one, which is the same sequence of inputs the
   original blends. Measured mid-fade at tick 202 that is worth a
   mean error of 1.97 against 4.32 for blending every tick. It cannot
   go to zero: the real cadence varies frame by frame with how much
   of the screen the geometry covers, and a fixed three cannot follow
   it.

8.  How the part ends

   NEITHER TEST ENDS IT ALONE. The runner loops at 1000:16bf and
   1000:16c9 while

       g_MusicPos < 4      OR      g_PalFade >= 0xff60

   so it leaves only once the module has reached order 4 AND the fade
   has gone past -160. Each on its own is satisfied far too early: the
   order arrives 1156 ticks in, less than half way, and the fade test
   is true from the first tick because g_PalFade sits at 0 until
   something arms it [verified].

   What arms it is the music. Tick_RockIntro2 at 1000:638a writes
   0xff00 into g_PalFadeTarget2 once g_MusicPos >= 6, and
   Tick_Default's walk - which is linear at one a tick for this gap,
   subsystems/music.txt section 5 - then takes 161 ticks to
   carry g_PalFade across 0xff60. So:

       tick    4   order 1       order boundaries every 384 ticks
       tick 1088   the handoff   g_LogoZoom reaches 0, section 3
       tick 1924   order 6       g_PalFadeTarget2 = 0xff00
       tick 2085   the exit      g_PalFade = -161

   and the opening is 2,085 ticks, 36.6 s at 57 Hz [verified;
   g_EnvMapAngle read at the exit, which only this scene's handlers
   advance].

   THE SCREEN IS NOT FULLY BLACK AT THE CUT. -161 >> 2 is a bias of
   -41 against a 6-bit DAC, so a palette entry of 0x3f still reaches
   22 and the brightest pixels are a quarter lit when the runner
   returns. The next scene carries the fade the rest of the way.

   THE 380 TICKS BEFORE THE SCENE ARE NOT A CONSTANT. Order 1 lands
   on tick 4 rather than 384, so the module was already 380 ticks
   into order 0 when the runner started - that is Demo_Main's sphere
   map build, and on a faster machine it is shorter and the opening
   is correspondingly LONGER [observed; DOSBox].
