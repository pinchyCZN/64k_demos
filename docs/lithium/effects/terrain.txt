PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                      LITHIUM: THE PLANET SURFACE


Abstract

   THE SURFACE IS NOT A VOXEL RENDERER. It is a grid mesh, rebuilt
   every frame from a 128x128 heightmap and pushed through the same
   polygon pipeline as the ships and the tower.

   That is worth stating plainly because it looks like a voxel
   landscape and because XCURSION's equivalent IS one - a y-buffer
   span fill, ../../xcursion/effects/ground.txt. The two demos solve
   the same shot in completely different ways.

   Two routines: Terrain_Build at 1000:4b3c emits the grid,
   Terrain_Height at 1000:4067 gives each vertex its y. Everything
   here is [verified] from those two unless marked.

1.  Terrain_Build

   It does NOT go through Mesh_Load or Mesh_Transform. It writes
   16-byte vertex records straight into the pool in segment 0x2ad2 and
   increments g_VertPoolCount itself - subsystems/mesh3d.txt section 2
   is the record layout it is filling in.

       strips = ((g_SpanRowMax - g_SpanRowMin) >> 5) + 1
       per strip, per column:
           INC g_VertPoolCount
           ES:[DI+0x00] = x       stepping 0x100 along the strip
           ES:[DI+0x04] = z       stepping 0x100 between strips
           CALL Terrain_Height    which writes ES:[DI+0x02], the y
           DI += 0x10

   So the extent is driven by g_SpanRowMin and g_SpanRowMax, in steps
   of 0x20, and the mesh is REBUILT EVERY FRAME from wherever the
   camera is. An endless surface then costs nothing to store, which is
   the whole point in 64 KB.

   Before the grid it builds a camera-aligned quad into g_PolyArgs at
   CS:0xe1b2 - four (x, y) WORD pairs, a square about 128,128 turned
   by the camera's yaw and sized by its pitch - and calls
   Poly_ScanEdges on it at 1000:4c0c. THAT QUAD IS THE FOOTPRINT, not
   a backdrop: scan-converting it is what fills in the row range and
   the per-row x spans that the two passes below then walk.

   So the extent is not computed, it is RASTERISED. g_SpanRowMin and
   g_SpanRowMax are the span row range Poly_ScanEdges writes, held as
   row << 5 because the table is indexed by byte offset [verified].

   1.1.  How the face pass knows where each row starts

      The vertex pass emits rows of vertices and the face pass joins
      consecutive rows, so it has to know the pool index each row
      began at. The original passes that between the passes by
      STASHING THE VERTEX-POOL BYTE OFFSET INTO THE SPAN RECORD -
      1000:4c5c and 1000:4c61 write it into one row's +6 and the
      next row's +4, and 1000:4cdc and 1000:4ce1 read it back and
      shift right 4 to turn 16-byte records into indices [verified].

      Nothing else in the demo reuses a span that way, and the two
      words it borrows are part of the u interpolant that this call
      never needs - Poly_ScanEdges is a 2D scan and writes no u or v.

   1.2.  The face pass

          strips = (g_SpanRowMax - g_SpanRowMin) >> 5     one fewer
          per strip, per column of that row's own x span:
              g_FacePoolCount += 2
              triangle (b+1, a+1, a) and (b, b+1, a)
              both carry g_TerrainAttr from 1000:8fa6
              a += 1;  b += 1

      So two triangles a column, a and b being the two rows' bases.
      That answers what emits the faces: Terrain_Build does, in a
      second pass over the same spans.

   1.3.  A strip spans the union of two rows

      1000:4c51 takes the smaller x0 of this row and the last and
      1000:4c7b the larger x1, and the count is that difference plus
      two - so neighbouring rows always have a vertex to meet at.

   1.4.  The count depends on the PREVIOUS frame

      Poly_ClearSpans resets only the rows the last polygon touched -
      ../subsystems/raster.txt section 6.4 - so when Terrain_Build
      scans its quad the table still holds leftovers from the last
      polygon of the frame before, and those widen the x spans it
      reads back. The emitted count is therefore frame-history
      dependent, and reproducing it exactly needs the whole frame
      reproduced [verified: a clean table gives 302 vertices and 536
      faces for the flyover's camera, where the demo emits 336 and
      600].

2.  Terrain_Height, and the world scroll

       ax >>= 8;  bx >>= 8            world x and z to map units
       bh = al
       bh -= g_SlotPosX >> 0xc
       bl += g_SlotPosZ >> 0xc
       bl += bl;  bx >>= 1;  bx &= 0x3fff
       al = DS:[bx]                   THE HEIGHTMAP BYTE
       ES:[DI+0x02] = (al - 0x80) << 3

   THE INDEX TRICK IS A 7-BIT STRIDE. ADD BL,BL drops BL's top bit
   and SAR BX,1 halves the pair, so what comes out is
   (row & 0x7f) << 7 | (col & 0x7f): a 128-wide map that wraps on
   both axes, which the 0x3fff mask then bounds.

   U AND V ARE THE SLOPE, NOT A POSITION - 1000:40a4 onward:

       du = (h - h[+1])    << 9       the next column
       dv = (h - h[+0x80]) << 9       the next ROW
       each += 0x3f80, floored at 0 and capped at 0x7e81, then >> 7

   So every vertex's sphere-map coordinate is the local gradient and
   THE SURFACE IS SHADED BY ITS OWN RELIEF through the chrome map -
   which is the other half of why it reads as finer than its geometry
   [verified].

   The 0x3fff mask makes the map 0x4000 bytes, so it is 128x128, and
   0x80 is sea level with the height scaled by eight about it. The map
   wraps, which is the other half of why the surface is endless.

   g_SlotPosX AND g_SlotPosZ ARE THE COARSE WORLD POSITION, in units
   of 0x1000, and scrolling them is what flies the camera over the
   surface. Mesh_Transform applies the same two values to every
   object, shifted right by 0xc and left by 8, which is what keeps the
   ships and the tower moving with the ground -
   subsystems/mesh3d.txt section 3.2.

3.  Why it looks like a fine mesh

   Three things together, and none of them is a voxel step:

   o The grid is dense. Vertices are 0x100 world units apart and the
     strips 0x100 apart, so a surface filling the screen is hundreds
     of quads.
   o Every one of those polygons is TEXTURE-MAPPED, because there is
     no flat-fill path at all - subsystems/raster.txt section 4. So
     the surface carries detail finer than its own geometry.
   o The heightmap is 128x128 and wraps, so the relief repeats at a
     scale much larger than the grid.

   The banding a voxel renderer gives comes from its column stepping.
   Here the same look comes from the palette: a texel plus the face
   attribute's band offset lands in a 64-entry ramp, and g_Pal2's
   first two bands are blue and cyan dominant -
   subsystems/texture.txt section 7.

4.  Where it appears

   Terrain_Build is called from FOUR scene runners: 1000:18a3,
   1000:1c4f, 1000:21eb and 1000:2496 [verified].

   That matches the four scenes the surface appears in -
   scenes.txt 2.5, 2.8, 2.9 and 2.12: the first fly-over, the starship
   chase, the tower with the two ships circling, and the battle.

   So one terrain routine serves every surface shot, and what differs
   between them is the camera, the palette and which models are also
   in the pools.

5.  Where the heightmap comes from

   Terrain_Generate at 1000:52dd, once:

       g_RndState0, g_RndState1 = 0xa5b5, 0xcab6     A FIXED SEED
       DS = 0x4ad2
       Terrain_DiamondSquare                          256 x 256
       fill 20 rows of 20 bytes at 0x7676 with 0xff
       cx = 5;  Tex_Blur
       copy 0x80 rows of 0x80 bytes from 0x4014 to 0x1e5f:0

   THE HEIGHTMAP LIVES AT 0x1e5f:0000 - the asset segment - and that
   is what Terrain_Height indexes. It is generated in the BACK BUFFER
   and only the finished 128 x 128 window is copied out, so the
   working grid is twice the size of the result and the ragged edges
   of the diamond-square are discarded.

   THE SEED IS FIXED, so the landscape is identical on every run -
   the same trick the starfield uses, effects/sky.txt section 4.

   THE PORT'S MAP IS BYTE-IDENTICAL to the demo's across all 16,384
   bytes of the 128x128 window [verified against a dump of
   1e5f:0000]. That is the whole of Terrain_Generate,
   Terrain_DiamondSquare and Terrain_Displace confirmed at once,
   including the torus wrap and the five blurs.

   THE MAP IS NOT IN THE IMAGE. The asset segment reads as zeros
   there, so it is BSS that only this routine fills - a port that
   indexes the image instead gets a dead flat surface.

   THE PLATEAU IS WHERE THE TOWER STANDS. 0x7676 is row 118, column
   118 of the working grid, and 20 x 20 of 0xff is a flat maximum, so
   the tower has level ground under it.

   5.1.  Terrain_DiamondSquare

      1000:3ed6. Classic midpoint displacement:

          [0] = Rnd_Next
          g_TerrainStep = 0x8000;  g_TerrainHalfStep = 0x80
          g_TerrainRough = 1
          per level:
              sweep in steps of g_TerrainHalfStep * 2:
                  centre = the four corners averaged, displaced
                  four edge midpoints = each adjacent pair halved,
                      displaced
              step >>= 1;  halfstep >>= 1;  g_TerrainRough += 1
          until halfstep == 0

      THE ROUGHNESS SHIFT RISES BY ONE PER LEVEL and
      Terrain_Displace shifts its random by it, so each level
      displaces half as far as the last. Persistence 0.5, written as
      an increment rather than a multiply.

      IT WRAPS: a midpoint that would fall off the right edge is
      written at si - 0x100 instead, so the map is a torus and the
      relief repeats seamlessly. That is what lets the flight scenes
      run over it forever.

   5.2.  Terrain_Displace

      1000:4044.

          dx = ((Rnd_Next & 0x7fff) - 0x4000) >> g_TerrainRough
          ax += dx;  clamp to 0 .. 0xff

      Masking to 0x7fff and subtracting 0x4000 gets a signed offset
      out of an unsigned generator with no sign test.

   5.3.  The blur is Tex_Blur

      The five smoothing passes call 1000:4aee, which is the same
      routine that smooths the chrome environment map -
      ../subsystems/texture.txt section 4. One blur serves both the
      texture and the landscape.

6.  The surface's texture

   Segment 0x5ad2, and THE SURFACE IS NOT DRAWN WITH THE SPHERE MAP -
   which is how this was caught, because the demo's back buffer holds
   values above 0x7f and no sphere-map texel can.

   Tex_BuildGlowNoise at 1000:57e1 fills it: a radial falloff from
   (0x30, 0x30) with noise on top.

       d  = sq(|x - 0x30|) + sq(|y - 0x30|)      the square table
       si = Math_Sqrt(d << 2) >> 4
       ax = 0x104 - si - (si >> 2)
       ax += Rnd_Next() >> 0xd, then that >> 1 again
       clamp 0 to 0xff, and anything past 0xfc is black
       one Tex_Blur pass

   THEN THE CONTRAST IS HALVED AND THE FLOOR PINNED - 1000:5883. A
   byte below 0x81 is raised to it and every byte becomes
   0x81 + (v - 0x81) / 2, so the finished texture lives in 0x81 to
   0xc0. Leaving that pass out puts it about 17 too bright everywhere.

   6.1.  Math_BuildSquareTable and Math_Sqrt

      1000:5334 fills 1,600 dwords with (n+1) squared, and
      1000:4a91 is a BISECTION OVER THAT TABLE rather than an
      arithmetic root - it returns the byte offset of the closest
      entry, which is why callers shift the result down.

      IT OVERLAYS THE SPAN TABLE. 1,600 dwords is 6,400 bytes, which
      is exactly 200 rows of 32 at 1000:c8b2, and the squares are
      only wanted while the textures are generated and no polygon has
      been drawn. 1000:5354 then leaves the span row range at the
      whole screen so the first Poly_ClearSpans sweeps all of it.

   THE PORT'S TEXTURE MATCHES IN 65,535 OF 65,536 BYTES [verified].
   The one exception is byte 0, which the demo holds at 1 - below the
   floor this pass pins - so something later in the transition writes
   it. Flare_BuildSprite and Tex_BuildThreeGlows both target the same
   segment.

7.  Open questions

   o What face attribute ZERO means in Poly_Fill, which the tower is
     drawn with. g_TerrainAttr at 1000:8fa6 is 0x5ad2 for the grid.

   o THE CAMERA SINKS THROUGH THE SURFACE in the port if it is left
     running. Terrain_HeightAtSlot NEGATES the map byte and scales it
     by 1 << 7 where the grid's own vertices take it unnegated at
     1 << 3, so the two are in different frames, and which one the
     drawn camera should be compared against is not settled.
