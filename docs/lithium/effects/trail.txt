PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                   LITHIUM: THE TRAIL BLOBS


Abstract

   The lasers and the explosions of scene 2.12. 64 particles with a
   life counter, drawn as additive blobs out of the SAME cone sprite
   the lens flare uses.

   THE LIFE COUNTER DOES THREE JOBS: it says whether the particle is
   alive, it scales the blob's brightness, and ITS BOTTOM BIT CHOOSES
   WHICH BLITTER DRAWS IT - one that recolours what it hits and one
   that only brightens it.

   All [verified] from Trail_Step and Trail_Draw unless marked.
   effects/sky.txt section 6 is the blitter family.

1.  The ring

   64 records of 0x16 bytes at 0x8ad2:0x7a80, ending at 0x8000. Both
   routines walk it with the same bound, and Tex_SnapshotFrame clears
   exactly 0x580 bytes there - 1408, which is 64 x 22 [verified].

       +0x00  dword  position x       +0x0c  word  velocity x
       +0x04  dword  position y       +0x0e  word  velocity y
       +0x08  dword  position z       +0x10  word  velocity z
       +0x12  dword  THE LIFE

   THE SAME RING IS READ A SECOND WAY. Tick_Greetings writes words at
   +0x00, +0x02, +0x04, +0x06 and +0x08 and a dword at +0x0c, which
   does not match the layout above - effects/text.txt section 4. The
   records are 22 bytes either way; each effect imposes its own
   reading. Neither is the record's definition.

   1.1.  g_TrailActive decides whether any of it runs

      Both routines return at once while the byte at 1000:8fbc is
      zero, and the ONLY thing that raises it is Trail_Spawn at
      1000:5ebf. Trail_Spawn is reached from one place - the weapon
      tail of Flight_Step - and that needs g_SlotFiring, which only
      track command 0x19 sets. So the trails exist exactly where a
      track shoots, and nowhere else.

      TRAIL_DRAW RE-ARMS IT EVERY FRAME, which is how the ring
      switches itself off: 1000:26bd clears the byte and 1000:26d1
      puts it back if a record is still alive, so once the last life
      runs out both routines go quiet until the next shot.

      SO THE SURFACE FLYOVER HAS NO TRAILS, even though it calls
      Trail_Draw at 1000:1b5e every frame. Measured from the scene's
      entry to its end: the flag is never written and stays zero,
      and neither of the two slots the scene flies issues command
      0x19 - the camera uses 0, 2, 3, 0xe, 0xf, 0x10, 0x17 and 0x18
      and the lead craft 0, 6 and 8 [verified].

      IN THE BATTLE IT ARMS MID-SCENE, at 1000:5ebf, and from that
      frame on the clear-and-re-arm pair runs every frame
      [verified]. That scene and maybe_Scene_TowerFall are where
      this effect is worth porting.

2.  Trail_Step

   1000:6164, once a tick, called by all three track handlers.

       return if g_TrailActive == 0
       per record:
           position += velocity, on all three axes
           if [+0x12] & 1:
               velocity_y--                        GRAVITY
               if [+0x12] < 0xaa00:
                   [+0x12] = 0x28000               respawn
                   velocity = (0, 0x80, 0)
           [+0x12] -= 0x7d0, floored at 0
           di += 0x16

   0x28000 is the same constant Scene_ProjectVerts uses as its fog
   threshold.

3.  Trail_Draw

   1000:26a4, once a frame. Three callers, one per scene that needs
   it: maybe_Scene_SurfaceFlyover at 1000:1b5e, maybe_Scene_Battle at
   1000:21cd and maybe_Scene_TowerFall at 1000:2478.

       return if g_TrailActive == 0
       DS = 0x4ad2    the back buffer
       ES = 0x7ad2    THE FLARE SPRITE, reused
       FS = 0x8ad2    the ring
       g_TrailActive = 0
       per record:
           skip if life <= 0
           g_TrailActive = 1
           g_BlobLife = life
           position less the camera, x and y negated, SAR 4
           rotate by the camera angles, perspective divide
           edx = g_BlobLife / z, capped at 0xfe, THEN INVERTED
           g_FlareColour = ~dl
           g_SunSavedX, g_SunSavedY = 0xa0, 0x64
           if g_BlobLife & 1: Blob_DrawBurn else Blob_DrawKeepBand

   g_TrailActive IS A LATCH, not a setting. Trail_Draw clears it and
   sets it again only if it drew something, so a frame with nothing
   alive costs one compare here and makes the next Trail_Step return
   at once. Neither routine has to count the living.

   THE BRIGHTNESS IS INVERTED BEFORE USE. g_BlobLife / z is capped at
   0xfe and then complemented, and the blitters SUBTRACT
   g_FlareColour from the sprite - so a larger quotient, meaning a
   closer or younger particle, leaves more of the sprite above the
   cutoff and the blob comes out bigger and brighter.

   IT BORROWS THE FLARE'S PLUMBING. The blitters add g_SunSavedX and
   g_SunSavedY to the offset they are handed, so setting those to
   0xa0, 0x64 - the centre of the screen - makes them place the blob
   at centre plus projection. Nothing about the flare is involved
   beyond the shared globals and the shared sprite.

   3.1.  The projection is done by hand

      img 0x26ef subtracts slot 0's position, NEGATES x and y,
      shifts all three down 4, and rotates Y then X then Z with a
      SAR 0xc between - the camera transform's order at a different
      scale, because a particle is a point and carries no vertex
      record.

      THE NEAR TEST IS A REJECT, img 0x275e, where every other
      projection in the demo clamps. A blob behind the camera is
      dropped rather than smeared across the screen.

      The divide is the usual 64-bit dividend, img 0x276a -
      subsystems/raster.txt section 2.

4.  Two blitters, one bit apart

   Blob_DrawKeepBand at 1000:48a3 and Blob_DrawBurn at 1000:4943 are
   the same routine except for one instruction:

       Blob_DrawKeepBand    dl &= 0xc0;  dh |= dl      keep the band
       Blob_DrawBurn        dh |= 0xc0                 FORCE band 3

   Both take al = sprite - g_FlareColour, skip the pixel if that
   borrows, shift it right by 3 for a 0 to 0x1f intensity, add it to
   the destination's low six bits and saturate at 0x3f.

   SO ONE BRIGHTENS AND THE OTHER RECOLOURS. A pixel Blob_DrawBurn
   touches ends up in the top 64-entry band whatever it was before,
   which is how a bolt reads as its own colour rather than as a
   brightening of the terrain under it.

   BIT 0 OF THE LIFE PICKS BETWEEN THEM, AND IT IS A TYPE FLAG RATHER
   THAN A PHASE. Trail_Step decrements the life by 0x7d0 and respawns
   it at 0x28000, both even, so the bit never changes for the life of
   a particle.

   IT IS THE SAME BIT THAT GATES GRAVITY in Trail_Step. So one bit of
   the life counter defines two kinds of particle:

   +-------+-----------+---------------------+
   | Bit 0 | Falls     | Drawn with          |
   +=======+===========+=====================+
   | 1     | yes       | Blob_DrawBurn       |
   | 0     | no        | Blob_DrawKeepBand   |
   +-------+-----------+---------------------+

   A heavy bolt that falls and burns its own colour, and a weightless
   spark that drifts and only brightens - told apart by the low bit of
   a counter that is otherwise a lifetime.

   SINCE THE RESPAWN VALUE IS EVEN, a recycled record always comes
   back as the weightless kind.

5.  The weapon that fills it

   The tail of Flight_Step, at 1000:5dc0 - the same routine that
   eases the attitude and applies the throttle, effects/flight.txt.

       if g_SlotFiring == 0: return          set by command 0x19
       cs:[si + 0x8ead]++
       return unless (that & 7) == 0          ONE TICK IN EIGHT
       Trail_Spawn
       [di+0x12] = 0x32000                    an EVEN life
       velocity = (0, 0x80, 0)
       eax = Terrain_HeightAtSlot + 0x280
       if the slot's y is still above eax: return
       cx = 0x32;  Trail_SpawnBurst           FIFTY particles
       zero the slot's position and velocity
       stop firing

   A BOLT EVERY EIGHTH TICK, THEN FIFTY PARTICLES WHERE IT LANDS.
   The bolt's life is even, so it is the weightless kind - a tracer
   that brightens what it crosses and does not fall. The fifty are
   odd-lifed, so they fall and burn their own colour.

   THE IMPACT TEST IS AGAINST THE TERRAIN. Terrain_HeightAtSlot at
   1000:4107 reads the height field under the slot, and the bolt is
   spent once it comes within 0x280 of it - so the explosion happens
   at the ground rather than at a fixed depth.

   5.1.  Trail_Spawn

      1000:5eba. It fills in only the POSITION, from the slot in SI,
      and leaves DI on the record - all three callers write the life
      and the velocity on the next instruction.

      A WRAPPING CURSOR IS THE WHOLE ALLOCATOR. g_TrailWriteCursor
      advances 0x16 and resets at 0x8000; there is no free list and
      no search for a dead record, so the 65th spawn overwrites the
      first whether it is alive or not.

   5.2.  The parity is forced, not chanced

      Trail_SpawnBurst at 1000:5e4d, CX times:

          Trail_Spawn
          ax = Rnd_NextB & 0xfffe
          [di+0x12] = ax + 0x38401
          vel x,z  = Rnd_NextB SAR 9       signed, so either way
          vel y    = Rnd_NextB SHR 8       UNSIGNED, so always up

      MASKING THE RANDOM EVEN AND ADDING AN ODD CONSTANT MAKES THE
      LIFE ODD EVERY TIME. Section 4 is what that bit means; this is
      the author making sure of it rather than leaving it to the
      generator.

      Trail_SpawnBurstBig at 1000:5e82 is the same with + 0x80001,
      velocities shifted by 7 instead of 9 and +0x80 on the vertical
      - a larger, longer-lived, rising burst.

   5.3.  A second generator

      Both bursts use Rnd_NextB at 1000:3e16, which is byte-for-byte
      the same algorithm as Rnd_Next on its own state.

      THE SEPARATION IS NECESSARY. Sky_SunAndStars reseeds the main
      generator with fixed constants every single frame to regenerate
      the starfield - effects/sky.txt section 4 - and Flicker_Step
      perturbs it from its own contents. Either would destroy an
      explosion's randomness if the weapon shared that state.

      So what must look identical every frame uses Rnd_Next, and what
      must never repeat uses Rnd_NextB.
