PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                      LITHIUM: THE GLASS SHATTER


Abstract

   The screen breaks into 140 textured triangles that tumble away
   under gravity - and the same code, run with one flag set, flies
   them back together into the picture.

   THE ASSEMBLE IS NOT AUTHORED. Shatter_Build plays the explosion
   through to its end off-screen, negates every velocity, and starts
   there. The inward flight is the outward one reversed, which is why
   it costs no extra code and lands every shard exactly on its cell.

   The effect carries no image of its own. It samples segment 0x5ad2,
   the buffer the title picture and the text renderer compose into -
   effects/text.txt section 5 - so whatever the scene last drew is
   what breaks.

   All [verified] from the four routines unless marked.

1.  The routines

       Shatter_Build      1000:2803  tile the screen, set the start
       Shatter_InitShard  1000:2c78  one shard's velocities
       Shatter_Step       1000:2c05  one tick of the simulation
       Shatter_Draw       1000:2ac1  project, texture and fill
       Scene_Shatter      1000:2919  the scene runner
       Tick_Shatter       1000:644e  the tick handler it installs

   Shatter_Build has two callers: Scene_Shatter, and 1000:094e inside
   maybe_Scene_BumpShatter - so the effect appears twice.

2.  Lith_Shard

   0x30 bytes, at 0x2ad2:0x4000 + 0x30n, g_ShardCount of them.

       +0x00  short[6]  three local corners, x,y pairs, all +/-16
       +0x0c  short[3]  position x, y, z
       +0x12  short[3]  rotation x, y, z
       +0x18  short[3]  angular velocity
       +0x1e  short[3]  velocity
       +0x24  short[6]  three source u,v - where it came from

   Every offset is touched by Shatter_Step or Shatter_Draw, so the
   layout is read off use rather than inferred from spacing.

   THE CORNERS ARE A QUAD CUT IN TWO. A cell emits two records:

       (-16,-16)  (+16,-16)  (-16,+16)
       (-16,+16)  (+16,-16)  (+16,+16)

   which is a 32 x 32 square split along its diagonal.

3.  Shatter_Build, and the reversal

   1000:2803.

       DS = 0x2ad2, DI = 0x4000, g_ShardCount = 0
       for dx = -0x54 to 0x74 step 0x20            7 rows
         for cx = -0x90 to 0xb0 step 0x20          10 columns
           emit the two records above
           source u,v = cx + 0x90, dx + 0x54
           Shatter_InitShard
           g_ShardCount++

   7 x 10 x 2 is 140 shards. The origins 0x90 and 0x54 are 144 and 84,
   a 320 x 200 screen less the 16-pixel half-cell, so the grid is
   centred.

   3.1.  The velocities are not noise

      Shatter_InitShard at 1000:2c78:

          pos   = (cx, dx, 0x100)
          angle = (0, 0, 0)
          angvel_{x,y,z} = Rnd_Next >> 7
          vel_x = (Rnd_Next >> 0xc) + (cx >> 6)
          vel_y = (Rnd_Next >> 0xc) + (dx >> 6)
                                    + ((|cx| - 0xa0) >> 3)
          vel_z = ((0x104 - (|cx| + |dx|)) >> 4) + 5

      EACH VELOCITY CARRIES A TERM IN THE SHARD'S OWN GRID POSITION,
      so shards far from the centre leave faster and the sheet opens
      outward. The random part only breaks up the regularity. A pure
      random velocity would dissolve the picture instead of bursting
      it.

      Z IS A CONE AND CARRIES NO RANDOM AT ALL, img 0x2ce1 to
      0x2cfd: both shards of a cell get the same one and it falls
      off with the distance from the grid's centre, from 19 in the
      middle to 5 at the corners. So the sheet does not merely open
      outward - its middle comes at the camera hardest.

      The tumble, by contrast, is pure noise - there is no positional
      term in angvel at all.

      THE WHOLE BUILD IS BYTE-EXACT against the emulator over all
      6,720 bytes of the array, seeded from the generator's state at
      img 0x2803: the grid bounds, the diagonal split, the per-corner
      source u,v, the three angular randoms and all three velocities
      [verified]. Five Rnd_Next calls a shard, in that order.

   3.2.  The reversal

      If g_ShatterAssemble is set, Shatter_Build does not return after
      building. It runs the whole effect:

          do { Shatter_Step x5;  Shatter_Draw }
          while g_ShardsOnScreen != 0
          per shard:  negate vel and angvel;  vel_y++
          Mem_ClearSegment 0x4ad2 and 0xa000

      THE SHARDS ARE LEFT WHERE THEY ENDED UP, carrying the exact
      velocities that took them there, reversed. Running forward from
      that state retraces the explosion backwards and every shard
      lands on its own cell.

      THE INC AT 1000:2903 IS THE GRAVITY CORRECTION, not a fudge. A
      forward step is pos += vel and then vel_y += 1, so the state
      that undoes it needs vel_y' = -(vel_y - 1) = -vel_y + 1.
      Negating alone leaves every shard a pixel a tick adrift and the
      sheet does not close.

      THE FIVE STEPS ARE A FAST-FORWARD. Only the draw refreshes
      g_ShardsOnScreen, so the loop samples the test once per five
      ticks of simulation rather than once per tick - the live effect
      steps once a tick. The pre-run draws into a buffer that is then
      cleared, so none of it reaches the screen.

      g_ShatterAssemble 1000:8f85 IS SET IN ONE PLACE: Demo_Main at
      1000:0305, immediately before the Scene_Shatter that transitions
      into the battle. maybe_Scene_BumpShatter clears it at 1000:0948
      for the forward break, and nothing else writes it.

4.  Shatter_Step

   1000:2c05, one tick, every shard.

       per Lith_Shard:
           pos   += vel
           vel_y++                     gravity, one unit a tick
           angle += angvel
           if pos_z <= 0x100:  zero vel and angvel
           bp += 0x30

   0x100 IS WHERE A SHARD BELONGS. Shatter_InitShard starts every
   shard at pos_z = 0x100, so in the assembling direction a shard
   flies in, reaches its home plane and stops dead. That one test is
   the whole arrival mechanism; nothing else checks whether a shard
   has got home.

5.  Shatter_Draw

   1000:2ac1.

       g_ShardsOnScreen = 0
       per Lith_Shard:
           g_RotAngleY = angle_x       NOTE THE SWAP
           g_RotAngleX = angle_y
           g_RotAngleZ = angle_z
           for each of the three corners:
               eax, ebx = the corner, ecx = 0
               Vec_RotY, SAR 9;  Vec_RotX, SAR 9;  Vec_RotZ, SAR 9
               add pos_{x,y,z} << 9
               ecx = max(ecx, 0x3e8)
               x = ((eax << 8) / ecx) + 0xa0
               y = ((ebx << 8) / ecx) + 0x64
               if x <= 0x140 and y <= 0xc8: g_ShardsOnScreen++
               g_PolyArgs gets x, y and the source u,v, the latter
               clamped to 0x13f and 0xc7
           GS = 0x5ad2, g_SpanRoutine = 0x3c46, Poly_Fill

   The near clamp at 0x3e8 is what stops a shard that has passed the
   camera from dividing by something near zero.

   g_ShardsOnScreen IS THE TERMINATION TEST, counted here and read by
   both callers: the shatter runs until no projected corner lands
   inside 320 x 200.

   THE TWO BOUNDS ARE NOT TESTED ALIKE. 1000:2b94 is a JA, unsigned,
   so a shard off the LEFT edge fails it, while 1000:2b99 is a JG,
   signed, so a shard above the TOP edge still counts. The effect
   therefore runs on while anything is above the screen and stops the
   moment the last shard leaves it sideways.

   THE FILL IS Poly_Fill through the fifth span routine, 1000:3c46,
   whose texture is a screen at stride 320 rather than a 256 x 256
   map - subsystems/raster.txt section 3.4.

   Build, Step and Draw are byte-exact against the emulator: the
   whole 6,720-byte array after a build and after a step, and all
   64,000 bytes of the frame the draw composes, measured on the
   106-shard prefix described below [verified].

5.1.  The draw races the tick

   THE SHARDS MOVE WHILE THE DRAW RUNS. Tick_Shatter is installed on
   the 57 Hz handler and calls Shatter_Step, and a full 140-shard
   pass takes longer than a tick - so the array is one step ahead for
   every shard drawn after the interrupt lands. Dumping 0x2ad2:0x4000
   at 1000:2ac1 and again at the RET of the same pass shows exactly
   one step applied, 1,173 bytes changed [verified].

   THE BUILD ITSELF IS OUTSIDE THE RACE. Scene_Shatter installs
   Tick_Shatter at img 0x291c, one instruction after Shatter_Build
   returns, so nothing steps the array while the build and its
   reverse pass run. The 6,720 bytes read at img 0x2923, the loop's
   first instruction, are reproducible, and that is where a build
   capture is taken - a halt already inside the loop has the array
   part-flown and cannot serve [verified].

   A frame of the shatter is therefore NOT internally consistent, and
   where the seam falls moves with the CPU. Two captures of the same
   pass from the same starting point differ by thousands of pixels. The
   first 106 shards are what the original gets through before the
   first tick at the measured speed, and that prefix is reproducible.

   This is the one place in the demo where a deterministic
   reconstruction cannot match a full-frame capture, and it is a
   property of the original rather than a gap in the port.

   The store addresses pin g_PolyArgs at 1000:e1b2 - x and y as words
   at +0x00 with stride 4, the source u at +0x14 and v at +0x24 as
   dwords - which agrees with g_FlickerTable sitting at 0xe1e6, four
   bytes past its end.

5.2.  The shards come back

   A SHARD'S POSITION IS THREE WORDS AND THEY WRAP. Shatter_Step
   adds the velocity straight into them at 1000:2c05 with no bound,
   and gravity adds 1 to the y velocity every tick - so y grows as
   half the square of the tick count and crosses 32767 about 252
   ticks after the build.

   The wrapped shards sweep back UP through the screen as a scatter
   of tiny triangles. Phase four ends at 293 ticks - section 6 of
   effects/rock.txt - so the original shows that for about forty
   ticks and then leaves; it is visible, and it is not a defect to
   be bounded away. A reconstruction that runs the phase on shows it
   for as long as it runs.

   THE DRAW NEVER STOPS EITHER. Nothing tests g_ShardsOnScreen on
   this path - the phase ends on the ROCK - so Shatter_Draw walks
   all 140 shards every frame from the break to the end of the shot,
   long after the glass has left the screen. At 1000:2b6b that costs
   nothing visible because the projection divides a 64-bit dividend
   and the shards land far below the screen; narrowing it to 32 bits
   is what puts them back ON it - subsystems/raster.txt section 2.

6.  Scene_Shatter

   1000:2919, called from the main script at 1000:030b.

       Shatter_Build
       g_TickHandler = Tick_Shatter
       loop:  Dirty_Reset;  Shatter_Draw;  cs:[0x8ffd] = 0x80808080
              Screen_PresentDirty;  Demo_PollKeys
       until: g_ShatterAssemble == 1 ? shard 0's pos_z <= 0x100
                                     : g_ShardsOnScreen == 0
       if assembling: Screen_BlitFull with AX = 0x5ad2

   THE TWO DIRECTIONS END ON DIFFERENT TESTS. The shatter ends when
   nothing is left on screen; the assemble ends when the FIRST shard
   reaches its home plane, read straight out of 0x2ad2:0x4010.

   g_PresentFill at 1000:8ffd IS THE ERASE COLOUR, not a flag.
   Screen_PresentDirty blits the dirty box and then fills that same
   box in 0x4ad2 with this dword, 1000:35ce - so a scene chooses
   what its background clears to, and 0x80808080 is the mid grey the
   shards fly over. maybe_Scene_BumpShatter sets 0 instead, because
   the tunnel covers every pixel and has nothing to clear to.

   6.1.  The script uploads the DAC itself, and the order matters

      1000:02ed sets the fade to -0x40, 1000:02f4 sets the TARGET to
      the same value, 1000:02fb calls Pal_Upload, and only then does
      1000:02fe point g_PalSource at g_Pal1.

      THE EXPLICIT UPLOAD IS LOAD-BEARING. Tick_Default refreshes the
      DAC only while the fade is still travelling - 1000:6d08 tests
      fade against target - and this scene sets the two equal, so
      nothing in the loop will ever upload. Without that one call the
      DAC keeps whatever the tunnel faded it to, which is black, and
      the whole assemble plays into a palette of zeros.

      SETTING THE SOURCE AFTERWARDS IS ALSO DELIBERATE. The upload
      has already happened, so the assemble is shown in the palette
      the TUNNEL left; g_Pal1 does not reach the DAC until the
      white-out moves the fade again - section 5.2 of
      effects/battle.txt.

   6.2.  The picture is pre-rendered

      Tex_SnapshotFrame at 1000:2496 builds it before the transition
      starts - subsystems/texture.txt - so what the shards assemble
      into is a frame of the battle's opening view that was never on
      screen. It puts slots 0 and 4 at 0x46000, fills 0x4ad2 with
      0x80808080, builds the terrain and draws it with the sky and
      the flare, then copies all 64,000 bytes into 0x5ad2.

      THE BLIT AT 1000:295b IS WHAT SHOWS IT WHOLE. The assembled
      shards are 140 triangles with seams; the scene ends by putting
      the original 0x5ad2 on screen, so the seams never show.
