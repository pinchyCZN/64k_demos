PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                   LITHIUM: THE RECORDED MOTION TRACKS


Abstract

   The demo's animation is RECORDED MOUSE INPUT, and the tool that
   recorded it ships inside the binary.

   Track_Play at 1000:5efe replays a three-byte-per-entry track into
   one slot of the parameter bank. Track_Record at 1000:5f56 captures
   the same format from INT 33h and the keyboard. Both read and write
   the asset segment, so the recording was done in the demo itself and
   whatever was in the buffer when the author stopped is what shipped.

   All [verified] from the two routines unless marked.
   subsystems/objects.txt is the bank they drive.

1.  The track format

   Three bytes per entry, consumed one entry every six frames:

       +0x00   signed byte   a horizontal delta
       +0x01   signed byte   a vertical delta
       +0x02   byte          packed: bits 0-5 a COMMAND, section 5,
                             bits 6-7 the throttle - flight.txt 3

   The deltas are scaled by 32 and ACCUMULATED, not assigned, so a
   track stores RATES OF TURN rather than absolute angles. That is
   what makes it mouse data: a mouse reports movement, never position.
   Flight_Step is what turns those rates into motion - flight.txt.

   A track lives in the asset segment 0x1e5f, and g_SlotTrack holds
   one offset per slot. 1000:18a3 hands out 0x7aca, 0x74fd and 0x809a,
   and 0x74fd is EXACTLY where the last model ends - img 0x15aed,
   ../formats/mesh.txt section 4. So the tracks were appended to the
   asset blob after the geometry.

   TWO OF THOSE THREE POINTERS LAND ON ZEROS. That is not a parse
   error: those slots have no recording. Only 0x7aca has data at its
   start.

2.  Track_Play

       g_SlotTrackTick[slot]++
       if it is still <= 5: return
       g_SlotTrackTick[slot] = 0
       di = g_SlotTrack[slot]
       g_SlotPitchAcc[slot] -= (signed) gs:[di+1] << 5
       g_SlotYawAcc[slot]   += (signed) gs:[di+0] << 5
       maybe_g_SlotKeyIdx[slot]  = gs:[di+2] & 0x3f
       g_SlotThrottle[slot] = gs:[di+2] >> 6
       g_SlotTrack[slot] += 3

   SIX FRAMES PER ENTRY is the number to keep. At a nominal 70 Hz that
   is about 12 entries a second, so a ten-second shot is 360 bytes -
   which is why appending the tracks to a 63 KB file was affordable.

   g_SlotPitchAcc and g_SlotYawAcc are two of the eleven word arrays
   in the bank, and Obj_Reset zeroes both, so a scene starts every
   slot's accumulated orientation at zero and the track steers it from
   there.

   NOTE THE SIGN ASYMMETRY: pitch is SUBTRACTED and yaw is ADDED. That
   is a screen-space convention, not a bug.

3.  Track_Record, and the author's keyboard

       g_SlotTrackTick[slot]++, return unless it passed 5
       al = IN from port 0x60                  the raw scancode
       scan g_RecScancodes for al, index into dl
       maybe_g_SlotKeyIdx[slot] = dl
       di = g_SlotTrack[slot]
       INT 33h AX=000Bh                        mouse motion counters
       cx = (cx + 4) >> 3                      horizontal
       dx = (dx + 4) >> 3                      vertical
       gs:[di] = cl;  gs:[di+1] = dl

   The +4 before the >>3 is rounding to nearest rather than
   truncating, which matters for a signed delta that is usually small.

   g_RecScancodes at 1000:904a is twenty-four raw AT scancodes:

       01 Esc      34 .        35 /        1e A
       2c Z        2d X        2e C        2f V
       1c Enter    36 RShift   02 1        03 2
       04 3        05 4        06 5        07 6
       08 7        09 8        0a 9        0b 0
       0c -        0d =        0e Backspace    39 Space

   Twenty-four entries fit the six bits a track entry allows. This is
   THE AUTHOR'S OWN FLYING AND CUEING KEYBOARD, left in the shipped
   binary - the number row for picking something, Z X C V and A under
   the left hand, Enter and Space for events.

   RECORD AND PLAY ARE TWO STATES OF ONE MACHINE, not two builds.
   Track_Record has a single call site at 1000:65e7, inside the
   per-tick dispatch region, and the block around it sets g_RecordMode
   to 1 while the playback block at 1000:6612 clears it.

4.  Who plays what

   Track_Play has FOURTEEN call sites, in three consecutive runs
   [verified]:

   +-------------+--------+----------------------------------------+
   | 1000:63cb   | 3      | three slots driven from tracks         |
   | 1000:661a   | 8      | EIGHT slots - the largest cast         |
   | 1000:679b   | 3      | three slots                            |
   +-------------+--------+----------------------------------------+

   Eight simultaneous tracks is the battle - scenes.txt 2.12 counts
   three enemy craft and two gold starships, plus the tower and the
   camera [inferred from the count alone].

5.  The key index is a command set

   The six-bit half of byte 2 is g_SlotCommand, and Flight_Step
   dispatches on it at 1000:5bac with a chain of compares - there is
   no table:

   +-------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | Command     | Effect on the slot                             |
   +=============+================================================+
   | 0x02 / 0x03 | roll +/- 4, then translate along               |
   |             | (g_SlotSpeed, 0, 0) rotated by pitch and yaw,  |
   |             | SAR 7 - A BANKED STRAFE                        |
   | 0x01        | g_SceneDone = 1 - END THE SCENE                |
   | 0x04 / 0x05 | g_SlotFlightY +/- g_SlotSpeed << 4             |
   | 0x06 / 0x07 | g_SlotRollRest -/+ 0x20                        |
   | 0x08        | ease g_SlotRollRest toward the nearest 0x2000  |
   |             | by a 16th, snapping when the step reaches zero |
   | 0x0a - 0x17 | g_SlotSpeed = (cmd - 9) * 2 - 1, 14 speeds     |
   | 0x18        | rotate (0,0,-0xa0) by the slot's angles, add   |
   |             | a fixed base, and WRITE the slot's position    |
   | 0x19        | g_SlotFiring = 1 - FIRE                        |
   | 0x1a        | copy this slot into slot 0x10 and set          |
   |             | g_TowerFalling - THE TOWER IS HIT              |
   +-------------+------------------------------------------------+

   SO THE DEMO HAS NO EVENT LIST IN CODE. The author flew each craft
   with the mouse and pressed keys as he went; the keystrokes went
   into the track beside the mouse deltas, and replaying the track
   replays the whole performance - when a ship fires, when it changes
   speed, when a scene ends, when the tower starts to fall.

   THAT IS WHY COMMAND 0x1A NAMES NO TARGET. It copies whichever slot
   executed it into the tower's slot, so the tower falls from where
   the shot came rather than from a position written down anywhere -
   effects/towerfall.txt section 3.

   0x02 AND 0x03 ARE THE ONLY SIDEWAYS MOTION. Their vector is
   (speed, 0, 0) - the craft's own X axis - where the throttle
   section of the same routine uses (0, 0, speed), straight ahead. A
   track made of one of them repeated is a craft flying a circle, and
   two of the battle's eight are exactly that - effects/battle.txt
   section 3.

   Command 0x08's snap is worth a note: it eases toward
   (roll + 0x1000) & 0xe000, which is the nearest eighth of a
   revolution, and assigns the target outright once the eased step
   rounds to zero. So "level out" converges exactly rather than
   creeping forever.

6.  Why this matters for the reconstruction

   IT MEANS THE ANIMATION CANNOT BE DERIVED, ONLY READ. There is no
   flight model to transcribe for these slots and no curve to fit - the
   motion is a byte stream someone recorded with a mouse in 1997.
   sim/lithium/ can replay it exactly, because the bytes are in the
   image, but nothing about it can be regenerated or tidied.

   It also sets the shape of the work left: for each of the three runs
   in section 4, find which slot gets which g_SlotTrack offset, and the
   scene's choreography falls out of the data rather than out of the
   code.

   XCURSION does the same thing - ../../xcursion/effects/flight.txt
   records that its mode 0x16 replays a recorded flight track - so
   this is a house technique across both demos rather than a one-off.

7.  Open questions

   o WHERE EACH TRACK ENDS. Nothing in Track_Play bounds the pointer,
     so either a track runs until the scene ends on music position, or
     a terminator exists that has not been found.

   o How the eased angles reach g_SlotAngle*. Flight_Step's tail
     eases cs:[si+0x8af9] toward g_SlotPitch and cs:[si+0x8b27]
     toward g_SlotYaw, each by a quarter - 1000:5d9b - but what reads
     those two arrays has not been found. They are two more slots of
     the same 0x2e-stride bank as g_SlotAngle*.
