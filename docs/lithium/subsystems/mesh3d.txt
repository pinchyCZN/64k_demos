PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                      LITHIUM: THE 3D PIPELINE


Abstract

   How a model becomes a screenful. Mesh_Load reads it and
   Mesh_Transform rotates it into two shared scene pools; raster.txt
   is the other half, where those pools become pixels.

   THE CHROME IS SPHERE MAPPING, and the whole trick is five
   instructions wide: the vertex normal is rotated into view space,
   shifted right by 0x11 and biased by +0x80, which lands it in a
   256-wide texture centred on 128. effects/scenes.txt scenes 1, 2 and
   14 are all this.

   Everything here is [verified] from the routines unless marked.
   formats/mesh.txt is the on-disk format; this is what runs.

1.  The shape of it

       Mesh_Load         1000:503a   byte-packed model -> work records
                                     and averaged per-vertex normals
       Mesh_Transform    1000:4d2c   one model -> the two scene pools
       Vec_RotZ          1000:35e1   rotate about Z
       Vec_RotX          1000:362a   rotate about X
       Vec_RotY          1000:3674   rotate about Y

   A scene is built by setting the per-object parameters and calling
   Mesh_Transform once per object. NOTHING IN IT CLEARS THE POOLS -
   g_VertPoolCount and g_FacePoolCount only ever grow - so the clear
   belongs to whatever drives a frame, and that has not been found.

2.  The two scene pools

   +--------------+---------+---------------------+---------------------+
   | Pool         | Segment | Stride              | Index               |
   +==============+=========+=====================+=====================+
   | vertices     | 0x2ad2  | 16 bytes            | g_VertPoolCount     |
   | faces        | 0x8ad2  | 8 bytes             | g_FacePoolCount     |
   +--------------+---------+---------------------+---------------------+

   A vertex record:

       +0x00  word   x, view space
       +0x02  word   y
       +0x04  word   z
       +0x06  word   not written here
       +0x08  word   u, the env-map column
       +0x0a  word   v, the env-map row
       +0x0c  ..     not written here

   A face record:

       +0x00  word   vertex index 0, REBASED into the pool
       +0x02  word   vertex index 1
       +0x04  word   vertex index 2
       +0x06  word   g_ObjFaceAttr, whatever the object sets

   The rebasing is what makes the pools shared: each model's indices
   have the pool position the model started at added to them, so one
   draw pass can walk every face of every object.

   SEGMENT 0x2ad2 IS ALSO MESH_LOAD'S WORK BUFFER. It is NOT the S3M
   module's base, however close the numbers look: a segment written
   S in the listing is image offset (S - 0x1000) * 16, so 0x2ad2 is
   image offset 0x1ad20 and the module sits at 0x2ad20, 64 KB past
   it. Measured by breaking at img 0x2e66, where Scene_ProjectVerts
   has just loaded DS: it reads 0x1c77, and 0x1c77 less the 0x1a5
   load base is image offset 0x1ad20. formats/mesh.txt section 5.

3.  Mesh_Transform, step by step

   3.1.  The cull

      Before anything:

          if |g_CurPosX - g_SlotHomeX| > 0x20000: return
          if |g_CurPosZ - g_SlotHomeZ| > 0x20000: return

      A box test on X and Z only, so Y is not culled. It returns
      having appended NOTHING, which means a culled object does not
      advance either pool index.

   3.2.  The position

          rotate Z by -(g_ObjAngleZ * 2)      then SAR 0xa
          rotate X by -g_ObjAngleX            then SAR 0xa
          rotate Y by -g_ObjAngleY
          SAR by g_ObjScaleShift
          x -= g_CurPosX >> 4
          y -= g_CurPosY >> 4
          z += g_CurPosZ >> 4
          x += (g_SlotPosX >> 0xc) << 8
          z += ((-(g_SlotPosZ + 1)) >> 0xc) << 8

      Note the Z ANGLE IS DOUBLED and all three are NEGATED, and that
      z is ADDED to where x and y are subtracted.

      g_ObjScaleShift = 16 MAKES THE WHOLE CHAIN THE IDENTITY. Three
      rotations by zero multiply by 4096 three times and two SAR 0xa
      take 20 bits back off, leaving the coordinate shifted left by
      16 - so a shift of 16 returns the authored byte exactly. Checked
      against all five models [verified]. A shift of 0 instead wipes
      the model: every coordinate is then a multiple of 0x10000 and
      the word store keeps nothing.

      THE LAST TWO TERMS ARE THE CAMERA, AND THEY ARE WHAT MAKES THE
      PLACEMENT RELATIVE. g_SlotPosX and g_SlotPosZ are read AT SLOT
      0, which every scene uses for its camera, so the object's own
      position comes off and the camera's goes back on: the model
      lands camera-relative without a view matrix anywhere.

      THE ADD-BACK IS QUANTISED TO WHOLE 4096-UNIT CELLS by the
      >> 0xc then << 8, and that cell is the one Terrain_Build is
      centred on - so what Scene_ProjectVerts is handed as the camera
      position is only the SUB-CELL REMAINDER, 1000:1b0e. The two
      halves have to agree or nothing lines up.

      READING THEM AS A FUDGE FACTOR IS THE TRAP, and it is a quiet
      one. Left at zero, a model is drawn at its ABSOLUTE position,
      which the three rotations then multiply by 64 - so a craft a few
      thousand units out lands past g_DepthFar and simply is not
      there, while one that happens to fall near the near plane
      smears across the screen as a few long triangles. Neither looks
      like a placement bug.

      THE Z TERM'S NEUTRAL VALUE IS -1, NOT 0, because of the INC then
      NEG - so a scene with no camera offset passes -1 and not zero.

   3.3.  The normal, and the map

      Section 5.

   3.4.  The faces

      Three words of vertex index, each with the model's pool base
      added, then g_ObjFaceAttr. g_ObjMirror additionally swaps the
      first and third index, which reverses the winding - consistent
      with it also negating the map coordinates.

4.  The rotations and the sine table

   All three rotations have one form. For Vec_RotZ, with x in EAX, y
   in EBX and z in ECX:

       di  = g_RotAngleZ & 0x1ffc
       sin = g_SinTable[di]
       cos = g_SinTable[di + 0x800]
       x'  = x*cos - y*sin
       y'  = y*cos + x*sin
       z <<= 0xc

   Vec_RotX mixes y and z and shifts x; Vec_RotY mixes x and z and
   shifts y. The untouched component is shifted left by 0xc so it
   keeps the same scale as the two that were multiplied, and THE
   PRODUCTS ARE LEFT AT FULL SCALE - a caller shifts them back itself,
   which is why Mesh_Transform uses SAR 0xa for positions and SAR 0xc
   for normals.

   g_SinTable at 1000:a0b2 holds 2048 dword entries, a FULL period, at
   12-bit fixed point so 4096 is 1.0. Hence:

   o A FULL CIRCLE IS 2048 ANGLE UNITS, and the mask 0x1ffc both wraps
     the angle and drops its low two bits, because the entries are
     dwords.
   o COSINE IS SINE 512 ENTRIES ON, which is +0x800 bytes and 90
     degrees.
   o The 512-entry copy at g_SinTable + 0x2000 IS A WRAP TAIL AND
     NOTHING ELSE. An angle near the top indexes up to 2047 + 512 =
     2559, and that tail is the only reason Demo_Main's first build
     loop writes a third destination while the second loop writes two.

   Demo_Main builds all of it from 512 quarter-sine words at img
   0x1a535 - README.txt section 4.

5.  The environment map

   The normal goes through the same three rotations as the position,
   with three differences:

       SAR 0xc after each, not 0xa, so it keeps the table's scale
       the Y angle is -g_ObjAngleY - 0x400, A QUARTER CIRCLE OFF
       a FOURTH rotation about Z by g_EnvMapAngle follows

   then SAR 0xb on x and y, g_ObjMirror negates both, and finally

       u = (x >> 0x11) + 0x80
       v = (y >> 0x11) + 0x80

   THAT IS SPHERE MAPPING. A view-space normal used directly as a
   texture coordinate reflects the same image regardless of where the
   object is, which is what makes a surface read as chrome, and
   g_EnvMapAngle turns the reflection without moving the model.

   The scale is right, which is a check rather than an assumption: for
   the 34-vertex model the coordinates come out spanning 22 to 217 and
   70 to 242 [verified by transcription], so a normal sweeps most of a
   256-wide texture and clips at neither end.

   The quarter-circle offset on the Y angle is what aims the map: it
   makes u follow the normal's X in a frame rotated 90 degrees from
   the model's, so the highlight sits beside the viewer rather than
   behind the object [inferred].

   WHAT THE MAP ITSELF IS HAS NOT BEEN FOUND. No 64 KB texture of
   anything sits in the image, and the palette is zero there too, so
   both are built at run time. sim/lithium/ therefore draws u and v as
   grey levels rather than looking anything up, and the banding it
   shows is the map coordinate, not the map.

6.  The per-object parameters

   +----------------------+-------------------------------------------+
   | g_MeshSrcOff         | the model, as an offset in segment 0x1e5f |
   | g_MeshNrmOut         | its per-vertex normals, in segment 0x2ad2 |
   | g_ObjAngleX, Y, Z    | orientation. Z is used doubled            |
   | g_ObjScaleShift      | a right shift; 16 is the identity         |
   | g_CurPosX, Y, Z      | subtracted from x and y, ADDED to z       |
   | g_EnvMapAngle        | spins the reflection                      |
   | g_ObjMirror          | negates u and v and reverses the winding  |
   | g_ObjAxisSwap        | swaps y and z and negates y, on the       |
   |                      | vertex AND the normal - a property of the |
   |                      | model as authored                         |
   | g_ObjFaceAttr        | goes into every face record at +0x06      |
   +----------------------+-------------------------------------------+

7.  What happens next

   THE DRAW IS READ. raster.txt has it: Scene_ProjectVerts applies the
   camera and the perspective divide, Scene_DrawFaces culls and sorts
   and calls the fill once per face, Poly_Fill rasterises into an
   off-screen buffer and Screen_Present blits the dirty rectangle.

   Two things it settles about the records on this page. The vertex
   record is REUSED, not copied - Scene_ProjectVerts writes the screen
   position over the view-space x and y - and the face attribute that
   Mesh_Transform copies in is A TEXTURE SEGMENT, with 0 and 1 reserved
   to mean a palette band. raster.txt sections 3.3, 4 and 6.

   Still missing: whatever clears the pools per frame, whatever drives
   g_ObjAngle* and g_CurPos* per scene, the env-map texture itself,
   and the palette.

8.  Open questions

   o Whether g_CurPos* is the camera or the object. The cull compares
     g_CurPosX against g_SlotHomeX, which would make the first
     the camera - but subtracting a camera position AFTER rotating is
     only correct if the object's own position was already folded in.
     The names say what the code does with them and no more.

   o Why the Z angle is doubled while X and Y are not. If g_ObjAngleZ
     is stored in 1024 units to the circle and the others in 2048,
     that would explain it and is testable once something writes them.
