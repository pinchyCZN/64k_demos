PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                 LITHIUM: THE TEXTURE AND THE PALETTES


Abstract

   Where the chrome comes from. The environment map is not stored
   anywhere - it is GENERATED in about eight lines of code from a
   random number generator, a 16x16 disc and a blur.

   IT IS NOT THE ONLY GENERATED TEXTURE. A second family builds
   RADIAL ones analytically - glowing discs and cones - out of a table
   of squares inverted by binary search, with no multiply and no
   divide anywhere. Section 6.

   One of the three palettes is generated too, and animated every
   frame, which is the colour cycling. The other two are stored in the
   image, and both are FOUR 64-ENTRY RISING RAMPS - the shape the
   rasteriser's band offsets need.

   Everything here is [verified] from the routines, and the numbers
   quoted for the generated map come from running the transcription in
   sim/lithium/parts.py.

1.  Why there is no texture in the image

   Tex_BuildSphereMap at 1000:5899, called from Demo_Main at
   1000:0168 - before INT 10h even sets mode 13h. Five steps:

       1  zero all 64 KB of segment 0x6ad2
       2  35 random walks: two Rnd_Next calls give a start point with
          the bytes complemented, then 450 calls to Tex_Stroke
       3  85 radial streaks: a random angle into g_SinTable and a
          random radius place a start point on a circle centred on
          0xde, 0xde, then 250 calls to Tex_Stroke
       4  Tex_Blur eight times over the whole segment
       5  halve every byte - DI walks down from 0xffff with
          SHR byte ptr [DI],1 - so the map ends in 0..0x7f

   That is 37,000 dots and two loops. A stored 256x256 texture would
   be 64 KB; the whole packed demo is 63 KB.

   ES IS LEFT AT 0xa000 DURING STEPS 2 AND 3. It looks like the
   routine is scribbling on the screen and it is not: this runs before
   mode 13h, and Tex_Plot writes through DS.

2.  Rnd_Next, and why its seed matters

   1000:3dfc, two words of state:

       ax = g_RndState0
       dx = g_RndState1
       xchg ah, dl              swap a byte between the halves
       ax += dx
       g_RndState0 = dx         shift the register along
       g_RndState1 = ax
       return rol(ax, 2)

   The ROL affects the returned value only, not the state.

   THE SEED IS LOAD-BEARING AND IT IS IN THE IMAGE: g_RndState0 and
   g_RndState1 ship as 0xa5b5 and 0xcab6 at img 0x8f20. A ZERO STATE
   IS A FIXED POINT - with (0, 0) the generator returns the same
   number for ever and the map comes out blank, so a transcription
   cannot default the state to zero.

   1000:52dd reseeds the generator, but Demo_Main calls it at
   1000:01f1, AFTER the map is built. Nothing is ever seeded from a
   clock, so THE DEMO DRAWS THE SAME MAP ON EVERY RUN - which is what
   makes caching the generated result safe.

3.  Tex_Plot and Tex_Stroke

   3.1.  Tex_Plot, 1000:597f

          reject if x >= 0xff or y >= 0xff   JNC, SO THE BOUND IS >=
                                             AND 255 ITSELF IS OUT.
                                             Unsigned, so a negative
                                             coordinate arrives as a
                                             large word and is dropped
          reject if x + y < 0x46             THE DIAGONAL
          si = (y << 8) + x
          16 rows of 16 bytes:
              [si] += brush[di]
              if the byte goes negative, store 0x7f    saturate
          si advances 0x100 - 0x10 per row, WRAPPING at 16 bits

       THE >= IS WORTH THE CARE IT LOOKS LIKE IT DOES NOT NEED.
       1000:5982 and 1000:5988 are JNC, not JA, so a brush ORIGIN at
       exactly 255 is rejected. Reading them as > admits 255, and
       because the brush then wraps at 16 bits the extra ink lands on
       the opposite edges - the interior of the map is unaffected and
       only its first and last rows and columns come out too bright.
       That was 10.4 per cent of the map, every byte of it high by 1
       to 9, and it put a uniform +1 through the whole rendered
       frame [verified; with the bound correct the port's map is
       byte-identical to the demo's across all 65,536].

      The brush at img 0x9f31 is a 16x16 DISC OF 1s - 174 of its 256
      bytes set - so one dot raises a round patch by one, and thousands
      of overlapping dots build a cloudy field that saturates at 0x7f.

      THE DIAGONAL REJECT IS THE DISTANCE FOG. The (0,0) corner of the
      map is where Scene_ProjectVerts drags the coordinates of a far
      surface, so keeping that corner dark gives fog for two divides
      and no blending - raster.txt section 2.1.

      DARK, NOT BLACK. Generating the map measures the protected
      triangle at a MEAN OF 3.1 against 23.4 over the whole map, with
      isolated values up to 49 along its hypotenuse: the eight blur
      passes bleed light back across the diagonal. So the fog fades
      toward a dark value, not to zero.

   3.2.  Tex_Stroke, 1000:5938

          bx = Rnd_Next & 0x1ffc                    a random angle
          r  = (Rnd_Next >> 3) + 0xa0               a random radius
          x  = (sin(bx) * r) >> 0x12  + g_StrokeX
          y  = (cos(bx) * r) >> 0x12  + g_StrokeY
          Tex_Plot

      IT DOES NOT STEP ALONG A PATH. Every call scatters one dot
      somewhere on a circle of random radius about the current stroke
      centre, and the radius reaches about 130 pixels - so a "stroke"
      is a sparse cloud, not a line, and plenty of its dots fall
      outside the map and are dropped.

4.  Tex_Blur, and its twin

   THERE ARE THREE COPIES OF THE SAME AVERAGING in the image, and
   they differ by one constant each:

   +--------------------+------------+--------+-----------------+
   | Routine            | Row stride | Decays | Used for        |
   +====================+============+========+=================+
   | Tex_Blur 0x4aee    | 0x100      | no     | the chrome map, |
   |                    |            |        | the heightmap   |
   | Image_Blur 0x4b15  | 0x140      | no     | 320-wide images |
   | 1000:08af          | 0x140      | YES    | the bump field  |
   +--------------------+------------+--------+-----------------+

   The first two are twelve instructions each and identical but for
   the stride, so one serves 256-wide buffers and the other
   screen-shaped ones. The third subtracts one after the shift,
   which is what makes a bump drain rather than merely spread -
   ../effects/bump.txt section 3.

   Neither of the first two bounds di: it wraps the whole 64 KB, so
   the first and last rows blur against the far end of the segment.

   4.1.  The algorithm

   1000:4aee, the mean of four neighbours, repeated CX times:

       ax = 0
       al += [di-1];      adc ah, 0        left
       al += [di+1];      adc ah, 0        right
       al += [di+0x100];  adc ah, 0        one row down
       al += [di+0xff00]; adc ah, 0        one row up
       ax >>= 2
       [di] = al
       inc di   until it wraps to zero

   THE +0x100 AND -0x100 NEIGHBOURS ARE WHY THE MAP IS 256 WIDE, and
   DI wrapping at 16 bits is why it is exactly 256x256. The ADC into
   AH sums four bytes into 16 bits without widening any of them.

   IT BLURS IN PLACE, so it is not a true box filter: the left and
   upper neighbours have already been replaced this pass, which makes
   the smear directional. A two-buffer blur gives a different texture,
   so this is load-bearing rather than a detail.

5.  What comes out

   From running the transcription:

   +------------------------+------------------------------------------+
   | range                  | 0 to 59, mean 23.4                       |
   | the x+y<0x46 triangle  | mean 3.1, max 49 - the fog corner        |
   | brightest corner       | 240,240 at mean 58.7                     |
   | build cost             | about one second in Python               |
   +------------------------+------------------------------------------+

   THE BAND DESIGN CHECKS OUT END TO END. Texels of a rendered model
   come out in 11..59, so Poly_Fill adding 0x40 lands them in 75..123
   and adding 0xc0 lands them in 203..251 - three distinct 64-entry
   bands, none of them straddling a boundary. raster.txt section 4.

   Step 5, the halving, is what guarantees that: without it a texel
   could reach 0xff and any band offset would wrap.

6.  The radial textures

   A second, entirely separate family. Where Tex_Plot and Tex_Blur
   build the chrome map out of noise, these compute a distance for
   every pixel and shade by it.

   6.1.  Math_Sqrt

      1000:4a91. EAX in, SI out AS A BYTE OFFSET - every caller
      shifts it right by 2 to get the root.

          bx = si = 0xc80                 the MIDPOINT, entry 800
          repeat:
              bx >>= 1;  bx &= 0xfffc     halve, stay dword-aligned
              compare |g_SquareTable[si] - eax| with the same at
              si +/- bx, and step toward the smaller

      THE TABLE IS INVERTED RATHER THAN A ROOT BEING COMPUTED. Ten
      halvings of a dword-aligned index land on the nearest entry -
      ten compares, no divide.

      g_SquareTable at 1000:c8b2 IS ZERO IN THE IMAGE.
      Math_BuildSquareTable at 1000:5334 fills it at startup, called
      from the main script at 1000:0232 and 1000:02c0:

          edx = 0x640;  di = 0xc8b2
          loop:  eax = 0x641 - edx;  eax *= eax
                 cs:[di] = eax;  di += 4;  edx--

      SO IT IS 1600 ENTRIES, entry i holding (i+1) squared, running
      0xc8b2 to 0xe1b2 - which is exactly where g_PolyArgs begins, so
      the length is confirmed by what follows it. The 0xc80 the
      search starts from is the midpoint of that, not its size.

      THE SQUARES ARE THE ONLY MULTIPLIES. After this one loop, every
      distance in the demo comes out of the table.

      AND IT IS NOT A STARTUP LOOP. The same 0x1900 bytes are the
      rasteriser's span buffer - ../subsystems/raster.txt section 6.4
      - so any polygon drawn destroys the squares. That is why the
      script rebuilds the table twice, each time immediately before a
      batch of texture generation.

   6.2.  The three generators

      All three walk g_TexX and g_TexY DOWN from their size while DI
      runs UP, so a centre written as c appears at pixel size - c.
      Anything reimplementing them has to mirror.

      +---------------------+-------+----------+-----------------------+
      | Routine             | Size  | Into     | Value                 |
      +=====================+=======+==========+=======================+
      | Tex_BuildThreeGlows | 256   | 0x5ad2   | three discs,          |
      | 1000:5721           |       |          | 0x3f - d, radius 0x3f |
      +---------------------+-------+----------+-----------------------+
      | Tex_BuildGlowNoise  | 256   | 0x5ad2   | 0x104 - d/16 - d/64,  |
      | 1000:57e1           |       |          | plus noise, clamped   |
      +---------------------+-------+----------+-----------------------+
      | Flare_BuildSprite   | 160   | 0x7ad2   | 0xff - d/4 - d/8      |
      | 1000:5363           |       |          |                       |
      +---------------------+-------+----------+-----------------------+

      THE FALLOFFS ARE SHIFTS, NOT DIVIDES, and two of the three start
      ABOVE full white - so the middle of a glow saturates into a flat
      core and only the skirt is a gradient.

      Tex_AddGlow at 1000:5786 is the per-disc helper the first one
      calls three times a pixel, at (0x40,0x70), (0xb0,0xc0) and
      (0xc0,0x40). It returns on |dx| >= 0x3f or |dy| >= 0x3f BEFORE
      taking the root, so a pixel outside the disc costs one compare -
      which is what makes three glows over 65,536 pixels affordable.
      Its 0x3f is one palette band exactly, so a disc never bleeds
      into the next band.

      Tex_BuildGlowNoise adds its grain AFTER the range test, so the
      noise clips away inside the saturated core and shows only in the
      skirt.

      FLARE_BUILDSPRITE IS THE ODD ONE: its cone is not sampled as
      colour at all. Flare_ClipSetup CROPS it - the source for an
      element of radius r starts at (0x50-r, 0x50-r) in the 160-wide
      buffer - so because the cone is linear, one sprite serves every
      flare size with no scaling. effects/sky.txt section 6.

      It shares segment 0x7ad2 with the bump map's height field, and
      that is reuse rather than a shared purpose: the bump scene
      builds its own field by stamping dust.

   6.3.  Tex_SnapshotFrame

      1000:2496 is a fourth way to fill 0x5ad2 and it computes
      nothing: it renders a single frame into 0x4ad2 and REP MOVSDs
      64,000 bytes across. The main script runs it immediately before
      Scene_Shatter, so it is what decides what shatters.

      It is not a scene. There is no loop in it.

   6.4.  One segment, four tenants

      0x5ad2 IS NOT "THE GLOW TEXTURE" - it is whichever of four
      things last filled it, and the scene running decides which:

      +-----------------------+-------------------------------------+
      | Tex_BuildGlowNoise    | the flyover's and the battle's      |
      |                       | terrain, and the tower fall's       |
      | Tex_BuildThreeGlows   | the bump scene's lighting picture   |
      | Tex_SnapshotFrom      | the frame the glass breaks out of   |
      | Tex_SnapshotFrame     | the frame the glass assembles into  |
      +-----------------------+-------------------------------------+

      THE MAIN SCRIPT REBUILDS IT BETWEEN SCENES RATHER THAN KEEPING
      COPIES, which is why Tex_BuildGlowNoise is called twice -
      1000:02c3 and 1000:032d - with the bump scene's picture and two
      snapshots in between. A reconstruction with a buffer per
      purpose must rebuild at the same points or it will draw a scene
      with the tenant before last.

7.  Pal_Animate, the colour cycling

   1000:6d49 rebuilds g_PalMain every time it is called.

       g_PalPhaseR += 0xd
       g_PalPhaseG += 0x10
       g_PalPhaseB += 0x15

   Three phases advancing at 13, 16 and 21 units of 2048 - coprime
   enough that the hue wanders a long way before repeating. Each
   indexes g_SinTable, and the sample becomes a per-channel SLOPE:

       slope = ((sin(phase) >> 6) + 0x40) << g_PalRampShift

   Then one 16-bit accumulator per channel is stepped by its slope and
   THE HIGH BYTE IS THE DAC VALUE, clamped at 0x3f. So each channel is
   a linear ramp from black whose steepness is animated, and what
   cycles is the colour the ramp climbs toward.

   IT FILLS 128 ENTRIES, from 0x96be to 0x983e, then writes black into
   entry 128. Entries 129 to 255 are not touched and nothing found yet
   writes them - so under this palette the top half is black and only
   band offsets 0x00 and 0x40 land anywhere visible.

   g_PalRampShift at img 0x8fe1 ships as 0, and four scene routines
   write it - 1000:078e, 1000:09b7, 1000:13d5 and 1000:1534 - so a
   scene can steepen every ramp at once.

8.  The two stored palettes

   Demo_Main selects a palette by writing its address into
   g_PalSource. Three addresses appear, 0x300 apart:

   +----------+-------------+------------------------------------------+
   | g_PalMain| img 0x96be  | ZERO IN THE IMAGE - Pal_Animate builds   |
   |          |             | it. Selected at 1000:01f7 and 1000:027f  |
   | g_Pal1   | img 0x99be  | stored. Selected at 1000:02fe            |
   | g_Pal2   | img 0x9cbe  | stored. Selected at 1000:0353            |
   +----------+-------------+------------------------------------------+

   BOTH STORED PALETTES ARE FOUR 64-ENTRY RISING RAMPS [verified by
   inspection], which is exactly what the band offsets need.

   g_Pal1 is four identical greyscale ramps, every band 0 to 0x3f in
   all three channels.

   g_Pal2 has four different ramps:

       band 0   entries   0..63   R 0..16   G 0..36   B 0..36
       band 1   entries  64..127  R 17..53  G 36..63  B 37..63
       band 2   entries 128..191  R 0..51   G 0..61   B 0..62
       band 3   entries 192..255  R 0..53   G 0..63   B 0..63

   BANDS 0 AND 1 ARE BLUE AND CYAN DOMINANT - red reaches 16 where
   green and blue reach 36. That is the cyan surface
   effects/scenes.txt scene 9 describes, and which band a face lands
   in is decided by its attribute, so one palette gives the surface
   and the tower different colours.

   A FOURTH PALETTE at img 0x9e71 is selected at 1000:03b3, for the
   final scene. It is not 0x300 from the other three, and exactly ONE
   of its 768 bytes exceeds 0x3f - which Pal_Upload clamps - so it is
   a DAC palette like the rest, just not on the same stride.
