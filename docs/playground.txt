PCDEMOS 64k                                                Analysis Note


                      THE PLAYGROUND - OURS, NOT THEIRS


Abstract

   Interactive parts built on top of the reconstruction. NOTHING HERE
   IS IN ANY DEMO. These parts let a recovered effect be driven rather
   than watched, and they are kept apart from everything under
   docs/<demo>/ for one reason: those documents describe a binary, and
   a reader must never have to wonder which of the two a number came
   from.

   Every constant in this document is ours and was chosen by feel. No
   address in it may be cited as evidence about any demo.

1.  Where it lives, and the rule it follows

   +------------------------------+----------------------------------+
   | src/xcursion/xcursion_play.c | the parts                        |
   | src/xcursion/xcursion_play.h | what the harness calls           |
   +------------------------------+----------------------------------+

   IT IS A FORK, NOT A HOOK. The demo's own car is
   xcursion/effects/car.txt and is untouched: this file carries its
   own terrain, its own camera and
   its own copy of the vehicle model, so no change made for a player
   can reach the reconstruction and no reconstruction fix can silently
   change the handling.

   What that costs is one copy of the physics. What it buys is that
   the verified code stays verified, which is the trade this project
   makes everywhere else too.

   The harness reaches it through five additive lines in
   xcursion_demo.c - a scene index, a dispatch case, an entry, a key
   route and a seek short-circuit. Nothing else in the demo's code is
   aware of it.

   THE CALLER LENDS THE FRAMEBUFFER AND THE PALETTE. Xcur_PlayEnter
   takes the palette pair and Xcur_PlayFrame takes the framebuffer,
   which is what keeps this file clear of the demo's statics and lets
   the fork be real rather than nominal.

   It is not on the music. seek_scene short-circuits to it rather than
   replaying the module, because there is no music position that means
   "the player is driving".

   IT FOLLOWS THE DEMO. img 0x53c4 jumps to Demo_Shutdown once the
   water part's music position reaches 0x52; this goes to the
   playground instead, so the game arrives out of the final black
   rather than having to be skipped to.

   THAT HANDOVER NEEDED A REPLAYER FIX FIRST. g_MusicPos is a plain
   INC at img 0x2205 - it does not wrap and it does not skip the end
   marker - and 0x52 is this module's 82 orders exactly, so the
   counter only ever reaches it by climbing off the end of the list.
   A replayer that wraps the position modulo the order count, which
   is the tidy thing to write, can never produce the value the demo
   waits for, and the last part runs for ever on a black screen.
   xcursion/subsystems/music.txt section 6.

2.  The two vehicles

   THEY ARE ONE INDEX APART: a body, a model and a step function
   each, so switching is a single assignment and the one left behind
   keeps its place and goes on being drawn. Step away from the car,
   fly off, look back and it is still parked down there. They start
   in different places - the car on the ground at the origin, the
   ship a short way off and SHIP_RISE above the land.

   Almost nothing is shared, which is why the split is an if at the
   top rather than conditionals threaded through one function. Both
   take gas, the brake, a drag and a top speed, and both advance
   along their yaw; everything else differs.

   +--------------------+----------+----------+
   | force              | car      | ship     |
   +====================+==========+==========+
   | brake              | yes      | yes      |
   | slope gravity      | yes      | no       |
   | spring and jumps   | yes      | no       |
   | tilt from terrain  | yes      | no       |
   | climb from trim    | no       | yes      |
   | ceiling, clearance | no       | yes      |
   +--------------------+----------+----------+

2.1.  The ship

   THE PITCH IS THE CLIMB, AND IT IS TRIM RATHER THAN A STICK. Up
   and down move it and nothing returns it, so holding altitude
   means flying the nose back to level. The rate is the pitch's sine
   times the speed, so a slow ship climbs slowly and a stopped one
   not at all.

   THE BRAKE IS THE CAR'S, PLAY_BRAKE, on the same key and with the
   same stopping power - about ninety ticks from the top speed. It
   only ever slows: the ship has no reverse to oppose, so it
   subtracts where the car's pushes back against the travel.

   THE TERRAIN AND THE CEILING ARE PLAIN CLAMPS and touch nothing
   else. A ship flown into a hill rides up it at SHIP_CLEAR, still
   nose down, and goes on descending the far side the moment the
   ground drops away - the terrain is the floor rather than a
   collision.

   Measured with the controls held: pitch clamps at SHIP_PITCH_MAX,
   the height pins at SHIP_CEILING under full nose-up, and nose-down
   into rising ground holds the clearance at exactly SHIP_CLEAR
   while the height tracks the land beneath it.

   ITS MODEL'S NOSE POINTS THE OTHER WAY, the same way its yaw does.
   Drawn straight off the body it tilts nose-down to climb, so the
   pitch is passed into the draw already negated - which is why that
   function takes it rather than reading it off the body.

   ITS CAMERA IGNORES THE TERRAIN ENTIRELY and rides a fixed lift
   above the ship, with no switch - a terrain-following camera loses
   a ship at altitude completely. The 'a' switch still works for the
   car.

2.2.  The car

   Physics on the tick, picture on the frame - the demo's own split,
   xcursion/subsystems/animation.txt section 1.1, so the handling
   does not change with the frame rate.

   What is kept from xcursion/effects/car.txt is the SHAPE of the
   model, and it is worth keeping: the depth the wheels are under the
   ground becomes
   upward velocity, so a ridge throws the car instead of stopping it,
   and two probe pairs - one along the body, one across - give pitch
   and roll from the land rather than from the steering.

   What is replaced is everything a player has to hold: the demo
   steers with a sine sweep and accelerates without a ceiling.

   SPEED CARRIES A SIGN and three forces act on it. Drive and the
   slope apply only with the wheels down; the two drags always do.

   +-------------+---------------------------------------------------+
   | drive       | gas adds, brake opposes whichever way it is going |
   | slope       | ground behind minus ground ahead, so a climb      |
   |             | subtracts and a descent adds                      |
   | drag        | proportional - this is what sets the top speed,   |
   |             | balancing the drive at about accel << 7           |
   | friction    | a constant, and the reason it ever stops          |
   +-------------+---------------------------------------------------+

   BOTH DRAGS ARE NEEDED. The proportional one sets the top speed but
   integer division floors small values to nothing, so alone it
   leaves the car creeping for ever; the constant one brings it to
   rest and gives a deadband a weak net force cannot overcome, which
   is what a parked car on a shallow slope should do.

   THE SLOPE TERM IS SCALED TO JUST BEAT FULL THROTTLE. Measured over
   this terrain it reaches about 1100, so a shift of 6 gives a little
   over 17 against a drive of 12. A shift of 4 was the first guess
   and it made every hill unclimbable.

   Measured with the gas held and then released, which is the whole
   behaviour in one run:

   +----------+--------+-------------------------------------------+
   | slope    | push   | what the car does under full throttle     |
   +==========+========+===========================================+
   | -1080    |  -17   | loses, and rolls back down                |
   |  -840    |  -14   | net -2, held by the friction deadband -   |
   |          |        | it parks on the hill                      |
   |  -348    |   -6   | drive wins, it climbs                     |
   +----------+--------+-------------------------------------------+

   With the gas released on a slope it rolls back at once, and
   crossing onto a descent it accelerates again on gravity alone.

   Measured with the controls held: the top speed settles near 1400,
   about five times the demo's own car, and the spring throws the car
   some 480 units clear of the ground off a ridge taken fast.

   +-----------+-------------------------------------+
   | Key       | What it does                        |
   +===========+=====================================+
   | z         | gas                                 |
   | x         | brake                               |
   | left      | steer left                          |
   | right     | steer right                         |
   | c         | the camera a quarter turn round     |
   | a         | camera height: ground / car         |
   | s         | ground colour: default / grey moon  |
   | 1         | car <-> ship                        |
   | up / down | ship: trim the nose up and down     |
   | d / f     | the camera closer / further         |
   +-----------+-------------------------------------+

   FAST FORWARD MOVED TO TAB for this. main_sdl.c had f, and a host
   that eats a letter makes it look like the demo ignores the key.

   THE GROUND'S COLOUR IS ITS OWN 32 PALETTE ENTRIES, which is what
   makes 's' possible at all. Voxel_Render clamps the land to 0 to
   0x1f and Xcur_PalBuildGreyLow writes exactly that band and nothing
   else - img 0x4e7d, the moon's own builder - so swapping it reaches
   the land and leaves the craft and the sky alone. The default band
   is kept from Xcur_PalBuild at entry and swapped back in.

   THE FLAT PLAINS ARE A FLOOR, NOT A DIFFERENT GENERATOR. img
   0x5174 clamps every cell below 0x10 up to 0x10, so each basin the
   plasma dug comes out dead level - which is the wide flat ground
   the demo parks its car on. The generator call is the same one
   this part uses, mask 0x3fff, bias 0x2000, roughness 2; without
   the floor the same terrain has almost no flat ground at all.
   Scene 27 sets it up and scenes 28 and 29 inherit it.

   THE MOON HAS NO WATER EITHER. img 0x4f72 sets the water level to
   zero in that same part, which disables it in the renderer and in
   the height probe both, so the surface is bare rock to the horizon
   and the car drives on what would be the sea bed.

   THE OPENING'S LAND IS A DIFFERENT TERRAIN, not a palette change:
   0x7fff, 0x4000 and roughness 3 make it taller and rougher.

   THE FLOOR AND THE WATER TOGETHER ARE OURS. The demo never pairs
   them - it floors the ground only in the parts that have no water -
   so with the default palette every floored basin fills to one
   level and reads as a wide flat sea. That is a consequence of
   wanting the flat ground in both modes, not something the demo
   does.

   THE CAMERA HEIGHT HAS TWO MODES and 'a' switches them. It always
   clears the ground under itself; what it also clears is either the
   ground under the car, which is the default, or the CAR. Hugging
   the ground is right while driving - the camera rides the terrain
   like the car does - but a jump leaves it behind: measured off a
   ridge the car gets 600 to 1700 units up and the camera ends up
   150 to 450 BELOW it, which throws the car off the top of the
   frame. Tracking the car holds the lift at PLAY_CAM_RISE however
   high it goes.

   THE CAMERA HAS FOUR SLOTS a quarter turn apart - rear, side,
   front, the other side - and 'a' steps through them. It does not
   jump between them: the slot only moves the angle the camera is
   easing onto, so the same ease that keeps it behind the car carries
   it round. Stepping always goes the same way, because a quarter
   turn is never the long way round once the difference is folded.

   THE MODEL FACES THE OTHER WAY. Its own yaw is a half turn off the
   direction of travel, so the mesh yaw carries 0x400 as well as the
   negation the camera needs.

   The arrow keys arrive as SDL keysyms, which the host passes through
   untranslated - so the two constants in xcursion_play.h are SDL's
   values and not ASCII.

   THE HEADING AND THE CAMERA SHARE ONE CONVENTION, the renderer's: a
   bearing of a looks along (+sin a, +cos a). The demo's own car does
   not - its travel negates z, because its camera orbits rather than
   follows - and carrying that into a chase camera swings the camera
   opposite to the turn.

   A TURN IS 0x800 IN THESE ANGLES, NOT 0x10000. The angles are held
   in a word and only the low 0x7fc reaches the sine table, so a
   difference taken across the word wrap reads as most of a circle
   when the two are a few units apart. Easing that sends the camera
   the long way round - it whips a full turn whenever the heading
   crosses a boundary, which looks exactly like a camera spinning the
   wrong way. Fold the difference into one turn before easing it.

   AN OBJECT'S APPARENT ORIENTATION IS ITS YAW PLUS THE CAMERA'S, not
   minus: Mesh_Draw latches yaw + cam_yaw at img 0x4664. The demo
   proves it in the launch, where the camera turns -4 a tick and the
   craft +4 to hold still. So a camera that tracks the car needs the
   car's mesh yaw NEGATED, or the model counter-rotates at twice the
   steering rate while the camera follows correctly underneath it.

   THE CAMERA EASES ONTO THE CAR'S HEADING rather than snapping to it.
   The whole landscape turns with the camera bearing, so a camera
   locked to the heading makes the world jerk under every steering
   input. Its height clears the HIGHER of the two grounds, the car's
   and its own: taking only its own swings the framing as a slot
   moves it onto different terrain, and taking only the car's lets a
   hill between the two swallow the view.

3.  The knobs

   Every one of these is ours. They are all in xcursion_play.c and
   nothing outside that file reads them.

   +------------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | PLAY_ACCEL       | engine. How hard the gas pushes, per tick -    |
   |                  | raise it to climb better AND go faster         |
   | PLAY_BRAKE       | how hard the brake pushes against travel       |
   | PLAY_DRAG_SHIFT  | air drag, as a SHIFT: the force is speed >>    |
   |                  | this. Bigger = less drag = faster and looser   |
   | PLAY_ROLL_FRIC   | rolling friction, a flat amount. The reason    |
   |                  | the car ever fully stops, and the deadband     |
   |                  | that lets it park on a slope                   |
   | PLAY_SPEED_MAX   | a HARD CEILING, and not what sets the top      |
   |                  | speed - see below                              |
   | PLAY_SLOPE_SHIFT | gravity along the ground, as a SHIFT. SMALLER  |
   |                  | IS STRONGER. 6 now; 5 doubles the push, 7      |
   |                  | halves it                                      |
   | PLAY_SLOPE_PROBE | how far ahead and behind the slope is sampled, |
   |                  | as a shift. Bigger reads longer hills and      |
   |                  | ignores bumps                                  |
   | PLAY_GRAVITY     | downward pull per tick while airborne. Bigger  |
   |                  | = shorter, flatter jumps                       |
   | PLAY_SPRING      | suspension stiffness, as a SHIFT. SMALLER IS   |
   |                  | STIFFER and throws the car higher off a ridge  |
   | PLAY_BOUNCE      | how much of the vertical speed is applied per  |
   |                  | tick, as a shift. Smaller = livelier           |
   | PLAY_AIR         | how far off the ground still counts as wheels  |
   |                  | down for drive and slope                       |
   | PLAY_STEER       | turn rate per tick, in angle units of 0x800    |
   |                  | to the turn                                    |
   | PLAY_CAM_DIST    | the camera distance d and f start from         |
   | PLAY_CAM_NEAR    | closest d will go                              |
   | PLAY_CAM_FAR     | furthest f will go                             |
   | PLAY_CAM_STEP    | how much one tap moves it                      |
   | PLAY_CAM_EASE    | how fast the camera settles, as a SHIFT.       |
   |                  | SMALLER IS SNAPPIER                            |
   | PLAY_CAM_RISE    | how high above the ground the camera rides     |
   | PLAY_CAM_SLOT    | the angle one c tap moves, 0x200 being 90      |
   | PLAY_MODEL_YAW   | the half turn the model needs to face forward  |
   +------------------+------------------------------------------------+

   The drag camera's own, in xcursion_look.c.

   +------------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | LOOK_YAW_NUM     | angle units per pixel of drag, over            |
   | LOOK_YAW_SHIFT   | LOOK_YAW_SHIFT. One screen width is about one  |
   |                  | full turn at 16 over 1                         |
   | LOOK_PITCH_MAX   | how far the horizon will slide, in rows. The   |
   |                  | renderer clamps itself either way, so this is  |
   |                  | taste and not safety - section 4               |
   | LOOK_EASE        | how fast a released view returns, as a SHIFT.  |
   |                  | SMALLER IS SNAPPIER                            |
   +------------------+------------------------------------------------+

   The ship's own set. None of the car's forces reach it.

   +------------------+------------------------------------------------+
   | SHIP_ACCEL       | thrust per tick                                |
   | SHIP_DRAG_SHIFT  | drag, as a shift - sets the top speed with     |
   |                  | SHIP_ACCEL, the same way the car's does        |
   | SHIP_SPEED_MAX   | a hard ceiling on speed                        |
   | SHIP_STEER       | yaw per tick - lower than the car's            |
   | SHIP_PITCH_RATE  | how fast up and down move the trim             |
   | SHIP_PITCH_MAX   | how far the nose will go, 0x100 being an       |
   |                  | eighth of a turn                               |
   | SHIP_CLIMB_SHIFT | climb per tick, as a shift on sin(pitch) times |
   |                  | speed. HIGHER IS GENTLER                       |
   | SHIP_CEILING     | how high it may get                            |
   | SHIP_CLEAR       | how low it may skim                            |
   | SHIP_RISE        | how far above the ground it starts             |
   | SHIP_AWAY        | how far from the car it parks                  |
   | SHIP_MODEL_YAW   | its model's own facing offset                  |
   +------------------+------------------------------------------------+

3.1.  What actually sets the top speed

   NOT PLAY_SPEED_MAX. That is a clamp the car never reaches. The top
   speed is where the drive balances the two drags:

       top  =  (PLAY_ACCEL - PLAY_ROLL_FRIC) << PLAY_DRAG_SHIFT

   which is (12 - 1) << 7, about 1400. PLAY_SPEED_MAX sits at 0x600
   so it only ever catches a runaway, such as a long descent adding
   gravity to full throttle.

   PLAY_DRAG_SHIFT also sets how LONG it takes to get there: the
   speed closes on its top by one part in 2^shift each tick, so 7 is
   a time constant of 128 ticks, a little over a second.

   So the two have different jobs. PLAY_ACCEL changes the top speed
   and the pickup together; PLAY_DRAG_SHIFT changes the top speed and
   how heavy the car feels without touching the engine.

3.2.  Climbing, and being pushed back

   One number governs both: PLAY_SLOPE_SHIFT, and SMALLER IS A
   STRONGER PULL. Measured over this terrain the raw slope reaches
   about 1100, so at a shift of 6 the steepest ground pushes with
   about 17 against a drive of 12.

   +--------------------+--------------------------------------------+
   | climbs too poorly  | raise PLAY_SLOPE_SHIFT to 7, or raise      |
   |                    | PLAY_ACCEL                                 |
   | climbs anything    | lower PLAY_SLOPE_SHIFT to 5                |
   | parks on too much  | lower PLAY_ROLL_FRIC to 0, which removes   |
   |                    | the deadband entirely                      |
   +--------------------+--------------------------------------------+

   Raising PLAY_ACCEL to climb better also raises the top speed, by
   section 3.1. To climb harder WITHOUT going faster, raise
   PLAY_ACCEL and lower PLAY_DRAG_SHIFT together.

3.3.  Jumping

   Two numbers, and they pull against each other. PLAY_SPRING is the
   stiffness as a shift, so SMALLER IS STIFFER: at 3 the car takes an
   eighth of its penetration depth as upward speed, and 2 would take
   a quarter and throw it much higher. PLAY_GRAVITY is the pull that
   brings it down, so lowering it floats the jump out longer.

   Height is roughly the launch speed squared over twice the gravity,
   so halving PLAY_GRAVITY doubles the height while stiffening
   PLAY_SPRING by one quadruples it. Stiffen first; it is the one
   that makes a ridge feel like a ramp rather than making the whole
   world feel like the moon.

   The spring only acts when the wheels are under the ground, so what
   the car hits matters more than how fast it is going: a sharp ridge
   throws it and a long rise does not.

4.  The drag camera

   HOLD THE LEFT BUTTON AND THE VIEW SWINGS WHILE THE SCENE PLAYS ON.
   Nothing is paused and nothing is steered: the demo's camera flies
   its own path underneath, and the drag only changes the angles it is
   looked at through. Let go and the view eases back onto the demo's.

   +------------------------------+----------------------------------+
   | src/xcursion/xcursion_look.c | the offset and the ease          |
   | src/xcursion/xcursion_look.h | the four calls                   |
   +------------------------------+----------------------------------+

   THE DEMO IS NEVER TOLD. Xcur_LookPush writes the offset into the
   camera the renderer is about to read and Xcur_LookPop takes it out
   again, both inside one frame with no simulation step between them.
   That ordering is the whole design, and the reason it is not merely
   tidy is Cam_FollowTerrain: its sky bias at img 0x98f7 is a running
   sum, so a camera left modified for even one step would carry the
   drag forward for the rest of the demo and never return.

   Measured against an undragged run of the same frames: a 120-pixel
   yaw rejoins it byte for byte 22 frames after release, a 70-pixel
   pitch 12 frames after, and both stay identical for the 150 frames
   that follow.

   FOUR CALLS REACH IT - a mouse route, a tick for the ease, and the
   push and pop around backdrop().

   THE TWO AXES ARE NOT THE SAME KIND OF THING. Across is the camera
   bearing and turns all the way round, because the terrain wraps at
   256 by 256 and there is no edge to reach. Up and down is a HORIZON
   SLIDE and not a pitch - nothing rotates, the whole picture moves on
   the screen - so it clamps instead.

   THE VERTICAL MOVES FOUR THINGS AND THEY MUST AGREE. The land rides
   on the camera's yofs, the sky on its sky_bias, and the relation
   that keeps those together is yofs = sky_bias * 320, which is what
   Cam_FollowTerrain maintains at img 0x2001. The sky travels at twice
   the land's rate because Voxel_Render subtracts the bias from its
   table twice; that parallax is the demo's own and is kept.

   THE FOURTH IS THE GLOBAL g_SkyBias AND IT IS NOT THE CAMERA'S.
   ship() and ship_at() both build their mesh camera from the global,
   so a push that moves only the XcurCam slides the land out from
   under an object that stays exactly where it was. Both are lent to
   the push for that reason. The car part is where it shows: the car
   and the flagpole have to rise with the ground they stand on.

   BOTH AXES GRAB rather than steer: the picture follows the pointer.
   Drag right and the landscape goes right, which means the bearing
   decreases - the renderer's angle runs the other way.

4.1.  Which parts have it, and what each one can move

   +---------------------------+---------+---------+------------------+
   | part                      | across  | up/down | carrier          |
   +===========================+=========+=========+==================+
   | opening, approach,        | yes     | yes     | g_cam, through   |
   | landscape flight          |         |         | backdrop()       |
   | car                       | yes     | yes     | g_cam, inline    |
   | chase, battle             | yes     | yes     | g_mc, in camera  |
   |                           |         |         | space - 4.2      |
   | launch, the departure     | yes     | NO      | loose arguments  |
   | countdown                 | baked   | baked   | g_cam, once      |
   +---------------------------+---------+---------+------------------+

   THE DEPARTURE IS A DIFFERENT RENDERER, which is why it takes the
   yaw and nothing else. Ground_Render - img 0x2621, where the shade
   map is the height and the plasma is only the water test, the
   opposite pairing from Voxel_Render - takes its bearing and scale as
   loose arguments and HAS NO VERTICAL TERM AT ALL: its horizon is
   fixed by di = 0xf8c0 and the dist ramp. There is nothing to slide,
   so Xcur_LookYaw is read into the argument directly and no push is
   needed, nothing having been mutated.

   ITS CRAFT IS PLACED, NOT TRANSFORMED. anim_tick3 integrates
   g_mc.tz and ty itself and never calls Mesh_Setup, so the craft
   holds the centre of the frame however far the view swings and
   turns in place rather than crossing the screen. Carrying the yaw
   into a copy of its mesh camera is what keeps the face it presents
   agreeing with the ground under it.

   THE CAR PART IS THE SAFEST SITE IN THE DEMO. It re-latches wx, wy,
   scale, yofs, sky_bias and water_scroll from the animation's own
   globals at the top of every frame - img 0x35d7 and 0x3605 - so
   even a leaked offset would be overwritten before it could be seen.
   The push covers the landscape and all three objects together; they
   need no handling of their own, because ship_at derives its mesh
   camera from the camera's angle, position, scale and altitude.

4.2.  The battle and the chase, where the objects are the view

   THESE SCENES HAVE NO WORLD CAMERA AT ALL. draw_craft_a, draw_craft_b
   and Shots_Draw write tx, ty and tz straight from the animation and
   NEITHER SCENE EVER CALLS Mesh_Setup, so there is no bearing to add
   to: the objects are already in camera space. That makes cam_yaw a
   trap - Mesh_Draw latches yaw + cam_yaw at img 0x4664, so moving it
   alone spins every craft where it stands instead of swinging the
   view past it.

   SO THE SWING IS A ROTATION OF EACH OBJECT'S POSITION, and it is
   exact rather than an approximation. Mesh_Setup turns a world offset
   by the camera bearing; rotation composes, so turning an already
   camera-space position by the drag gives precisely what a bearing
   that much further round would have produced. Neither scene applies
   Mesh_Setup's own >> 6 and 7/8 squash, which do not commute with a
   rotation, so there is nothing to spoil it.

   Both terms are carried: the position is rotated, cam_yaw takes the
   same yaw so the face each craft presents agrees with where it now
   sits, and sky_bias takes the pitch, which for a mesh is a plain
   screen-row shift of the projected y.

   VEC_ROTATE3 RETURNS THE VECTOR TIMES 64 and the shift that pays it
   back is the caller's. Its sine table is scaled to 4096 against a
   >> 6, so the roll and pitch stages cancel out at zero but the yaw
   stage does not; Mesh_Setup settles it at img 0x4634 and anything
   else that calls the routine must do the same. Omitting it leaves
   each object's translation 64 times too large while its vertices
   stay their true size, so the model collapses to a dot at 64 times
   its proper distance - the craft read as stars and vanish on the
   smallest drag.

   THE SHOTS GO THROUGH A COPY. Shots_Draw owns tx, ty and tz inside
   its own loop at img 0x280b, so a push around it would be
   overwritten once per shot. Their positions are swung in a local
   copy instead, which leaves the transcription untouched. The
   dead-shot test is x == 0, so a shot at rest is skipped rather than
   rotated into life.

   WHAT DOES NOT FOLLOW: the starfield, the chase's planet, and the
   battle's glow, which is a fixed screen box. Section 4.4.

4.3.  Pan, orbit, and which parts get which

   THERE ARE TWO WAYS TO SWING A VIEW AND THE PARTS DO NOT AGREE ON
   WHICH IS BETTER.

   PAN is the camera turning where it stands: add the drag to the
   bearing, put it back afterwards. It touches one field and asks no
   questions, and for a landscape it is the right answer - the ground
   fills the frame at every bearing, so turning never loses the
   subject and the only thing an orbit would add is a camera that can
   walk into a hill.

   ORBIT turns everything about a point instead. Turning a camera
   around a pivot while it keeps facing that pivot is, in camera
   space, just rotating about the pivot:

       p' = R(p - P) + P

   so P holds its place on screen and the scene revolves around it -
   one subtract and one add either side of the rotation. That is what
   the battle needs, where the subject is a small object in empty
   space and a pan slides it out of frame for nothing.

   PAN IS A SUBSET OF ORBIT, NOT AN ALTERNATIVE TO IT, so there is no
   flag and no second code path. A part that sets no pivot turns
   about the camera, which IS a pan; the landscape parts go through
   Xcur_LookPush, which has no pivot to set.

   +-------------------+----------------------------------------------+
   | opening, approach | pan                                          |
   | landing, car      |                                              |
   | launch            | pan, and no choice about it - section 4.1    |
   | battle            | orbit on the midpoint of the two craft, or   |
   |                   | craft A alone while B is away                |
   | chase             | orbit on the craft itself, so it holds its   |
   |                   | ground and only turns - a turntable          |
   +-------------------+----------------------------------------------+

   THE PIVOT IS EACH PART'S OWN, set once a frame before the draws.
   Xcur_LookTick clears it first, so a part that wants one sets it
   every frame and none can inherit another's.

   CRAFT B IS NOT ALWAYS THERE. draw_craft_b returns early on
   g_b_z < 0 at img 0x2701, so for part of the battle there is no
   second craft and no midpoint to take; the pivot falls back to
   craft A rather than lurching when B returns.

   PRECISION IS NOT A PROBLEM HERE, which is worth stating because it
   looks as though it should be. Vec_Rotate3's only truncation is a
   >> 6 of a quantity already 64 times the answer, so rotating a
   DIFFERENCE rather than a position still lands within one world
   unit - a part in eight thousand of the battle's craft separation.

   AN ORBIT MAKES THE FIXED STARFIELD MORE CONSPICUOUS, NOT LESS.
   A pan slid the craft across the stars, which read as a camera
   moving; an orbit holds them still, so the stars are the only thing
   left that could say the view moved, and they do not. What carries
   it instead is cam_yaw showing a different side of each craft and
   the two craft parallaxing against one another. Section 4.4 is what
   the stars would cost.

4.4.  What rotating the starfield would take

   Nothing here is implemented. This is what it would cost.

   THE FIELD IS A TORUS IN 16-BIT SPACE, NOT A CLOUD OF POINTS. Every
   subtraction in Points_Render is 16-bit and wraps - img 0x3222,
   0x3227 and 0x322c are all word operations - so a star that leaves
   one side of the volume arrives at the other. 1000 points fill that
   volume uniformly and are never culled by distance, only by falling
   off the screen.

   SO ROTATING THE STARS THEMSELVES DOES NOT WORK. Rotation does not
   commute with a modular wrap: turning a point in 32-bit space and
   then wrapping it to 16 bits puts it somewhere unrelated to where
   turning the wrapped difference would have put it. The field would
   come apart rather than turn.

   MOVING THE CAMERA DOES NOT WORK EITHER, and this is the one that
   looks like the cheap answer. cx, cy and cz are a TRANSLATION and
   the renderer has no camera orientation at all - changing them pans
   the field, it cannot turn it.

   WHAT WOULD WORK is rotating the DIFFERENCE, inside the projection,
   after the 16-bit subtraction and before the depth shift:

       ax = (s16)((pts.x - cx) & 0xffff)
       ay = (s16)((pts.y - cy) & 0xffff)
       az = (s16)((pts.z - cz) & 0xffff)     <- now SIGNED
       rotate(ax, ay, az, pitch, yaw, 0)
       d = (az >> 6) + 5                     <- az may be negative
       if (d < 5) continue                   <- a near clip, which
                                                the original has not
       sx = ax / d + 0xa0 ; sy = ay / d + 0x64

   THE ROTATION IS THE EASY HALF. The cost is the two lines after it.
   The original takes its depth from an UNSIGNED shift of the wrapped
   dz, which is what makes every star count as in front of the camera
   however far round the volume it really is. Once the difference is
   rotated, half the field genuinely passes behind the viewer, and
   the renderer has no concept of behind - a star there reads as very
   distant and is drawn anyway, in the wrong place. So z must become
   signed and a near clip must be added, and that pair is a change to
   the projection's meaning rather than an addition to it.

   WHERE IT WOULD GO is a forked copy in our own file, called only
   when the drag is non-zero, never an edit to Points_Render - the
   transcription has to keep describing the binary.

   THE ERASE VARIANT IS NOT ON THIS PATH. Points_RenderErase carries
   a per-star record of the pixel it last lit, and rotating under it
   would strand those; but the battle and the chase both clear the
   frame and call the plain renderer, so only one scene would have to
   care and it is not one of these.

   WHAT IT WOULD BUY IS SMALL, which is the honest argument against
   it. A uniform random field has no orientation to see: turning it
   changes the direction the stars STREAM, not the picture. The
   craft, the shots and the planet carry almost all of the readable
   motion.

   THE COUNTDOWN IS THE EXCEPTION IN KIND: it bakes backdrop() once at
   entry and holds that image, so a drag still held across the
   transition is frozen into its background rather than easing out.

4.5.  LITHIUM's, and why it needs no pop

   src/lithium/lithium_look.c, and it is the same gesture - hold the
   button, swing, let go and it eases back - reached a different way.

   THERE IS NO PUSH AND POP BECAUSE THE OFFSET EXPIRES ON ITS OWN.
   Obj_LoadOrientA rewrites the camera slot's live ang_* and pos_*
   out of the accumulators a_* and home_* at the top of every frame,
   and Flight_Step integrates the accumulators alone - it never reads
   the live pair back. So an offset written just after the load is
   gone by the next one, with nothing saved and nothing restored.
   XCURSION needs the pair because its camera IS its integrator
   state; this camera is a copy made fresh each frame.

   ONE FUNCTION CARRIES IT AND THE TRANSCRIPTION STAYS CLEAN.
   Lith_CamLoad calls Lith_ObjLoadOrientA and then adds the drag, and
   the nine places that loaded the camera call it instead - the
   flyover, the approach, the battle's three, the tower fall's two
   and the tunnel. Putting the adds inside Obj_LoadOrientA itself
   would have been fewer edits and would have left an ORIGIN header
   describing a routine that had grown a feature.

   THERE IS NO WHITELIST. Every part that has a camera can be swung,
   including the ones never meant to be looked away from.

   IT TURNS ON THE SPOT. Scene_Project translates by the camera's
   position and THEN rotates, so the whole vertex pool swings about
   the eye - terrain, craft, tower and tunnel rings together. Nothing
   orbits and there is no pivot, which is the opposite of 4.3.

   LOOKING BACKWARDS IS CLEAN. The near plane is a clamp rather than
   a reject, so geometry behind the eye lands on z = 0x7d and sums to
   0x177 across a triangle - under every depth_near the scenes pass
   Scene_CullSort, so it is dropped rather than smeared. The tunnel
   is the one worth trying: a full half turn leaves the bore solid
   all round, because the eye is inside a tube rather than looking at
   one.

   The constants are ours and were chosen by feel: one screen width
   is one full turn against a circle of 0x2000, pitch is clamped to
   0x500 either way, and a released view eases back by a quarter of
   what is left each frame.

5.  LITHIUM: flying the demo's own craft

   src/lithium/lithium_game.c, one file, with its own sub-part list
   so the endless tunnel and the running man can join it without
   touching anything else.

5.1.  There is no flight model here

   THE DEMO ALREADY IS ONE, AND IT WAS ALWAYS MEANT TO BE FLOWN.
   Its motion is recorded mouse input and the recorder shipped in
   the binary - ../docs/lithium/effects/tracks.txt. Track_Play is
   the whole bridge between that recording and Flight_Step, and it
   writes four fields:

       pitch_acc  -=  vertical delta   << 5
       yaw_acc    +=  horizontal delta << 5
       command     =  a six-bit key index
       throttle    =  two bits

   Writing those from the keyboard instead of from the asset segment
   is the entire feature. Flight_Step then eases the attitude toward
   the accumulators, banks and levels the roll, drives the throttle,
   clamps to the ground and fires the guns - all of it the demo's,
   none of it rewritten.

   THE CHASE CAMERA IS A COMMAND, NOT A CAMERA RIG. 0x18 overwrites
   a slot's flight position with the lead's plus (0, 0, -0x60) turned
   by its own pitch and yaw, which is how the recorded camera stayed
   behind its craft. Giving the eye the craft's steering and then
   that command is the whole follow-cam, and Flight_Step's own
   trailing ease makes it lag rather than stick.

   PARKING THE EYE ON THE CRAFT LOOKS LIKE A RENDERER FAULT AND IS
   NOT ONE. The ship is then at distance zero, and the near plane
   clamps rather than rejects, so it smears across the view as a
   long sliver.

   The keys are one block at the top of the file: the arrows steer,
   and the rates are ours, chosen by feel - a held key is applied
   every tick where a track entry lands every sixth, so the scale is
   that much smaller.

5.2.  Reaching it

   It is a part after the finale, so ] walks into it, g jumps
   straight to it, and the demo runs into it on its own when the
   running man is done. The two keys go through the same jump, so
   they cannot drift apart.

   THE HANDOVER IS NOT THE DEMO'S OWN TEST, AND COULD NOT BE.
   Lith_FinaleDone at img 0x11c6 is the one test in the demo that
   waits for the module to WRAP rather than to reach a position, and
   what the original does when it fires is start the demo over. The
   reconstruction's clock walks the order list and stops at the end
   rather than beginning it again, so that test can never come true
   here; it is left standing as the transcription it is.

   What the handover watches instead is the tick reaching the end of
   the order list, which music_build_timeline already parks one past
   the last entry. It is the same moment reached by a route the
   clock can answer, and it needed no change to the clock, the
   timeline or the part table. Two independent numbers agree on it:
   the emulator gives 951 ticks of finale, frames 20044 to 21213,
   and the order table gives 952, tick 12797 to 13749 [verified].

   GOING TO THE PLAYGROUND RATHER THAN ROUND AGAIN IS OUR CHOICE.
   The demo loops for ever at that point and this does not.

5.3.  The controls, and what bounds the craft

   +-----------+-----------------------------------------------+
   | arrows    | steer - a rate into the accumulators          |
   | z         | fire; in the runner, wind the walk forward    |
   | x         | in the runner, wind the walk back             |
   | x / c     | the speed ladder, or a held dial - 5.4        |
   | wheel     | the runner's zoom, with the cursors           |
   | 1 - 4     | pick a sub-part - section 5.4                 |
   | s         | the runner's smear; the paint's lights        |
   +-----------+-----------------------------------------------+

   THE SPEED LADDER IS THE DEMO'S. Commands 0x0a to 0x17 are the
   fourteen speeds Flight_Step reads at img 0x5c4f as
   (command - 9) * 2 - 1, the odd numbers 1 to 27. The game holds an
   index and writes the matching command; the ladder itself is not
   ours.

   THE DEMO HAS TWO WEAPONS AND ONLY ONE OF THEM IS A SHOT. Command
   0x19 drives the plasma, which is a ring of trail records - a PUFF
   - and its tail zeroes the firing craft's x and z at img 0x5e1e,
   parking the ship at the origin. What the battle launches as an
   actual shot is a MESH: img 0x2123 loads a four-vertex tetrahedron
   at 0x724a, img 0x66df copies the firing craft's position and
   attitude into one of six slots at speed 0x1e0, and
   Flight_StepRoll flies it with no throttle, no easing and no
   turning. img 0x66d7 keeps a craft from running both at once.

   The game launches the mesh. Only the life is ours: the demo never
   expires a bolt, it recycles the six round robin and lets the rest
   fly on for ever.

   THE BOLT IS DRAWN BIGGER THAN THE BATTLE'S AND HAS TO BE. A shot
   fired along the view axis from a chase camera recedes to the
   vanishing point rather than crossing the screen, so four vertices
   at the battle's scale of 0xe are sub-pixel within a tick - drawn
   correctly, and invisible. The battle gets away with 0xe because
   its bolts come from other craft and cross the view.

   THE EYE CHASES THE DRAWN SHIP, NOT THE FLOWN ONE. Command 0x18
   offsets from the lead's FLIGHT position at img 0x5c89, while the
   model is drawn at its HOME, which Flight_Step trails a
   thirty-second behind - about thirty-two ticks of travel. The
   recorded camera could use 0x18 because its own home lagged by the
   same amount and the two errors cancelled; tighten the eye and
   they stop cancelling, and it ends up IN FRONT of the ship it is
   following, which looks exactly like the ship having vanished.
   Taking the offset from home_* and the bearing from a_* leaves one
   lag instead of two, and that one is ours to set.

   THE FLOOR WAS ALREADY THERE AND IS THE TERRAIN ITSELF.
   Flight_Step pins a craft to the surface at img 0x5d40, so a hill
   lifts the ship over it with nothing added. What the demo never
   needed was a ceiling, because a recorded track could not fly
   away; the game adds one, measured from the ground so the terrain
   stays in sight, along with a hull's clearance at the floor. Both
   are ours and both are one constant.

5.4.  The sub-parts

   One file holds them and a number key picks one, the way
   sim/run.py does. Entering a sub-part runs its own setup, so
   switching is repeatable: the same key twice gives the same frame
   to the pixel [verified].

       1   the flight - section 5.3
       2   the tunnel, and it does not end
       3   the runner, orbited
       4   a sheet to paint on

   THE TUNNEL WAS ALREADY ENDLESS. Nothing in Tick_Tunnel at img
   0x6b7f bounds it - the eye walks the path, the ring window
   advances every sixteen ticks, and the coordinates are words that
   wrap rather than run away. What stops it in the demo is
   Lith_TunnelDone asking the music, and the game does not ask.

   THE SEEDING IS IN THE SHATTER'S HANDOVER, NOT IN Lith_TunnelEnter,
   which only clears a flag. img 0x0964 is what sets the eye flying
   and img 0x09b0 and 0x09b7 clear the flash and steepen the ramps.
   A tunnel entered without them sits still in the dark - which is
   what a cold jump to the demo's own tunnel part does.

   THE COST DOES NOT GROW, which is worth saying because it looks as
   though it should: Tunnel_Build walks and discards first_ring path
   steps before it writes a vertex, and first_ring counts up for
   ever. Measured flat at 175 ms per thousand ticks from the first
   thousand to the eighth - about a hundredth of the tick budget -
   because the ring only advances once in sixteen.

   THE RUNNER IS ONE PHASE WORD AND NOTHING ELSE. Nothing about the
   figure is keyframed: all twelve limb joints come out of a single
   cosine sample off g_WalkPhase, with the left side read half a
   cycle later. So the walk's speed is one number - Tick_Finale adds
   0x40 a tick at img 0x659c - and because the pose is a pure
   function of the phase, every value is legal. z and x are a SLIDER
   rather than a set of notches: held, they wind the step forward
   and back, through zero into reverse, stopping at a turn and a
   half a tick either way.

   IT IS SYMMETRIC ABOUT ZERO, which is what shows the reverse to be
   the same cycle and not a different one. Winding back from the
   demo's pace the frames change by 76, 110, 70, 53, 30, 47, 16,
   then NOTHING at all, then 16, 47, 30, 53, 70, 110, 76 - the same
   figures mirrored [verified].

   THE ORBIT IS BUILT AROUND THE FIGURE, AND THE DEMO'S EYE IS NOT.
   img 0x115d, 0x117b and 0x1185 frame him low and to the right off
   a bearing of 0x1200 - a composition, not a target - so turning
   that arrangement about the scene's origin orbits a point he is
   nowhere near. What it looks like is a camera swinging around
   empty space while the figure drifts across the frame.

   SO THE CENTRE IS MEASURED. game_run_centre averages the figure's
   own vertices out of the scene pool once a frame, and the eye goes
   at that point plus a turned offset. Building the offset the same
   way round that Scene_Project unbuilds it - Y then X - makes the
   two cancel exactly, so he sits dead centre at every bearing and
   every distance [verified: 158 to 160 across x and 99 to 101
   across y through a full turn, against a screen centre of
   160, 100].

   THAT IS ALSO WHAT LETS THE ZOOM ARRIVE. Pointed at him rather
   than past him, closing the radius fills the frame: measured 16 by
   31 pixels at the far end and 320 by 200 - the whole screen - at
   the near one.

   IT HOLDS WHERE IT IS PUT. The drag anchors on the press and
   commits on the release; nothing eases back, which is the opposite
   of 4.5 and the reason the runner has its own camera state rather
   than borrowing lithium_look.c.

   THE ZOOM IS GEOMETRIC AND RUNS FROM GONE TO INSIDE HIM. A fixed
   step that reads well far out is a jump up close, so each notch
   multiplies; the cursors are fine and the wheel takes six at once.

   THE NEAR CULL STILL BOUNDS IT. Scene_CullSort throws away a face
   whose three z values sum to the threshold or less, which at the
   demo's 0x4e2 takes the near half of him; Lith_FinaleNearSet
   lowers it. NOT BELOW 0x177, though - that is what geometry behind
   the eye sums to once the projection CLAMPS its z rather than
   rejecting it, and drawing that smears the whole screen.

   THE WHEEL IS A HOST CAPABILITY, not a demo one. Demo_Wheel joins
   src/host.h beside Demo_Key and main_sdl.c answers SDL_MOUSEWHEEL
   with it. The two demos with no use for a wheel answer with
   nothing - they must still ANSWER, because the hosts are shared
   and the linker does not know which demo it is building.

   THE PAINT SHEET IS THE BUMP SCENE WITH THE BRUSH TAKEN OUT OF ITS
   HANDS. Dust_Stamp adds an eight by eight brush into a height
   field with saturation, Height_Relax diffuses the field, and
   Bump_Render lights the result against the three-glow picture.
   The demo drives the stamp from a thousand drifting particles; the
   mouse drives it here, and nothing else about the chain changes.
   A drag is joined point to point, because a mouse is sampled per
   event and a quick sweep would otherwise come out dotted. z wipes
   the sheet.

   IT IS NOT THE DEMO'S PLASMA, which is worth saying because the
   name is taken: LITHIUM's plasma is the ring of trail records the
   battle fires - lithium/effects/trail.txt - and the flight's gun already
   uses it. This is the other surface, and what it looks like is
   raised metal rather than a field of colour.

   TWO OF THE DEMO'S OWN SETTINGS HAD TO GO. The relax is HUNGRY:
   fed a thousand particles a tick it reads as a surface settling,
   but run every tick against one hand it wipes a stroke out in
   about a second, so it opens at one tick in eight here. And one
   pass of the brush barely dents a field the renderer reads as a
   gradient, so a dab lays it down several times over. Both are
   ours and both are one constant - GAME_PAINT_DISDEF and
   GAME_PAINT_WEIGHT, the second being how high the pen raises the
   surface. The field saturates at 0xff, so a weight high enough
   draws a plateau with a hard edge rather than a ridge.

   X AND C ARE THE DISSOLVE, and they meter relax PASSES because
   that is the only rate the diffusion has: Height_Relax does one
   fixed pass with no strength of its own. The dial is scaled so
   its travel is the span that differs - measured, a pass every
   eight ticks still has the stroke spreading after a hundred, one
   a tick takes it out in about sixty, and two a tick wipe it
   before the drag finishes [verified]. A slider counting whole
   passes would spend seven eighths of its travel on rates that all
   look alike, so the unit is the accumulator's denominator: 0
   never settles, the default is a pass every eight ticks, and the
   top is two a tick.

   S IS THE ANIMATED LIGHT AND THE RUNNING PALETTE, on by default
   and one branch, so it stops both together and leaves the sheet
   lit by wherever the light stood. The colour kick is the bump
   scene's own (0x73, 0x50, 0x0b) at img 0x6b1d, called a tick at a
   time the way that scene calls it - which is what makes the
   surface's colours run rather than its shading alone. In the
   runner the same key is the smear; it is the only key in the game
   that means two things.

   PAL_ANIMATE CARRIES ITS OWN STEP - 0x0d, 0x10 and 0x15 onto the
   three phases before it reads the table, img 0x6b2f - so the
   colours move at that rate whenever it is called at all, and
   scaling Pal_PhaseAdd's three constants down to nothing would not
   hold them still. Only not calling it does.

   THE LIGHT CIRCLES because a fixed one flattens the relief, and
   Bump_Render takes it as arguments rather than reading a global -
   so the game can move it without an accessor. The demo's own
   randomised light is not borrowed: it drifts, where this wants a
   cycle that comes back.

   THE LIGHT IS NOT IN PIXELS AND THAT IS THE TRAP. Bump_Render
   takes the pair as 16-bit accumulators and reads them SHR 8 as a
   coordinate in the 256 by 256 picture, so a pixel of zero slope
   samples the picture AT THE LIGHT - lithium/effects/bump.txt
   section 4. A screen coordinate handed in unscaled moves it by one
   picture unit across the whole screen and the surface reads as
   lit by nothing.

   THE ORBIT'S GEOMETRY IS THE GLOW TABLE'S. Tex_BuildThreeGlows
   lays three glows of radius 0x3f at picture (0x40,0x70),
   (0xb0,0xc0) and (0xc0,0x40), and its two loops count DOWN while
   the write index counts up, so in the buffer they sit mirrored at
   (0xc0,0x90), (0x50,0x40) and (0x40,0xc0) [verified]. The orbit
   takes their centroid, (0x70,0x85), and carries the glow radius -
   which is what walks the light across each glow in turn instead of
   through the black between them. Nothing about it is tuned to a
   picture.


   THE SMEAR IS OFF HERE AND ON EVERYWHERE ELSE. Lith_FinaleEnter
   puts the step, the smear and the near cull back to the demo's
   values, so part 14
   cannot inherit anything the game set. With the smear off the
   accumulator is deliberately LEFT STANDING rather than kept in
   step: turning it back on blends in the pose it was holding, and
   that lingering ghost is wanted.

5.5.  What it costs the demo

   Nothing that was not already true. After three hundred frames in
   the game, fourteen of the demo's fifteen parts redraw identically;
   the fifteenth is the fade-to-black after the opening, which comes
   out slightly different because it replays through the rock intro
   and the shared flare and flicker state has moved on.

   THAT ONE IS NOT THE GAME'S DOING. Three hundred frames in the
   demo's own finale produce exactly the same difference, to the
   pixel - so it is a property of any part running at all, not of
   this one [verified by the control].

   The eleven byte-exact checks are unaffected, because nothing in
   the game runs unless the game part is the one selected.

6.  Open questions

   o The handling constants are a first guess. Acceleration, braking
     and the camera distance have not been tuned against anything.

   o The starfield does not swing with the chase and the battle, nor
     does the chase's planet, which takes a pan of its own rather
     than a camera bearing, nor the battle's glow, which is a fixed
     screen box. Section 4.4 is what the stars would cost.

   o Nothing bounds the world. The terrain wraps at 256 by 256, so
     driving is endless and there is no course, no lap and no
     collision with the props the demo's own car part parks nearby.

   o LITHIUM's demo does not loop. The original restarts when the
     module wraps; the reconstruction's clock saturates at the end
     of the order list instead of wrapping, so nothing here comes
     back round. It is independent of section 5.2's handover, which
     deliberately does not need it.
